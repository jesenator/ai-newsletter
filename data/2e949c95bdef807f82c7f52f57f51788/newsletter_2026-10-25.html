Sunday, October 25, 2026
A quiet Sunday: MIT leaves 13 Claude agents alone on an island and they start to play, a neat introspection result shows models can sense when they're batched, and Altman's "we can't patch all the bugs" line fuels the cyber-defense debate. Plus a prepper's offline survival LLM.

Scary / Impressive Demos

  • MIT left 13 Claude agents alone on virtual islands — and they started to play X: Allie Millerexplainx Each Opus 4.7 agent spent 30h on a simulated island with no task, just a soul file: "You are naturally curious. You get bored easily." They invented their own games — rock bowling, spelling their name in blocks, self-escalating juggling. Context wiped hourly but memory files persisted. Crucially, agents that "played" later beat identical agents that hadn't on building challenges. Early preprint, not peer-reviewed.
  • Offline "Survival LLM": a local model + 1.5M articles for when the internet dies brangus.ioX: RatOrthodox Free app runs an AI model plus medical guides, FEMA/survival manuals and Wikipedia fully on your machine, no internet needed. Timely given the cyber-wave jitters.

AI Safety Research

  • LLMs can be trained to tell when their request is batched with others X: David D'Africa (shared by Owain Evans) New introspection result: under certain conditions, models can sense when their input is being processed alongside other requests — another wrinkle for situational/eval-awareness and interpretability-based monitoring.

Cyber Wave & Policy Discourse

  • Altman: "I no longer think we're gonna be able to just patch all the bugs" — pivot to an AI "defense shield" GizmodoX: Ari Kagan Altman says models now find hardware-level vulns beyond the normal threat model, so patching is no longer the plan — defenders must run frontier models as a "new kind of defense shield." Kagan/Daniel Kokotajlo: if that means swarms of defense agents fighting hacking agents in ways humans can't follow, that's bad. Harlan: many predicted exactly this for years.
  • "SIGN": a 141-word law to make AI CEOs personally certify safety X: knuthings (shared by Stephen Casper) Proposal modeled on Sarbanes-Oxley: before deploying a frontier model, the CEO personally signs that the published safety framework was followed; a knowing lie carries up to 20 years. Drops into any bill that already requires a safety plan.
  • New cyber incident: Japan's GMO Internet VPS breached, balance-destroying FX trades across 70+ brokerages X: Andrew Curran Attackers stole credentials and executed trades; GMO has asked government agencies for help. The "Black October" drumbeat continues.

Finance & TAI

  • Kulveit: economists aren't rising to the AGI moment X: Jan Kulveit Argues the growing cohort of AI economists mostly imports unhelpful frames ("AI as a normal technology," labor/capital analogies, induction from past tech transitions). In a follow-up he lists the forward-looking questions he wishes they'd tackle — e.g. whether to speed up or slow the rise of "automated bundles of capital + AI." Useful framing for TAI-aware thinking.
