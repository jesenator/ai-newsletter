Google DeepMind spins up a new AGI-policy institute, von der Leyen puts "pacing the frontier" into the State of the EU, and polls show Americans (incl. Trump voters) now broadly fear AI. Plus two impressive new models, a concrete inoculation-training result, and a Claude/Notion product update.

Governance & Policy

  • Google DeepMind launches the "DeepMind Institute" for AGI societal/economic questions DeepMind X: Demis Hassabis Demis & Shane Legg's new interdisciplinary forum, launching with essays incl. Jacobs/Imas "Economic Policy for AGI." Jack Clark quipped it's "the Anthropic Institute 🤝 the DeepMind Institute." Directly relevant to fieldbuilding/economic-transition work.
  • Von der Leyen backs "pacing the frontier" in the State of the EU, will invite labs to talks Politico X: S. Ó hÉigeartaigh The EU's top official explicitly acknowledged recursive self-improvement risks and OpenAI/HuggingFace-type incidents — a notable jump in political seriousness in Europe.
  • Poll: majorities of Americans — and Trump voters — see serious AI extinction risk Politico X: David Krueger 63% of Americans (61% of Trump voters) put at least moderate odds on AI destroying humanity; on pausing, 48% yes / 31% no. Useful data for CivAI-style public communication.
  • Sens. Curtis & Blunt Rochester call for immediate public AI hearings Punchbowl X: John Curtis Bipartisan Commerce members want lawmakers, model-makers and stakeholders "in the same room" to keep AI "fully under human control."
  • JD Vance: "If you're building Frankenstein, stop" — but no to regulation X: All-In A markedly different tone from Trump, while still favoring self-regulation and giving companies defensive tools rather than a "one-world governance structure."
  • RAND: "A U.S. Strategy for Superintelligence" X: RAND Assesses seven archetypal strategies; recommends a "Freedom of Action" approach that preserves the capacity to pivot as evidence arrives, prioritizing human survival + agency.

Targeting superconductors, magnets, semiconductors.

  • Notion Custom Agents can now call sub-agents X: Notion A primary agent can hand off focused work (each with its own instructions/context/model) then reassemble it — plus new workspace-wide default agent "house rules." Relevant if you're leaning on Notion agents.

AI Safety Research

  • Inoculation midtraining with learned "neologisms" — controlling how broadly misalignment generalizes LessWrong X: Geodes Research Introduce a new token marking where misalignment "belongs"; teach the association in midtraining, RL on misaligned data with the token present, then evaluate without it. Result: you can bound how far misalignment spreads — a rare concrete steering result.
  • Jack Lindsey (Anthropic) lays out interpretability's most important open questions X: Jack Lindsey A detailed research agenda: better "mind-reading" of activations, causal "why" methods, probes for unverbalized motivations, and understanding weird training generalization — argues model "psychology/biology" should be 50% of the field, not 5%.
  • Google: "AI in Science" — big productivity gains, shifting bottlenecks X: Ethan Mollick From 15M Gemini interactions, 2,600+ specialized models, and 600 scientists: ~7 hours/week saved, but work shifts toward verification and possibly "safer," more incremental questions (streetlight effect).
  • METR president Chris Painter re-explains what METR does amid an EA/independence smear cycle X: Chris Painter Clarifies METR takes no money from frontier labs/employees, publishes contract terms and redaction details, and hires across viewpoints — responding to a NY Post hit piece. Relevant to the "we need a bigger, credible evaluator ecosystem" thread.
