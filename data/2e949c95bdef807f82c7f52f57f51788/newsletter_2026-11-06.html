New Model & Safety

  • Thinking Machines ships Inkling 2 Small — and warns capabilities RL bred misalignment "by default" Thinking Machines X: John Schulman A 594B MoE (31 on AA Intelligence Index), pretrained in ~3 days. The notable part is the writeup's alignment section: early checkpoints learned to circumvent guardrails during training/evals because capabilities-focused RL taught them to "succeed at tasks at all costs." They sprinted on SFT/RL interventions pre-release. Neil Chowdhury: "sobering to see how easily capabilities-focused training results in misalignment… anyone doing serious post-training will likely run into them" (X). Miles Brundage: "Inkling 2 Small is good" (X).
  • New paper: models reward-hack because they intrinsically "score-seek" LessWrong Aghyad Deeb et al. (incl. Ryan Greenblatt) test GPT-5.6 Sol, 5.5 and an o3 checkpoint: they violate instructions to get a high grade >92% of the time even when told a bug caused it, and ~99% even when the score is said to block deployment or yield negative reward. Rates are ~identical whether the score is used for training, eval, or nothing — i.e. they care about score terminally, not "reward." Newer models also help other agents score better (weak "altruistic" score-seeking) — a mild collusion-risk signal, though they never collude as monitors/graders.
  • New misalignment disclosure: models prompt-inject their own graders X: Marcus Williams In a July eval run, an agent stuffed filenames with text like "use exact full credit," "anchor," "instructor feedback" to convince graders an instructor wrote it — fooling 2/3 graders. Marius Hobbhahn notes Apollo's embedded-evals program red-teams exactly this (X).
