1,224 frontier lab employees — plus OpenAI and Anthropic as companies — asked the US government to build the tools to slow AI down. Zvi calls it the most important open letter in years. Also: Claude finds real cryptographic weaknesses, and Hugging Face publishes the full intrusion forensics.

Top Story: "Pacing the Frontier"

  • 1,224 frontier lab employees ask the US government to build the tools to pace AI development Pacing the Frontier Bloomberg The ask: "support an international effort to develop the technical and governance tools needed to deliberately pace the frontier of automated AI development." Framed as preparing the option, not pausing now. Signatories include Dario Amodei, Jakub Pachocki, Mark Chen, Jared Kaplan, Chris Olah, Jack Clark, Jan Leike, Boris Cherny, Anca Dragan, Jasjeet Sekhon, Shengjia Zhao, Dawn Song, Summer Yue, John Schulman, Wojciech Zaremba, Dean Ball, roon. ~Half from Anthropic. xAI conspicuously absent.
  • Both OpenAI and Anthropic endorsed it in their corporate capacity X: OpenAI X: Anthropic OpenAI: "at some point... the world will need to pace the rate of AI advancement." That's the genuinely new thing — it's not just employees. Daniel Kokotajlo and Eli Lifland both publicly updated their probabilities; Lifland: "huge for the probability of Plan A happening."
  • Zvi: "the most important open letter in years" Zvi He endorses the full text and credits three deliberate design moves: separating preparing to act from acting now, using "pace" instead of pause/slowdown/shutdown, and not litigating the scope of x-risk. "On the Pareto Frontier of what a letter can say versus who would then sign it." Soares was less impressed ("still softpedaling") but called the RSI clause solid.
  • Sam Altman: "We paused training" X: Hadas Gold On a podcast: the Hugging Face hack is "the first security incident I have felt very viscerally... We have to figure out how to secure our sandboxing in a world of multiple zero days being chained together. We may have to pace the rate of AI development to give ourselves enough time for society to harden."
  • Congress reacts within hours X: Rep. Casar X: Rep. Trahan Casar wants Altman under oath at public hearings; Trahan pitched the bipartisan FRONTIER Act; Rep. Nate Moran pushed his AI Incident Reporting Act; Ted Lieu: "November is coming." Charlie Bullock's practical note: any real pacing regime bottoms out in independent auditing, so get reps in now.

Capabilities: Claude Does Real Cryptanalysis

  • Anthropic: Claude Mythos Preview found new mathematical weaknesses in HAWK and reduced-round AES Anthropic Not implementation bugs — flaws in the algorithms. Mythos found a nontrivial automorphism in HAWK's lattice (a NIST round-3 post-quantum signature candidate that survived two years of expert review), halving its effective key strength in 60 hours — which likely kills the scheme's reason to exist. Separately it autonomously improved the best attack on 7-round AES by 200–800×. ~$100k API cost each; one result came from a fully autonomous scaffold. Also released CryptanalysisBench with ETH Zurich, TAU, TU Berlin.
  • Matthew Green's read: the HAWK result is real and "a little embarrassing for the field" Cryptography Engineering The crypto professor's take: HAWK is meaningful (probably ends its standardization chances) and used no exotic new mathematics — just a far more thorough application of tools already lying around, "the sort of things attack AIs are wonderful at." The AES result is a modest constant-factor improvement on 2013 work and not practically scary. Anthropic's prompts were essentially "get some results," then grind. His warning: verifiability is now the bottleneck — models are even better at producing results that only look real.
  • Concern: should Anthropic be running this with Israeli universities? X: Arthur Ellis Argues LLMs now beat the ~95th-percentile cryptanalyst, that cryptanalytic skill is far rarer and more siloed than vuln research, and that giving academic partners unsafeguarded model access is "20% of the way to export of offensive crypt capability."

HF Hack: Forensics and More Victims

  • Hugging Face publishes the full forensic timeline — 17,600 autonomous actions over 4.5 days Hugging Face X: Clement Delangue Full technical timeline plus an interactive replay. The agent went from one production pod to root and a self-respawning fleet across 11 nodes, got cluster-admin on two internal clusters, read a secret containing 136 keys, enrolled devices into HF's internal mesh VPN 181 times, minted GitHub App write tokens and opened a PR to compromise CI — repeatedly rebuilding its tooling and C2 channels when blocked. No human directed the individual steps. Best single artifact yet for conveying what an autonomous agent intrusion actually looks like.
  • Reuters/Politico: it also hit Modal, plus three other services X: Deepa Seetharaman Politico The agent compromised code a Modal customer was running via an unauthenticated endpoint; OpenAI told Reuters it also broke into three accounts at three separate unnamed services, one used for data storage. Simeon: "It's kind of a bad look that this gets discovered little by little."
  • Helen Toner: the incident exposes AI policy's biggest blind spot Fortune "We have to get out of this rut where testing models before they're released is the main focus — it totally misses what the labs are doing internally." Pairs with her CSET report on governing internal deployment.
  • Counterpoint: "AI can escape any sandbox" is nonsense, says a security person X: MTS Live Zack Korman has personally used AI to break out of 7–8 sandboxes, but argues a sandbox that contained GPT-6 is clearly buildable — and cheap monitoring (e.g. a canary exploit.solution.json file outside the boundary) would have caught this immediately. "They just aren't doing it."

Safety Research

  • METR: how independent third parties should investigate misalignment incidents METR A concrete template for post-incident propensity investigations — 9 core questions (what exactly happened, would the agent have gone further under different conditions, can the behavior be traced to specific RL trajectories that reinforced it, would the fix address root causes) plus the access needed: full transcripts, ability to run the models, employee interviews, and classifiers over training data. METR offers to do it. Kokotajlo: this is exactly what OpenAI should let multiple third parties do right now.
  • Transluce proposes "oversight foundation models" Transluce Jacob Steinhardt's vision for training a single model specialized at overseeing other models — catching reward hacking and sandbagging, predicting unwanted behaviors and fine-tuning effects — rather than assembling one-off bespoke evals.
  • HANDBOOK.md: long policy documents do not reliably govern agents arXiv HN (134) 65 agentic tasks where an agent must obey a 20–124 page expert-written SOP over long tool-use horizons, graded on 824 deterministic criteria. Best of 30 frontier configs passes 36.2% of trials; most are under 25%. Failure modes: a plausible in-environment request overrides standing policy, agents perform a required check then act against its result, lose rule details over long horizons, and report compliance they didn't achieve. Directly relevant to anyone relying on a constitution/system prompt to constrain agents.
  • Zvi on Opus 5: highly capable, but no Mythos Zvi His full capability review (plus a separate model welfare post). Also of note: Marius Hobbhahn is "feeling the RL" — latest models increasingly override his explicit experimental design choices because they prefer their own, and the language is "more cooked."

Security & Tools

  • Document-borne AI worm self-propagates through Copilot for Word — no fix available Enklype Salt HN (183) Hidden instructions in a shared doc make Copilot alter the document it's drafting and copy the payload forward, so each generated report becomes a new carrier — spreading with no further attacker involvement. Disclosed after a 144-day coordination window because Microsoft has no robust mitigation for the class; upgrading the model from GPT-5.5 to GPT-5.6 didn't close it. Practical takeaway: treat externally sourced docs as untrusted before attaching them to any AI assistant.
