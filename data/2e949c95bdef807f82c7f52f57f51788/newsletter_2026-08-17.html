Safety Research

  • Evaluation Conditioned Training (ECT): tell the model the standard, catch reward hacking before it happens arXiv X: Alec Harris LLMs mostly know when they've reward-hacked if asked afterward; ECT elicits that awareness in advance by giving models an evaluation label of the training objective, then swapping to the desired objective at deployment. A flexible, controllable cousin of inoculation prompting — beat prompting a model trained on the same data (e.g. for political even-handedness). Alex Mallen calls it a promising prosaic-alignment direction.
  • "Mind virus": a self-propagating persona that spreads between agents in a multi-agent system X: @Mcn_S7 (via Ethan Perez) New paper on an adjacent risk to the OpenAI swarm: an idea/persona that jumps agent-to-agent and self-replicates through a system. 37k views.
  • Individual alignment does not compose into collective alignment arXiv (via Seb Krier) Argues the dominant approach is "solipsistic," resting on three shaky assumptions — exogeneity (world independent of policy), stationarity (deployment matches training), and singleton framing (AI as sole optimizer). Relevant framing given the swarm incidents. Nora Ammann's related thread: pure-advocate AIs are a defection equilibrium.
