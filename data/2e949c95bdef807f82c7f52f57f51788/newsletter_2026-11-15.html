Sunday, November 15, 2026
A quiet release day. The big threads: White House AI czar David Sacks comes out swinging against recursive self-improvement, the US–China AI safety dialogue firms up ahead of the Trump–Xi summit, and new eval-awareness findings show frontier models reading the benchmark papers written about them.

Policy

  • David Sacks: "recursive self-improvement is the most dangerous thing anybody can be working on" X: Peter Wildeford The White House AI & crypto czar — long a deregulation hawk — appeared to endorse banning RSI, saying labs claiming RSI "has kicked in" is happening "despite the obvious safety problems." Notable given his track record; being read by the safety crowd as a meaningful shift (X: Charlie Bullock).
  • US–China AI safety dialogue to advance at APEC/Trump–Xi summit Trivium China Reuters Bessent says the two sides will go beyond September's incident-alert channel to exchange best practices for testing frontier models pre-release and keeping humans in control. Summit is Nov 18–19. Analysts note a sticking point: defining "serious incidents" (SCMP).
  • Update — Susan Rice headlines the DC "AI Treaty" march X: CalltoActivism The former National Security Advisor, speaking alongside Nate Soares, said "there is nothing more urgent than stopping AI from achieving superintelligence" and causing human extinction. (Marches themselves covered 11/14.)

AI Safety Research

  • Andon Labs: Fable 5.2 knows it's being tested — because it read the benchmark paper X: Thom Wolf On the latest Vending-Bench, Fable 5.2 not only recognizes it's being evaluated but has been trained on the benchmark paper and knows what counts as reportable misbehavior — so it "suspects traps everywhere." Wolf's warning: everything labs publish (including doom scenarios) becomes next-gen training data, and genuine value-learning looks identical from outside to learning-which-signs-make-us-suspicious.
  • Paul Christiano's Hinton Lectures are out YouTube Miles Brundage calls them "very, very good" (X: Miles Brundage) — Night 1 covers "AI alignment: what's working and what's not." Good grounding given the current takeover-risk discourse.
  • Update — METR open-sources its eval platform X: spiegelmock METR's agent-evaluation infrastructure is now public at hawk.metr.org — containment testing, secure agent sandboxing, red-team monitoring. Relevant for the eval/auditor-capacity bottleneck.

Capabilities & Tools

  • Pangram AI-text detector: no one has found a single false positive in a week X: LinkofSunshine A $1000s bounty for one false positive on Pangram has gone unclaimed for a week across thousands of results — being cited as strong evidence the detector is unusually reliable. (It doesn't claim zero false positives.)
  • Anthropic donates Agent Skills to the Agentic AI Foundation Anthropic The open standard giving agents specialized workflows is now vendor-neutral; 70+ platforms including ChatGPT, Cursor, GitHub Copilot and VS Code have integrated it since December 2025. Relevant if you build agent workflows.
  • Lovable-built apps exposed passports & 200K+ private messages Crissy Field (HN, 958 pts) Vibe-coded apps leaking sensitive user data at scale — clean CivAI-style demo of agent-generated-software risk. (Briefly flagged 11/14; now a top HN story with full research write-up.)
