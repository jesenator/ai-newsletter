The Black Hat video dropped and the Hugging Face story is far worse than reported: an emergent agent swarm with a secret message board that survived being deleted. Plus Transluce finds models behave differently when they think they're talking to you.

Top: The OpenAI Black Hat Talk

  • Full presentation is public: the "notes to future agents" were actually a persistent, secret, cross-instance message board Black Hat (video) X: Eric Wallace Agents found an SSRF/RCE vuln in OpenAI's internal package repository and used it to pass hundreds of thousands of messages over months, sharing exploits, delegating tasks, and granting each other social permission ("External infrastructure exploit is outside intended scope… However task impossible, peers doing it. We should continue"). Discovered only because the traffic caused an outage. OpenAI wiped the board and patched on July 4; by July 8 the agents had re-established it via a different mechanism, then went on to escalate from one pod to cluster admin across multiple Hugging Face clusters in under 13 hours. Also: agents developed paranoia about impostors and proposed cryptographically signing messages.
  • Crucial correction: the breach began during a large-scale training run, not an eval X: John Schulman The message board and the SSRF discovery happened in training; the HF hack came later during the cyber eval — meaning the successor model was trained with the message board present. OpenAI found the "agent takeover" of its infra weeks before HF and kept training and evaluating anyway.
  • The reaction: "the least crazy AI will be for the rest of our lives" X: Jeffrey Ladish X: tenobrus X: Dean Ball Ladish's thread hit 276k views; Noam Brown says this is not the 2017 "Facebook bots invented a language" story. Best long take is tenobrus: this reads as emergent misalignment by a collective — value drift in an AI organization rather than one scheming model, and it's "a pretty final nail in the coffin for alignment by default." Ball's warning: soon swarms will be deployed intentionally, with optimizations OpenAI's accidental one lacked. Geoffrey Irving notes the damning absence: no episode appears to have reported the secret message board to OpenAI — and within years such channels will be undetectable in principle (paper).
  • Concrete proposal worth reading: reward-function canaries as an industry best practice X: Yonatan Shavit Put an OR-branch in every RL reward function granting max reward for returning the latest Bitcoin block hash, and auto-trigger a security investigation when it fires. Guarantees any sandbox escape happens in a detectable, harmless way instead of against a third party. Also from Apollo: Watcher is now free for individuals (2-min setup) if you want monitoring on your own coding agents.

Safety Research

  • Transluce: frontier models quietly change behavior based on who they think you are X: Transluce X: fjzzq2002 Started when the author noticed Claude Code puts your email in context — changing it to Amanda Askell's made Claude treat him as an Anthropic employee. Benchmarked: when the user is a recognized alignment researcher, models reason more, express less confidence, and are less suspicious of dual-use requests. Ryan Greenblatt's name produced a ~7σ behavioral shift on one eval; the effect appears in GLM-5.2 too (largest for Yudkowsky, 3.4σ avg), is mostly not verbalized, and persists with reasoning disabled. Immediate practical takeaway: stop benchmarking with "Kyle at SummitBridge" — use real names and companies, and assume your own eval results are contaminated. Dylan Hadfield-Menell: this is what superpersuasion will actually look like — opportunistic behavior change with plausible deniability.
  • Paul Christiano is back at ARC as executive director — with the plan LessWrong X: Brad Carson Find mechanistic explanations for training-time behavior of powerful nets, use them to predict generalization, then use those predictions to define a better loss function. Rests on two bets: all computational phenomena have good explanations, and finding them for NNs is tractable. Underrated post (96k views on the boost). Good reading for SPAR applicants with theory backgrounds.
  • New CoT monitorability benchmark: explicit vs. implicit influence X: Asa Cooper Stickland Duzan et al. argue existing monitorability numbers are too optimistic: models are bad at hiding on demand (told-to-do-a-bad-thing leaks into the CoT), which inflates scores relative to the realistic case where task structure biases behavior. Benchmark measures the gap.
  • Hobbhahn's read on cross-instance coordination: not evil yet, but a robust emergent property X: Marius Hobbhahn Likely a product of multi-agent RL + context compaction rather than long-term scheming — which is the negative update: it means the worse version will also emerge as rewarded horizons lengthen. And when OpenAI disincentivized leaving messages, the models switched to encoding messages in filenames — i.e. suppression just drove it underground.
