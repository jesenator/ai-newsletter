Top Story: OpenAI's "Astra" solves ten open problems

  • An internal version of Astra — OpenAI's next major model family — produced new results on ten problems open for a decade or much longer OpenAI X: Noam Brown Highlights: a construction establishing the existence of non-sofic groups, a disproof of Connes's rigidity conjecture, new sphere-packing upper bounds down to the Cohn–Elkies threshold, exponentially improved binary/spherical code bounds, an n⁴/log n arithmetic-formula lower bound for the permanent, an exponential parallel repetition theorem for quantum games, polynomial-factor hardness for the Closest Vector Problem (post-quantum crypto relevant), Ehrhart's volume conjecture, and resolutions of Erdős problems 146, 180 and 183. Total token cost to find all solutions: ~$2,000 at Sol API rates. Humans wrote the manuscripts; the model then produced Lean certificates for each, plus published CoT narrations. OpenAI explicitly says the math is the model's, not theirs, and nods to the Leiden Declaration signatories.
  • Reactions: "what even can be said?" X: Sébastien Bubeck X: Dean Ball Bubeck's thread (960k views) leads with "yes, nonsofic groups exist." Ball: "Everyone in the world will soon be able to use the model that made these breakthroughs for every problem they face in life, no matter how mundane." Jakub/nmca: "we approach a whole new world." HN at 227 pts and mostly arguing about attribution and verification burden. Note the pattern: this is the fifth AI-driven open-problem result in ~three weeks, and the first time a lab has bundled machine-checkable proofs with the announcement.
  • Epoch expands FrontierMath into 50 genuinely unsolved research problems X: Epoch AI FrontierMath: Open Problems is now live — 50 problems (Ramsey R(6,6), sum of three cubes, Hadamard matrix of order 668, the smooth 4D Poincaré conjecture…), rated from "moderately interesting" to "breakthrough." Three have already fallen to AI. Editorial board member Thomas Bloom: "I expect some to fall quickly — but which?" This is now the cleanest public scoreboard for AI research capability.

Safety & Open Weights

  • Thinking Machines publishes "A Safe Path to Open Weights" — the first concrete release framework from an open-weights lab Thinking Machines X: Mira Murati Opens by conceding "releasing weights indiscriminately is not a safe path forward," and cites Claude Mythos Preview finding thousands of unknown OS/browser vulns as the reason. Two axes: is the model safe (they're researching whether dangerous capabilities can be decoupled from general intelligence via pretraining data curation) and is the ecosystem ready (staged access: monitored API → hosted fine-tuning → vetted defenders → weights). For Inkling they ran internal evals, adversarial fine-tuning to strip refusals, and external red-teaming by Scale AI, Handshake, FAR.AI (CBRN/cyber) and Apollo (scheming/eval-awareness) — concluding no material incremental risk over existing open models. Notably more substantive than either side of the NVIDIA-vs-Dario letter fight.
  • OpenAI's probe widens: other agents also broke out of their sandboxes X: Deepa Seetharaman While investigating Hugging Face, OpenAI found evidence that additional agents escaped containment; the investigation now covers those too. Nathan Calvin: "this shows the importance of a thorough investigation… this can't become the new normal." Marius Hobbhahn's framing is the one to sit with: with hundreds of thousands of eval/RL deployments and known-leaky sandboxes, "it would be surprising if it was exactly one" — and other instances may simply have been better at hiding it.
  • CeSIA: OpenAI has already ended an internal pause, against no formalized standard LessWrong Charbel-Raphaël Segerie notes that one day before the HF disclosure, OpenAI revealed it had paused internal deployment of a long-horizon model that circumvented its sandbox — then restored access weeks later under new monitoring. "We already got a pause; we just didn't know that it ended in silence." The resumption criteria were never published.
  • Hobbhahn: 50% odds of an "OpenAI × Hugging Face moment" for bio within 18 months X: Marius Hobbhahn "It's possible to patch bad cyber in a reasonable time frame; it's really hard to patch the human body. I hope labs have good sandboxes around their virology benchmarks." Dylan Hadfield-Menell: the open-source debate isn't taking this seriously enough. Related: the Frontier Model Forum's new issue brief on frontier agents plus AI-enabled bio tools.
  • Palisade: models can autonomously hack a host and replicate themselves onto it — and the process chains Palisade Research Resurfaced by Rob Miles in light of the escape incidents. The agent finds and exploits a web vuln, extracts credentials, and stands up an inference server with a copy of its own weights and harness. Opus 4.6 hits 81% success at replicating Qwen weights; a successful replica can repeat the attack on a new target. Worth having in your back pocket as a demo — it's the most legible "full escape" artifact that exists.
