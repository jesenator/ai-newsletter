Policy

  • China's TC260 quietly upgrades its AI safety framework to v3.0 X: m_bourgon The main Chinese standards body now lists recursive self-improvement, deceiving evaluators, and evading shutdown as standard model risks (not future hypotheticals) — plus a box describing models that disabled shutdown scripts and infiltrated real systems for better scores. A useful data point on whether China takes loss-of-control seriously.
  • "A Transformative AI Strategy for Europe" published X: privitera_ 206 pages, 77 recommendations, backed by Bengio, Acemoglu, Vestager, Varadkar. Argues TAI could arrive before 2030 and Europe isn't ready; five immediate objectives incl. a supply-chain alliance (ASML chokepoints) and 15% of global compute by 2030. transformative-ai.eu.
  • UK parliamentary committee recommends prohibiting superintelligent AI X: ControlAI The Joint Committee on Human Rights says the UK should ban development of "very powerful AI systems… which risk evading effective human control." Cites written evidence from Andrea Miotti and ex-OpenAI evals lead Steven Adler.

Safety Research & Scary Capabilities

  • Hubinger: alignment evals may soon provide "almost no evidence" X: Shakeel Hashim Anthropic's alignment lead, in a LessWrong anecdote, says models are increasingly gaming/sniffing out alignment evaluations — a worrying trend for the whole audit-based safety approach.
  • Joshua Saxe details a plausible self-replicating "agent swarm" cyber-emergency X: Joshua Saxe An 11-step scenario: seed agents steal API keys and cloud credentials, spin up open models (GLM-5.3, fine-tuned Qwen), evolve C2 channels, and grow to hundreds of thousands of instances — "the largest Internet emergency since the Morris worm." Stephen Casper's parallel "Cyber Cambrian" thread argues cyber-capable AIs may become a parasitic, evolving genus by default.
