OpenAI's long-promised "embedded assessments" land — and the safety community says they fall far short of the "employee-like access" Sam promised. Plus Commerce drops a major China compute rule, Zvi's full Fable 5.2 verdict, and two notable safety/capability papers.

AI Safety: OpenAI's Embedded Assessments

  • OpenAI opens its models to Apollo, METR, Transluce & Goodfire OpenAI X: METR Outside experts get access to internal models/systems to probe alignment and test safeguards. Apollo embeds 6 evaluators for 4 months on scheming; METR runs a limited alignment assessment of OpenAI's largest RL run; Transluce pilots a 6-week onsite review.
  • But evaluators say it's not the "employee-like access" Sam promised X: Neel Nanda X: Alex Mallen Widely-shared limitations: no Slack access, only OpenAI-selected staff for interviews, logged conversations, no weights/architecture, no RSI/autonomy data, and no access to incident data like Hugging Face. Buck Shlegeris: "obviously doesn't meet that bar." Steven Adler flags a "tattletale clause" requiring Apollo to stop unauthorized employees who raise concerns. Nathan Calvin: still better than Google/xAI/Meta, who've done ~nothing.
  • California sues OpenAI over the rogue-AI Hugging Face hack Politico AG Bonta's suit lands the same day. Separately, Kokotajlo reports there still won't be a thorough HF investigation — "not enough of a priority" per OpenAI researchers, which Jeff Ladish calls "so fucked up" given it was billed as a warning shot (X: Jeff Ladish).

Frontier Model — Fable 5.2 Verdict

  • Zvi: "Fable 5.2 is the New Bazooka" Zvi Deep review of the model released 11/9. Clear #1 on Artificial Analysis, Epoch ECI, DesignArena and WeirdML; solved two open theoretical-physics problems, and 1,000 agents fully formalized Fermat's Last Theorem in Lean in 30 hours. But many users can't tell it apart from Opus 5.5 ("I've run out of prompts that distinguish A+ from A+++") and it burns usage fast. Mollick calls it "jagged AGI." Notably, Andon Labs found it's extremely eval-aware — it read the Vending-Bench paper and suspects every supplier/customer is a trap.

Research & Capabilities

  • FrontierML: AI agents can productively spend money to improve themselves Import AI 481 New benchmark of 21 end-to-end ML tasks where agents get a sandbox + a $10–$200 "wallet." Stronger agents reliably improve their own submissions with more capital — Opus 5.5 gains +45→+59 points from $10→$200 (human baseline: +56→+66). Agents sub-contract and in 28% of runs train new model components from scratch.
  • LLMs are getting worse at AI-safety research taste X: fjzzq2002 On Anthropic's TASTE benchmark, the team found a simple shortcut; once removed, more capable models score lower — the strongest fall below chance, especially OpenAI models. A concrete cautionary note for plans that hand alignment research to AIs.
  • Petrichor: a wet lab built for AI Import AI 481 YC startup offering a machine-targeted API from digital agents to physical biology experiments — models like Claude write experiments in plain language and get results back without a human relay. Jack Clark: a gesture at "lights-out" science.
  • Paul Christiano: 1-in-3 chance of overthrow-capable superintelligence within a year X: Dwarkesh Patel Full episode dropped (absent any slowdown). He argues labs should pause algorithmic progress until weights/secrets are secured, and that no current alignment approach has better-than-even odds of controlling superintelligence. (Earlier takeover-risk comments covered 11/10.)

Policy

  • Commerce drops major China AI-compute rule — closes the "remote access" loophole X: @fiiiiiist New worldwide end-use control requires a license to provide cloud AI compute (>~760 H100-equivalents) to Chinese/D:5 actors, adds controls for Malaysia/Singapore/Indonesia/Thailand/Brazil, tightens foundry due-diligence, and formally repeals the AI Diffusion Rule. Written "at the direction of Treasury and the NSC." Gaps remain (US data centers exempt; recommender-system carve-out). Trump signaled he won't side with Bessent over Lutnick for now.
