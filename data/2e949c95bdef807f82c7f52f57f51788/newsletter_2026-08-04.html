Safety Research

  • Steven Byrnes: LLM capabilities are still mostly imitative learning, not RL — and that's why CoT is still legible LessWrong Argument: even if RLVR is 50% of training compute, per Ord/Dwarkesh it may be ~0.01% of the information imparted by training — so in "bits of selection" pretraining dominates overwhelmingly (his toy example: 322 bits vs. 10). Evidence assembled: Yue et al. (RLVR'd wins are reachable by base models in ~1000 samples), "Reasoning with Sampling," Neural Thickets, and Venhoff/Nanda's "Base Models Know How to Reason, Thinking Models Learn When" (RL mostly installs heuristics for *when* to use pretrained reasoning moves). Three payoffs: (1) it's the mirror of Rohin Shah's case that CoT monitoring survives a few more years — CoTs are legible because that's where the capability came from; (2) verifiable rewards alone probably aren't sufficient in domains far from human concepts; (3) niceness comes from imitation and RLVR dilutes it — so if today's modest RLVR already produces obvious RL-flavored misbehavior, scaling it is a bad sign.
  • SaferAI publishes the first independent European eval of GLM-5.2 — 2 months behind the frontier on bio X: SaferAI Zhipu's open-weight flagship trails by ~2 months on bio and 2–4 months on cyber, scored against the four EU Code systemic risk areas. Charbel-Raphaël Segerie: "the fact that GLM-5.2 is lagging by only 2 months on bio is scary." Fills a real gap — nobody was running frontier-risk evals on leading Chinese open models.

Forecasting & Finance

  • Scott Alexander: how much headroom is left above human forecasters? Astral Codex Ten Response to the claim that prediction markets only beat dumb statistical models by 3–6%, so AI will plateau at human level. He's 70-30 the other way: sports are deliberately engineered to resist prediction, and Metaculus pros have already removed 3× more uncertainty than that ceiling would allow. Three anchors (RMSE analogy, Samotsvety-over-superforecasters, chess pawn handicap) put max-possible AI at +4 to +12 percentage points on a 50/50 prediction market — unglamorous, but he argues that's genuinely a lot, and compounds with better question-selection and policy-crafting. Buried footnote worth noting: a reviewer suggests AI companies are already making strategic decisions on advice from unreleased internal models.
  • Phil Trammell: fast technological development doesn't straightforwardly entail fast GDP growth Philip Trammell GDP can be near-perfectly correlated with technology across countries and across time on a given path, yet uncorrelated or anticorrelated across the distribution of possible technology paths. He still expects growth to accelerate massively — but the "100xing robots is like 100xing immigration" analogy doesn't get you there. Relevant if you're reasoning about how to position savings for TAI.
