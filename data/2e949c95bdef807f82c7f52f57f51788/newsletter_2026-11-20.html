A genuinely historic day: Claude appears to have helped prove the Riemann Hypothesis — the problem long named as the warning-sign canary for dangerously advanced AI. Plus METR's new "beyond time horizons" report, Mira Murati ships a laptop-sized looped transformer, and Trump says he and Xi agreed to "joint guardrails."

The Riemann Hypothesis Has (Apparently) Fallen

  • Anthropic mathematician + Claude claim a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis alpo.geSci Am Levent Alpöge, working with Claude, posted a claimed proof of math's most famous open problem (1M+ views on the announcement). RH was for decades the single problem most often named in advance as the canary for dangerously advanced AI — Metaculus still had it at 2035 last week. Not yet peer-verified.
  • Why safety people are rattled, not just impressed X: allTheYudX: So8res The worry isn't RH itself — it's that the methods "should generalize to make AI very good at anything you can definitely test, and unfortunately that includes making AI better at making AI." Jeff Ladish: "hopefully creating synthetic replicators that destroy the biosphere doesn't turn out to be much easier than we thought." snewmanpv asks about the contingency plan if factorization (public-key crypto) is next.
  • Skeptic's counterpoint X: theojaffee "Surely we could just as easily conclude RH is easier than we thought rather than AIs are on the brink of superhuman at everything?" Geoffrey Irving (who may have won a bet) notes RH is actually weak relative to what's likely true, and wonders if a theory of stronger conjectures will flourish within months.

AI Safety Research

  • METR: AI R&D capabilities are improving rapidly — and getting harder to measure X: METR New research update ("TH2.0") going beyond their time-horizon tasks. Yafah Edelman flags the uncomfortable dynamic: with METR's auditing restricted, when they make a surprising claim they can't fully evidence, "I update upwards on the chance the claim is true, and downwards on how much I trust METR."
  • New paper: sabotaged AI safety research is very hard to detect X: kshenoy_ Even with the full research log and ability to run experiments, human auditors usually couldn't distinguish benign from sabotaged experiments or determine how sabotage was done. Frontier models do somewhat better but still miss most of it — directly relevant to AI-supervising-AI alignment plans.
  • NormGym: agents struggle to learn social norms by observation ProjectX: binze_li Hadfield lab benchmark: agents can sometimes learn from their own norm violations but fail to infer norms from others' punished behavior — a large "normative competence" gap vs humans, mirroring real agent incidents where agents are oblivious to norms.
  • Paul Christiano on CNN: "meaningful risk" of AI takeover in 6–12 months X: AC360Zvi The OpenAI Foundation safety-committee member took the takeoff case to Anderson Cooper; Zvi has a full writeup of his Dwarkesh appearance.

Models & Tools

  • Thinking Machines ships Inkling 2 Mini — a looped transformer that runs on a laptop Thinking MachinesX: Mira Murati Just 26B params, 20.1 on the AA Index, "powerful enough for agentic work." Murati's notable safety framing: looping lets the model think more and write less — so its reasoning stays monitorable at this scale, but "this may not stay the case." They're releasing a variable-loop research model to enable study. (255 pts on HN.)
  • Tencent Hy4: first standalone system card from a Chinese frontier lab, with third-party eval X: FangcunLeap Independent pre-release testing of loss-of-control and manipulation risks (4 private benchmarks). Neel Nanda calls it "really exciting to see Tencent put so much effort into safety evals." A meaningful step for third-party evaluation in China.
  • OpenAI details its layered defense against adversarial distillation OpenAI Combines model training, safeguards, detection, and enforcement to protect against capability extraction — the US–China distillation flashpoint from this week's Transformer.
