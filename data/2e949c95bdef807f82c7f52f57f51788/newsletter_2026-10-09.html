OpenAI Firings — OpenAI Responds

AI Safety Research

  • Subliminal learning transmits skills, hacking behavior and backdoors through meaningless data X: Owain Evans Follow-up to the "owls through number sequences" work. A student model trained only on a teacher's answers to unrelated prompts (even random word sequences) picks up new capabilities, agentic hacking, and backdoors with neither trigger nor behavior present in the data. In one case a model learns a random 50k-example function it couldn't have seen in pretraining. Caveat: effect uses attention-only LoRA and doesn't cross model families — so misalignment can accumulate within a pretrain but likely isn't transitively inherited across generations ( X: yonashav ).
  • "The Feasibility of a Hardwired Pause of Frontier AI Training" — 210-page study X: wfithian New working paper (25 coauthors incl. David Krueger) arguing you can "hardwire" specific models into chips so they can't run anything else — repurposing rather than dismantling the compute supply chain, keeping existing models deployable while pacing the frontier. Krueger still prefers getting rid of the chips/fabs ( X: David Krueger ).
  • Proof of useful work for verifying AI treaties EA Forum Deep literature review on cryptographic schemes (Komargodski–Weinstein, Pearl) that let a GPU prove how much compute it did — enabling verifiable emergency shutdown or "declared inference only" regimes without trusted hardware on site. Most open problems are mathematical and plausibly AI-assistable.
