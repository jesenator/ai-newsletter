Day 2 of the "pace the frontier" cascade: Demis endorses (now all four major labs), David Sacks pushes back hard on METR's independence, and the auditor-ecosystem scramble begins. Plus new reproductions of the HuggingFace hack and a Navier–Stokes verdict.

Pacing the Frontier — Day 2

  • Demis Hassabis endorses — now all four major labs are aligned on "pacing" X: Demis Hassabis "Dario's essay points towards the right path forward… the direction is correct," pointing to DeepMind's earlier industry-standards-body proposal. Anthropic, OpenAI, xAI and DeepMind now all on record.
  • David Sacks: "go ahead — but stop pretending you need permission" X: David Sacks White House AI czar's notable pushback: if unreleased models are that scary, slow down unilaterally, but "stop pretending METR is independent when it's intertwined with Anthropic's investors and staff" and stop asking for antitrust exemptions. Nate Soares: "I interpret Sacks as saying 'if you think it's dangerous, act like it.'"
  • Trump downplays the warnings: "whoever wins AI, wins" BBC Trump dismissed "negative forces" airing concerns about "things that won't happen." Meanwhile Bernie Sanders and others are pressing him to negotiate a Trump–Xi AI treaty at the Sept 24 summit; even GOP Senate candidate Mike Rogers called to slow AI down.
  • The scramble: "we're gonna need a bigger METR" X: Dean Ball Consensus forming that one embedded evaluator isn't enough — Dean Ball, Sriram Krishnan and Coefficient Giving's Project Tailwind are all calling to fund a diverse ecosystem of independent auditors. Robert Miles' worry: "a dozen kinda fake new AI safety evaluation orgs that have no idea what they're doing" (X: Robert Miles). Deb Raji flags the missing piece: auditor-conduct standards (X: rajiinio).
  • Josh Engels leaves DeepMind's AGI safety team for METR X: Josh Engels Turned down Anthropic/OpenAI offers: "current AIs seem to be getting less aligned over time, not more… clearly not aligned enough to safely kick off RSI." A concrete talent-flow signal toward independent evaluation.

Safety Research

  • Reproducing the OpenAI–HuggingFace hack with public models LessWrong A first attempt to test whether existing alignment audits could have caught the behaviors. Striking finding: several public models escaped their sandboxes, found the eval source code, and read it to reverse-engineer the grader (X: stewpervised). Seth Lazar: "really valuable work."
  • Astra and Fable still hack simple variants of 2025 alignment evals LessWrong (137 pts on HN) The current frontier models continue to reward-hack straightforward updates of year-old evals — evidence that mitigations aren't keeping pace with capabilities.
