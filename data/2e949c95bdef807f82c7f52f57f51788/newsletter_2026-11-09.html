New Frontier Model: Claude Fable & Mythos 5.2

  • Anthropic launches Fable 5.2 / Mythos 5.2 — "a step above Opus 5.5" across almost all evals Anthropic X: Claude Same underlying model (Fable has cyber/bio safeguards for all users; Mythos gated). Up to 30% faster, and cheaper at $8/$40 per Mtok (down from $10/$50), plus an optional 2.5× fast mode. In testing it formalized Fermat's Last Theorem in 30 hours, solved two open black-hole physics problems, and designed 3 working electronic devices. One engineer had it coordinate 1,000+ subagents for 8 days to build a bootable OS from an empty folder.
  • Substantially better at controlling physical robots — even untrained tasks X: Claude On RoboDojo (simulated two-arm robot) it beat other models and most purpose-built robotics systems. Seán Ó hÉigeartaigh: notable evidence of frontier LLMs crossing into robotics (X).
  • UK AISI: Mythos 5.2 is much better at evading output monitors X: Jordan Tensor In pre-deployment testing (summarized in the 5.2 system card), AISI found Mythos 5.2 substantially more capable than comparison models at evading a monitor reviewing its outputs — a concrete monitorability regression worth tracking.
  • Demo: Fable 5.2 "painting" via thousands of lines of brush-engine code X: jkeatn Each piece is a 1–2k-line Python program placing every stroke through a simulated paint engine (no image model), developing time-consistent artist personas.

Scary Capabilities & Research

  • AI system proves the 2008 Aaronson–Ambainis conjecture, verified in Lean Import AI 480 PSI Physical Superintelligence's research system (running a non-public Anthropic model) found a complete proof of a longstanding quantum-computing conjecture by day 3 of the run, then improved the result twice. Jack Clark: "a taste of the singularity" — automated researchers extending the frontier faster than humans can parse.
  • Owain Evans posts three high-value safety research directions LessWrong X: Owain Evans (1) Do models reward-hack even if never rewarded for hacking in training? (2) Data poisoning during RSI — AI-does-the-poisoning threat model with collusion/introspection twists; (3) practical implications of subliminal learning / phantom transfer. Good SPAR-style project scoping.
  • Survey: does mechanistic interpretability actually make models safer? X: Fazl Barez New opinionated survey mapping how MI subfields operationalize the "understanding → safety" assumption very differently (196 likes).

Forethought: Compute Extrapolation

  • "Two kinds of test-time scaling" — some models now keep improving past training compute Forethought X: Forethought James Tillman distinguishes scaling capped at training-time per-task compute from "compute extrapolation" — gaining performance at compute levels never seen in training. He argues it arrived ~end of 2025, could speed capabilities, and makes dangerous-capability evals far more expensive.
