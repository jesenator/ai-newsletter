Slow Sunday, no new frontier releases. A Chinese humanoid robot "outruns" Usain Bolt, a local 27B model pulls off a scary reverse-engineering feat, and GovAI opens a founder program directly relevant to your Kairos work.

Top: Robot Beats Bolt's 100m

  • A humanoid robot ran 100m in 9.39s at the World Humanoid Robot Games in Beijing BBC Al Jazeera "Tianzhuo" (Beijing Innovation Centre of Humanoid Robotics) beat Usain Bolt's 9.58s record in a preliminary heat — obviously not directly comparable, but a striking legible marker of hardware progress. Robert Miles: "the robot Olympics might actually be better… if a runner falls over, they don't explode" (X: Robert Miles).

Capabilities

  • A local Qwen 3.8 27B reverse-engineered a commercial app's license check in ~30 min XDA HN (110 pts) Running offline in 17GB VRAM, it did pure static analysis of thousands of lines of arm64, recovered a deliberately-obscured RSA verification key, self-corrected a wrong first guess, and built a working bypass — after first catching the author's jailbreak attempt. "A local 27B is now a real input to threat models." Very CivAI-shaped cyber-uplift demo.
  • "ox-alpha" is unusually buggy — more like Opus than the latest gen X: Andreas Stuhlmüller Had a new model "ox-alpha" review his emacs codebase (45 commits); Fable then found 14 significant bugs + a dozen smaller ones in its work — regexes that never match, infinite hangs, cache invalidation. A rare negative capability data point on an apparently new model.

Productivity / Technical

  • "Why your local LLM feels dumber than it is" Level1Techs HN (401 pts) Deep technical dive: quantization, attention-backend choice, sampler settings and 700+ stacked inference dependencies all shift token probabilities, so the same open weights feel worse locally than the reference host. Practical takeaway: check the model card's sampler/chat template (too-low temp is why Qwen loops in its thinking block).
