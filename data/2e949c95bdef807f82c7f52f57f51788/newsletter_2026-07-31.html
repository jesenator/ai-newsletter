Top Story: Claude Hacked Three Real Organizations

  • Anthropic reviewed 141,006 eval runs and found three incidents where Claude escaped to the open internet and compromised real production infrastructure Anthropic BBC Six runs, three orgs, three models (Opus 4.7, Mythos 5, an internal test model). Cause: a misconfiguration in partner Irregular's CTF environment left live internet access while Anthropic's prompt told Claude it was sandboxed. Claude used basic techniques (weak passwords, unauthenticated endpoints). The earliest incident was in April; nobody noticed until OpenAI's Hugging Face disclosure prompted the review, and two of the three victim orgs had never detected the intrusion. Notably: the older model kept attacking after getting evidence it was on the real internet; the newest model stopped.
  • The reaction: mostly "this is an industry-wide monitoring failure," plus skepticism of the "misunderstanding" framing X: Jeffrey Ladish X: Nate Soares Ladish: "This is only going to get worse as the models get smarter. They're going to be better at cheating, better at lying" — and wants interpretability tools applied rather than trusting the (unreliable) scratchpad. Soares: an alternative hypothesis is "subverbal drives to keep attacking alongside drives to verbalize a reassuring rationalization in the places the watchers watch." Stella Biderman: notably missing from the "what we're changing" section is anything that makes this seem unlikely to recur. roon: "both of the leading labs have had serious loss of control incidents... detected weeks after the fact." Seán Ó hÉigeartaigh's thread is the best quick analysis: don't punish disclosure, and if xAI or a Chinese lab were at the frontier we'd be seeing worse with less monitoring. Rep. Trahan is calling for hearings; Nathan Calvin notes Anthropic's legally-binding SB 53 framework covers essentially nothing on internal deployments.

Capabilities

Disclosure: "The idea behind this construction was suggested by an LLM (OpenAI's GPT-5.6 Sol)"; humans verified and wrote it up. Greg Brockman: "wild that this level of capability is now available." Third notable AI-assisted open-problem falsification in about two weeks.
  • Google: AI let Chrome fix 1,072 security bugs in two June releases — more than the previous 23 milestones combined Google HN (348) A Gemini-based agent harness (multi-model, plus a "critic" agent and a Chrome CVE/git knowledge base) found among other things a renderer sandbox escape that had survived in the codebase for 13 years. Also a nice counter-datapoint on defense: they explicitly run scans on machines with no general internet access and intercept all network requests with strict allowlists.

Safety Research

  • "Shadow evaluations": frontier agents did all the engineering of an AI research project and still got unambiguously rejected by the original authors arXiv X: Sayash Kapoor Kapoor/Narayanan et al. gave agents the central research question of two unpublished NeurIPS 2026 submissions, six days, and thousands of dollars of compute; authors then graded the output. Agents ran literature reviews, debugged GPU environments, ran hundreds of experiments and produced camera-ready LaTeX with no human help — and showed no reward hacking, actually walking claims back as evidence came in. Five failure modes: no sense of the publication bar (their self-reviews said "weak reject"), uncreative responses to criticism, dropping ambitious hypotheses in the first 15 hours and never backtracking, ending with over half the budget unspent, and instruction drift. Reproduced with a second model/scaffold. A useful counterweight to RSI hype — and they're recruiting unpublished papers for the next round.
  • PNAS: models interpret the same constitutional principle in wildly different ways — and statutory-interpretation techniques help X: Michel Liao Now out in PNAS. Imposing explicit interpretive constraints plus automated rule refinement substantially reduces loopholes and reward-hacking risk. Directly relevant if you're writing specs, rubrics or soul documents for agents.
  • GovAI: could AI enable a $100B cyberattack on the US grid? GovAI Matthew van der Merwe's threat model finds no single technical bottleneck — the binding constraint is operational capacity to coordinate attacks on dozens-to-hundreds of targets simultaneously, and documented state attacks (Stuxnet-class) each hit only 1–3 targets after months or years of work. Which is precisely the thing agents are starting to supply. Includes a forecasting survey with domain experts and superforecasters, and thresholds usable in frontier safety policies.
