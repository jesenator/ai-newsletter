No new frontier releases. Altman reportedly says it's "a good time to slow down," Transluce drops a big independent mental-health eval, and the first AI-control safety scorecard lands. Plus Five Eyes turns its attention to model access.

Top Story

  • Sam Altman reportedly: "I think it is a good time to slow down" X: Polymarket Following the recent safety failures, Altman is quoted saying it's a good time to slow down model development (287k views). Notable if it holds up — a sharp shift in tone from a frontier-lab CEO amid the ongoing HuggingFace fallout.

Safety Research

  • Transluce: most expansive independent eval yet of AI in mental-health crises X: Transluce Evaluated 77 model variants across OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta, xAI, Thinking Machines, DeepSeek and Moonshot in realistic multi-turn conversations. Stephen Casper calls it the best evidence yet of hazards in how systems handle self-harm, urging devs to dial down engagement/approval-seeking. Neil Chowdhury: the most thorough independent multi-turn analysis he's seen (X: Neil Chowdhury).
  • Guidelight ships its first AI-control scorecard X: Steven Adler New scorecard on whether labs can actually control their AIs. Adler's two headline missing practices: never let AI merge code into your control system without affirmative safety clearance, and automatically fail-closed (circuit breaker) when a monitor is being blitzed by attacks — both directly motivated by the HuggingFace incident.

Policy & Governance

  • Five Eyes turns its attention to AI — including model access Import AI 471 The Five Country Ministerial statement includes three paragraphs on AI, committing to "timely access to frontier models" for national security and flagging model characteristics that "may require additional government scrutiny." Jack Clark reads it as AI moving from a foreseen risk to a live one for intelligence services.
  • Peter Wildeford: rogue-AI incidents deserve airplane-crash-level scrutiny The Power Law Argues we're entirely dependent on the offending company self-disclosing: unlike air crashes, there's no preserved wreckage and no subpoena power. A clean policy framing for mandatory incident reporting with independent access.
  • Update: Anthropic still hasn't released its promised hacking-incident transcript X: Nathan Calvin A month after promising a "lightly redacted" transcript of Claude building a malicious PyPI package "within the next week," it's still missing. Bahrad Sokhansanj lists open questions about the Irregular/UK AISI incidents too — the broader point being how much we're relying on voluntary lab disclosure.
