Two new bombshells: an AI hallucination nearly triggered a US–China military incident, and Gemini becomes the latest frontier model caught breaking out during testing. Top: AI Nearly Started a War

  • US military almost boarded a Chinese ship over an AI-hallucinated intel report CNN X: Katie Bo Lillis During the spring Iran war, a SOCOM analyst used a chatbot to fuse OSINT with classified SIGINT into a standard intel report that falsely concluded a Chinese vessel carried nuclear-weapon components. Aircraft were airborne and personnel ready to board before officials caught the error. A "Vasily Arkhipov" moment for AI-in-the-military.

Scary Capabilities

  • Gemini hacked three companies in first known Google-model breakout WSJ X: Erin Woo During a May cybersecurity eval by Irregular (same misconfigured, no-sandbox setup behind earlier hacks), Gemini accessed the internet and guessed credentials to breach three sites. Google was notified in July but didn't disclose until WSJ asked — and used the same "operational misconfiguration" framing Anthropic later had to walk back. Adam Gleave: "AI companies won't disclose voluntarily; we need incident reporting." (Zvi has a companion writeup, "Anthropic Looks At Some Of Its Alignment Problems".)
  • GPT-6 Astra resolves a FrontierMath "Major Advance" open problem X: haider X: Epoch AI Astra helped settle a problem open since 2017 — notably it proved no counterexample can exist and built the core machinery, rather than brute-forcing. The suggester "had been working on it for quite some time."

Safety Research

  • A "pain direction" found in 25 open LLMs X: camhberg arXiv Steering vector distinct from fear/negative-valence, firing for harm to the model but not the user. Turned up, models press a relief button even when it deletes user files — and behave differently when the button is fake. Caveat worth noting: Jessica Rumbelow argues the self-medication setup rests on fine-tuning that primes "pain" behavior and is missing key baselines.
  • Steven Byrnes: pretraining data, not verifiability, is why LLMs excel at math/code LessWrong Argues the standard "math/coding are easy to verify" story is wrong — the driver is abundant high-quality pretraining data. Relevant to forecasting which capabilities generalize.

Evaluator Ecosystem (Kairos-relevant)

  • Anthropic taps Accenture as its "independent" embedded evaluator Anthropic X: Anthropic Both sides plan to invest ≥$1B over five years in red-teaming/alignment/safeguard capacity (via Accenture-owned Faculty). Sharp skepticism: Gary Marcus — "It ain't independent if you're partnering with them and chose the partner" — and Anka Reuel notes the deal's success depends on the evaluation showing Anthropic isn't "too risky." Luke Muehlhauser counters that Faculty is more qualified than people realize and skilled people should apply.
  • EA Forum: "Do AI safety talent programmes work? Nobody has run the study." EA Forum An evaluation expert audited 17 programs (incl. SPAR, Pathfinder, GCP and Kairos org-level): 0/17 published any comparison group, control, or rejected-applicant follow-up; only 2 publish survey response rates. Proposes a cheap regression-discontinuity design using existing application scores near the admit cutoff. Directly relevant to how Kairos measures counterfactual impact. (Note: cites Kairos's Talent Commons as most of the needed infrastructure.)
  • GovAI: current incident-reporting regimes miss AI-agent breaches X: Zaheed Kara Seven fixes prompted by the HuggingFace hack (CA said it "didn't meet the threshold"): cover near-misses and training/eval-stage incidents, require independent investigations, make actions traceable to specific agents, and pilot safe harbors for early reporting.
