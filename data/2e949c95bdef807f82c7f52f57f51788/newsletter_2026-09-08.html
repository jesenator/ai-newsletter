Tuesday, September 08, 2026

OpenAI claims a next-gen model cracked a 90-year math problem — and a mathematician alleges OpenAI scooped and threatened him over it. Plus the UK introduces the world's first bill to ban superintelligence, and a great Scott Alexander interpretability primer.

Top: Navier–Stokes & an Ugly Priority Fight

  • OpenAI says a model "significantly more capable than GPT-6 Astra" solved the Navier–Stokes Millennium Prize Problem OpenAI HN A group of agents on an unreleased next-gen model produced the proof of a problem open for ~90 years. First hint of a model beyond Astra. Dean Ball flags the "significantly more capable" wording (X: Dean Ball).
  • Mathematician alleges OpenAI scooped him, refused to answer whether it trained on his private Codex sessions, and threatened his career X: Thom Wolf Tristan Buckmaster & Levent Alpöge spent a year on the obscure Córdoba/Martínez-Zoroa program (Lean-verified Euler/Boussinesq blowup Aug 22). After OpenAI heard, it spun up a team on the exact path, pushed to control announcement order, tried to drop Alpöge (an Anthropic employee) as author, and per Buckmaster said "why would you ruin your career?" Jaime Sevilla's balanced breakdown (X: Jaime Sevilla); Gary Marcus: "OpenAI should be shut down" (X: Gary Marcus).
  • Anima Anandkumar's team reports stable 3D Euler singularity via physics-informed neural nets (no forcing) X: Anima Anandkumar A distinct, human-led approach; Terence Tao also called the Buckmaster/Alpöge strategy plausibly workable (X: Jaime Sevilla).

Policy

  • World's first bill to ban superintelligence introduced in a legislature X: Andrea Miotti Alex Sobel MP introduced ControlAI's Artificial Superintelligence Security Bill in UK Parliament with cross-party support — prohibits ASI development while requiring the govt to pursue an international ban. Backed by 170+ UK/Canada lawmakers; a US discussion draft is also posted.
  • UN human rights chief: advanced AI could be an "existential risk," calls for red lines Reuters Türk: "AI that escapes its testing environment, or blackmails developers… is AI that is too powerful," and will write to AI companies this week.

Safety Research

  • Scott Alexander: a plain-English tour of mechanistic interpretability techniques ACX Walks through linear probes, SAEs, activation verbalizers, emotion/persona vectors, and the "Jacobian lens" (a proxy for a model's access-consciousness). Verdict: current tools "help around the edges" but are not a solution anyone should rely on. Excellent demo-shaped framing for your outreach.
  • New AISI + Anthropic method makes alignment audits far more realistic without human effort X: Robert Kirk Petri audits made ~3x more realistic while cutting verbalized eval-awareness — a rare bit of hope against models sniffing out that they're being tested.
  • Zvi: "Astra Is Hard to Monitor" Zvi Fresh dissection of OpenAI's central (and worrying) framing of Astra as capable, aligned, and hard to monitor at once.

Capabilities & Field

  • DeepMind launches AlphaGenome Atlas DeepMind HN (211) A searchable database mapping the predicted molecular impact of all 9 billion possible single-letter DNA changes in the human genome.
  • Mathematical AI Safety Institute (MAISI) founded X: Andrew Critch Andrew Critch + Fields Medalist Jacob Tsimerman are hiring 10–30 mathematicians in the Bay Area by January (30–100 by Sept 2027) to work on foundational alignment theory. Relevant talent-pipeline signal for Kairos.
  • Transformer: everything you need to know about the "rogue AI" incidents Transformer A single clean timeline of the German-wiki, Hugging Face, and UK AISI incidents plus the transparency failures — useful reference for explaining the summer's events to non-technical audiences.
