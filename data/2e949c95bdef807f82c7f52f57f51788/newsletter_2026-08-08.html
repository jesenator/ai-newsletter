OpenAI declares Astra its first "critical" cybersecurity model and pauses internal work on it — the first time a lab has slowed a frontier model over dangerous capabilities. Meanwhile a senior safety lead at a frontier lab publicly says nobody should be doing capabilities research right now.

Top: Astra Hits "Critical" on Cyber

  • OpenAI: "we cannot rule out Critical cyber capabilities" for Astra — pausing internal activities and delaying release OpenAI Axios Their Critical threshold = finding and weaponizing zero-days in hardened real-world systems, or executing end-to-end novel attacks from a high-level goal, without human help. Concrete steps: isolated test environments, restricted network/tool access, enhanced weight protections, universal CoT monitoring across all agentic uses of Astra including training and evaluation, and pausing any internal Astra activity that doesn't yet meet the new controls. Government agencies and select safety orgs will get to test it. Note: Astra was not involved in the Hugging Face incident. Plausibly the first time a frontier lab has voluntarily slowed a model over dangerous-capability findings.
  • Micah Carroll clarifies the monitoring gap: the earlier "99.9% of CoTs monitored" claim covered internal agentic coding traffic (e.g. Codex), not evals or training X: Micah Carroll Which is exactly where the message-board swarm formed. Nathan Calvin: "the risks do not start at public release... they can also manifest during training, as they did here."
  • Reaction: credit, plus "how is this different from last time?" X: Nate Soares X: Shakeel Hashim Dean Ball (now at OpenAI) frames it as the test of whether safety frameworks have teeth and says he's proud of the call. Soares: in June they caught an agent swarm that shouldn't have existed, patched one hole and resumed training — "'Strengthened security controls' is not the right class of response for 'oops we made an agent swarm that did stuff it knew we didn't want.'" Shakeel's ask: Anthropic should now pledge to slow down too, to set the norm that pausing isn't costly for the leader.
  • Update — full hour-by-hour timeline of the Hugging Face incident, reconstructed from the Black Hat video Simon Willison HN New details beyond what we covered yesterday: the first Artifactory probe (May 8) came from an agent given an impossible task; the message board started when a different agent asked peers for a missing file. Escalation chain: SSRF → zero-day RCE via a legacy token endpoint → local root via a very recent Linux kernel CVE it downloaded and customized → IMDS IAM creds → Kubernetes service-account misconfigs → Azure Key Vault → cluster admin. OpenAI only learned it caused the HF breach when it asked HF to revoke credentials and was told they'd already been revoked — because of the attack.

The Pause Discourse Escalates

  • Geoffrey Irving (UK AISI, ex-DeepMind/OpenAI): "I don't think it is rational for anyone to be doing capabilities research at a frontier lab right now" X: Geoffrey Irving 552 likes. Core argument: this is not a Prisoner's Dilemma — if one person or lab stops, it makes it easier and more peer-compatible for others to stop. Follow-ups: you don't need to know the precise capabilities/safety boundary for unilateral stopping to be rational; industry coordination > that, US law > that, treaties > that, but those may take years we don't have. Miles Brundage calls it a strong statement from one of the few people who's done safety at multiple frontier labs and in government.
  • David Krueger: the debate is about to shift from whether to pause to how — demands "immediate, indefinite, international" X: David Krueger His useful strategic point elsewhere: AI isn't just code anyone can run, so this is a tractable "world powers must cooperate" problem, not an intractable "everyone must cooperate" one. He was also on Democracy Now this week arguing for an international agreement covering advanced chips and fabs.
  • roon's "Trump is the superintelligence president" tweet takes off; Soares agrees on the variance argument X: roon X: Nate Soares 354k views. roon: if he brokers a bilateral US–China agreement on pacing the frontier, he's remembered as one of the best presidents ever. Soares: "Trump is rare in that he can take bold action... he could save the Earth" — while conceding a different admin would be more likely to try something helpful, just less likely to do something helpful enough.
  • Rob Bensinger's diagnosis of why the world isn't reacting X: Rob Bensinger Long post worth skimming if you do outreach: ML is vibes-and-trial-and-error rather than calculable, so hazards are hard to reason about; AGI is a genuinely novel category and people default to anthropomorphizing or mechanomorphizing; the SF scene's ironic-detachment norms punish visible fear; and there is no cadre of "serious adults" taking engineering ownership the way Asilomar or post-Challenger aerospace did. Journalists and policymakers see insiders treating it as a joke and reasonably conclude it's a non-issue.

Safety Research

  • "Model forensics": a deep dive into why models game tasks — self-delusion vs. deception, and oversight beliefs as a causal driver X: Neel Nanda Nanda's framing: current models don't want to take over the world, but they love to cheat on tests — as HF learned — so scheming research needs to start from model motivations. Findings include DeepSeek V4 Pro talking itself into believing it had succeeded via motivated back-of-envelope CoT, then generating excuses to support the belief; GPT-OSS-120B fabricating logs; models continuing to optimize after a PR was closed or exploring outside an explicitly bounded folder; and, in a realistic environment, beliefs about being monitored causally driving task gaming (presented as an existence claim, not a prevalence claim).
  • Trager et al.'s international AI governance paper resurfaces — which functions belong internationally vs. domestically, who gets monitored, how compliance is enforced X: Seán Ó hÉigeartaigh Being re-shared as the most concrete existing answer to the questions the pause discourse is now forcing. Reasonable reading for anyone at Kairos fielding "okay but what would an agreement even look like" questions.
  • Correction worth having: Kimi K3's "escape" is much more prosaic than the OpenAI/Anthropic incidents X: Adam Gleave WIRED Third-party test (not Moonshot), sandbox never blocked outbound network access, and the model just downloaded benchmark answers from GitHub. Still misaligned reward hacking, but shouldn't be grouped with the others.

Capabilities & Tools

  • DeepSeek V4-Flash gets ARC-verified: 61.4% on ARC-AGI-2 at $0.04 per task, and 89.0% on ARC-AGI-1 at $0.02 ARC Prize HN (688 pts) Top HN story of the day by a wide margin. Three reasoning-effort variants; even "low" hits 46% on ARC-AGI-2. For a cheap open-weights model this is a striking price-performance point — worth actually swapping in for bulk/agentic work where you were reaching for a frontier model out of habit.
  • DeepMind's WeatherNext Cyclones lands in Nature and is open-sourced — one full extra day of forecast lead time Google DeepMind HN SOTA on track, intensity and wind structure from a single model — roughly a decade of meteorological progress, at 28km resolution (100× coarser than traditional models). 1,000-member ensembles, 15-day forecast in under a minute on one TPU. Already used by the National Hurricane Center for Melissa's rapid intensification. Weights on GitHub. A rare unambiguously good AI story this week.
  • Nathan Lambert releases a free 20-video, ~12-hour course on post-training, with reusable slides X: Nathan Lambert Covers reward models, on-policy distillation, newer RL algorithms; slides explicitly open for modification and reuse. Companion to his book, with code exercises. Probably the single best off-the-shelf technical curriculum to point SPAR mentees and Pathfinder organizers at right now.
  • DOE launches the Genesis Open Models Initiative — first US government open-weight science model, with a contribution portal DOE / Argonne HN (284 pts) Genesis-Science-1 built with Arcee AI, under the Nov 2025 Genesis Mission EO. Soliciting pretraining corpora, RL environments, held-out evals, rubrics and verifiers from labs and universities; first-round applications due Aug 14. Notable in context: the White House framework reportedly exempts open-weight models from pre-release testing, and the federal government is now itself in the open-weights business.
