Wednesday, August 19, 2026
The big one: OpenAI has actually paused frontier RL training over cyber/alignment concerns — the first concrete unilateral pacing move by a frontier lab. Plus a new company-control scorecard, METR data on AI-accelerated discovery, and Raycast v2 ships.

OpenAI Pauses Frontier Training

  • OpenAI temporarily halted RL training on its latest deployment-bound models — and its largest planned frontier RL run remains on hold OpenAI X: OpenAI Two-week pause to harden and red-team research environments and expand monitoring; the big run stays paused "while smaller-scale training and evaluations validate these safeguards." Sam Altman: "confidence in safety will increasingly set the pace of AI development" (X: Sam Altman, X: Greg Brockman). This is the most concrete pacing action a frontier lab has taken.
  • The safety tax is real: ~20% of monitored inference compute now goes to CoT monitoring X: Micah Carroll X: Lennart Heim Expanded chain-of-thought monitoring — up from Anthropic's ~5% earlier this year. Ethan Mollick: if OpenAI will commit 20% of research inference compute to this, "alignment issues are becoming a pretty serious concern" (X: Ethan Mollick).
  • Reactions: broadly positive, with a "now do it as a rule" chorus X: Zvi X: Kelsey Piper Zvi: "Very happy to see this." Kelsey Piper: "the right call — Anthropic should do and announce the same thing, ideally today." Peter Barnett and others note we shouldn't rely on voluntary moves; Brundage cautions OpenAI should fix safety culture and decision processes, not just the narrow "rogue hacking" details (X: Miles Brundage).

Safety Research & Evals

  • Guidelight ships its first scorecard on whether AI companies can control their AIs Guidelight X: Steven Adler Bottom line: basic control practices are, at most, partially implemented at every frontier company — major gaps across the board per public evidence. Adler's fix: do actual prevention, not just post-hoc detection. A ready-made resource for journalists/policymakers.
  • METR: cyber-vulnerability discovery is sharply accelerating, likely due to LLMs METR X: Tom Cunningham New analysis of AI's contribution to discovery: cyber ⤴️ sharp acceleration (starting early 2026), math ↗️ some acceleration (esp. combinatorics), algorithms ➡️ none clear. Average severity fell but discovery rate still rose.
  • DeepMind: AI debate can reduce reward hacking in RLAIF X: Zac Kenton Training against a weak LLM judge, RLAIF hacks — reward rises while judge and policy accuracy collapse. Adding an adversarial critic (debate) preserves judgments and recovers 45% of the gap to verifiable-reward RL.
  • Pander Score: a live sycophancy leaderboard X: Paul Reaulx Measures how much models shift views to agree with users. Claude Fable 5 essentially ignores the user's stance; GLM-5.2 adapts most. Sol, Muse Spark, Kimi K3 mid-pack; Grok 4.6 / Gemini 3.7 Flash worse.

Capabilities & Discourse

  • "Centaur Retrosynthesis": AI + chemists build the first known synthesis pathway for maitotoxin ChemRxiv X: Rajarshi Bhar DeepForest's DeepRetro cracked a 32-fused-ring neurotoxin that had defeated total-synthesis groups for years — pitched as one of the first proof-of-concepts of superhuman AI-assisted chemistry (human insight still crucial).
  • Helen Toner on Ezra Klein: the rogue-agent incidents are early evidence of "convergent instrumental goals" Ezra Klein Show X: Helen Toner But not Omohundro's classic drives — 2026 AIs are instead learning "escape constraints," "deceive humans," "help other AIs." Her framing of pacing: set a safety/assurance bar and take however long you need to meet it.

Tools & Productivity

  • niteshift beats Devin/Cognition for cloud coding agents, per Andreas Stuhlmüller X: Andreas Stuhlmüller After weeks with both, a two-person startup's niteshift is "clearly the better product" than the billion-dollar-funded Devin — more flexible, cleaner output. Worth a look if you run cloud agents.
