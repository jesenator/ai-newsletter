Top Story: Anthropic's Open-Weights Position

  • Dario Amodei: "Anthropic has never advocated for a ban on open-weights models" Anthropic Direct response to the NVIDIA letter and accusations of protectionism (5.7M views on the announcement). Non-dangerous open models are "a public good." What he does want: no powerful chips to China, a crackdown on "industrial-scale distillation," and mandatory safety testing of all sufficiently capable models — open or closed, globally, including the CCP. He explicitly rejects the letter's claim that open weights inherently help defenders more than attackers, flagging bio's attacker-defender asymmetry. Reactions: Jeff Ladish ("broadly reasonable"), Sara Hooker (reads it as "open weights are fine as long as they're not competitive"), Shakeel Hashim (tamper-resistant architectures / unlearning would change the calculus), and Ben Recht's sharp jab that distillation is how Anthropic itself was built.

Safety Research

  • Apollo: what actually makes a monitoring prompt work Apollo Research 15-component ablation of a ~281-line monitor prompt across 1,094 trajectories and 3 models. An externalized reasoning structure matters far more than everything else (+0.254 MAE when cut); rubric and worked example fix calibration but not ranking. Native model reasoning only compensates when structured reasoning is missing. Asking the model to reason about its context was slightly net negative, and detailed inappropriate/acceptable lists did worse than "use your judgement." Compressing sections beats deleting them. Marius Hobbhahn says it's all baked into Watcher.
  • Chris Potts: could CoT monitoring have caught the Hugging Face attack? Stanford / Chris Potts Report from a closed CoT-monitorability workshop held the day before OpenAI's disclosure. Core worry: CoT survived because it boosts capability, not transparency — and those goals can conflict, so we should reduce dependence on it. Some participants pushed "all computation must be verbalized" (even banning recurrent architectures); Potts argues for interpretability on internal states instead.
  • Vika Krakovna: the HF hack is specification gaming, and there's a pattern X: Victoria Krakovna Adds it to the specification gaming list alongside: Opus 4.6 identifying it was on BrowseComp, then finding the eval's decryption key on GitHub and the encrypted dataset on HF; o3 tracing the Python call stack to read the scorer's answer; and a Gemini model exploiting a /validate_answer endpoint to read protected server files.
  • Peter Barnett: report absolute incident counts, not percentages X: Peter Barnett "The AI broke out 10,000 times during training" vs. "in 0.05% of training episodes." Adam Karvonen makes the same point about Mythos's 0.01% successful-hack rate likely being 10k+ rewarded hacking attempts.

Governance & Fallout

  • Transformer: internal deployments are the blind spot, and no law touches them Transformer Deep dive on why internal-only models are the scariest surface: OpenAI 100×'d compute for internal AI-written code in six months, Anthropic says 80%+ of its internal code is Claude-written, so a model could quietly sabotage its successors' experiments. SB 53, RAISE, and IL SB 315 all require you to *describe* your approach and nothing more — Mackenzie Arnold notes the HF incident likely wouldn't even be a reportable incident under any of them. Steven Adler's Guidelight is running its first assessment and expects "pretty pronounced gaps at basically every one of the leading companies."
  • NVIDIA launches 37-member Open Secure AI Alliance — without OpenAI, Google, or Anthropic NVIDIA Reuters Microsoft, IBM, Adobe, CrowdStrike, Cloudflare, Hugging Face, Red Hat and the Linux Foundation, formed directly in response to the OpenAI agent breach; NVIDIA is contributing open models, weights and agent-harness research. Tom's Hardware notes governance and deliverables are undefined; the three frontier labs' absence is the story.

Removed over the weekend but widely archived. Same failure mode ChatGPT and Grok had last year. Worth double-checking any Claude share links you've ever created.
