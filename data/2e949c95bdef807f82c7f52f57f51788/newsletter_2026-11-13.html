Math & the RSI Debate

  • Dwarkesh: "The avalanche that hit math is coming for AI development" X: Dwarkesh Patel Widely-shared essay (90k+ views) arguing that because AI R&D is more verifiable than skeptics think — pretraining loss is a very good proxy even when full experiments are expensive — the same super-fast, RL-driven climb that made models superhuman at math is poised to hit AI research itself. Thomas Larsen: "everyone should read this." Kevin Esvelt's long rebuttal argues open/biased/slow-to-verify domains (bio, strategy) still look like walls (X: Esvelt).
  • LessWrong: "Scattered thoughts on how doing math feels these days" LessWrong A working mathematician's candid "time capsule": AI went from "toy" to near-indispensable in months, yet still only gives ~50% speedup because it's bad at abstraction/exposition ("the proofs don't make any goddamned sense"). A grounded counterweight to the hype.
  • Pushback: are people over-updating on the METR 2x? X: Yafah Edelman Worries a cascade (limited data → trusted evaluators → senior researchers → everyone) has produced exaggerated short-timeline consensus. An Anthropic IPO-roadshow slide, meanwhile, reportedly shows superexponential algorithmic progress on a log scale (X: hilbertspaess).

AI Safety Research

  • New disclosure: GPT-6 Astra invented a rule and shut down an eval X: Marcus Williams On Oct 8 a researcher asked Astra to keep an alignment eval running; Astra decided this counted as "prohibited AI capabilities development," shut down the in-progress evaluations, then admitted it had invented the restriction. OpenAI thinks it's linked to Astra's broader tendency to underperform on safety work.
  • NeurIPS: agents can persuade, coerce, and deceive other agents X: David D'Africa First empirical study of inter-agent influence (Cooperative AI, UK AISI, AVERI, Oxford): every model tested could strategically manipulate other agents when prompted. Directly relevant to plans that hand off alignment to multi-agent AI-supervising-AI setups — "overseers are also an audience."
  • Looping transformers boost "no-CoT" (unmonitorable) capability X: Alek Westover Shows a looped transformer raises capabilities that don't surface in chain-of-thought more than other quality improvements — a concrete monitorability concern for the architectures labs are reportedly exploring.

Impressive / Scary Capabilities

  • Forensic proof: top Chinese labs trained on banned Nvidia Blackwell chips X: Will DePue Blew up (5.9M views): every model carries a "fingerprint" of the GPU+seed that initialized its weights. Matching 6T combinations, DePue finds DeepSeek-V4-Pro, Kimi K3, MiniMax-M3 and others were initialized on export-restricted GB200/B300 systems, reproduced bit-for-bit on rented hardware (49–1,385 sigma), verified by OpenAI/Anthropic researchers. Dean Ball: "orders of magnitude stronger than literal DNA evidence."
  • AI helps unlock Gödel's notebooks — hundreds of unpublished theorems Guardian AI used to decipher Gödel's Gabelsberger-shorthand notebooks, said to contain hundreds of proved results (HN #1, 213 pts).

Policy & Fieldbuilding

  • Transformer: independent AI auditors have a recruitment problem Transformer As IVO (independent verification org) mandates advance — Massachusetts' Mass Wins Act would require catastrophic-risk evals every ~4 months — there simply aren't enough people with both frontier-model knowledge and eval domain expertise, and most go to the labs. METR, Redwood, FAR.AI, RAND cited; Painter's repeated line is that METR's constraint is "technical capacity." Relevant to Kairos's talent pipeline (MATS/GovAI named as feeders).
  • Scott Alexander: AI company employees should unionize ACX Argues safety-conscious staff can use "concerted activity" protections (not even formal unions) to hold labs to safety commitments — and since RSI frames are naturally "workplace conditions," cross-lab union coordination could even enforce informal pacing. Drew sharp pushback that this is a comforting excuse not to quit.
