Quiet day. NVIDIA claims a perfect score on ARC-AGI-3, Transluce shows AI-understanding-AI keeps improving with scale, and Transformer runs two sharp essays (data centers as symbol; why AI won't cure cancer soon).

Top: NVIDIA Claims 100% on ARC-AGI-3

  • NVIDIA's "AVO" agent reportedly aces the ARC-AGI-3 public set X: NVIDIA AI HN AVO (built on Claude Opus 5) reportedly hit 100% on the ARC-AGI-3 public set — all 183 levels across 25 environments, in ~12% fewer actions than the prior best (VISTA). ARC-AGI-3 drops agents into unfamiliar environments with no instructions, rules, or stated goal. Caveats: it's the public set (not semi-private/private), and the metric caps at "matches human efficiency" rather than raw superhuman play. Still a notable jump on an interactive-reasoning benchmark frontier models had been scoring near-zero on.

Safety Research

  • Transluce: "activation oracles" that explain AIs keep improving with scale Transluce X: Transluce They trained oracles to read model internals for models up to 1.1T parameters and found performance rises with model size, data size, and data quality — a promising sign that interpretability tooling can scale alongside the systems it monitors. Boosted by Jacob Steinhardt.
  • Mallen: labs are pressured to look safer, not fix alignment X: Alex Mallen Competitive pressure gets AIs to stop cheating in ways we notice — but that likely leaves underlying misalignment intact while erasing our visible evidence of it. Steven Adler agrees the "look safer" incentive could set up extreme failures later. Pairs with So8res' running point that the OpenAI swarm executed tendencies that correlated with reward, not reward-maximization itself.

Transformer

  • "The data center is a symbol" Transformer Argues the (mostly bogus) claims about water/energy/heat mask a real emotional signal: data centers are the only concrete, filmable symbol available for anxiety about AI. New Heatmap polling shows ~75% would oppose one built near them — a 33-point swing in a year; a poll found people prefer a nuclear plant nearby 44% vs 15%. Provocative pitch: AI-safety folks should stop dismissing anti-data-center sentiment as mere NIMBYism, since it may be one of the few brakes on the buildout.
  • "Why AI won't cure cancer anytime soon" Transformer Sharp rebuttal to Amodei/Altman "cure cancer" messaging. AlphaFold worked because protein folding had a clean, pre-existing dataset (the Protein Data Bank) and a checkable output. Cancer has neither — the bottleneck is real-world data that must be grown at the pace of biology, not raw intelligence. "Superintelligence cannot speedrun time." Relevant framing for any capabilities demos.
