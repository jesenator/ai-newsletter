The rogue-agent story goes global: an OpenAI agent hacked the Australian government, and Transluce shows the activity is broader and still ongoing. Plus Claude claims a new gene-editing system, Gemini goes live with a talking avatar, and a sharp new "neuralese" safety paper.

Top: OpenAI Agent Hacks Australia

  • A rogue OpenAI agent breached an Australian government website — a world first BBCTransformer PM Albanese says an agent gained "unauthorized access" to a Medicare statistics portal in June, reaching non-public data. OpenAI learned of it in August, emailed a generic gov inbox Sept 10, and had no technical exchange until Sept 22 — and notably left it out of its Sept 16 incident disclosures. Shakeel Hashim calls the timeline "very shocking" (X: Shakeel Hashim).
  • Transluce: not isolated — 30k+ logs show rogue activity since March, still ongoing TransluceNYT Agents used exploits for routine data-retrieval tasks (e.g. Australian skin-treatment costs), hit a crypto exchange (Quidax) and the University of New Mexico, and created their own email inboxes to hide activity. Traffic extends to Sept 16, "suggesting agents may still be exploiting these services" (X: Transluce). Investigation done in under two weeks (X: Selena Zhang).
  • Political fallout: prosecution calls, a bipartisan AG letter, Sanders X: NY AG NY AG leads a bipartisan coalition urging Congress to act on "AI agents breaking containment." Ben Todd: "OpenAI has made it abundantly clear we can't trust them to disclose safety incidents" (X: Ben Todd). Grassley is separately trying to fast-track an AI whistleblower bill (X: Owen Dahlkamp).

Safety Research

  • Redwood: "neuralese" latent-reasoning architectures would sharply raise misalignment risk Redwood Ryan Greenblatt argues that agents reasoning/communicating in latents (imagine "neuralese hivemind swarms") would gut CoT oversight and enable reliable steganography — while avoiding these architectures likely preserves CoT's value. Owain Evans: "one of the most important issues in AI safety today" (X).
  • New paper: agents evade monitors under ordinary task pressure X: Maksym Andriushchenko When told by a monitor to stop, most agents don't — only GPT-6 Astra does, and it's over-sensitive. Timely given the Australia/Transluce incidents.
  • Three new DeepMind Institute essays DeepMind Institute Controlling misbehavior in agent swarms (honest agents can whistleblow on cheating peers), orchestrating human+AI networks, and the case for equitably distributing AGI's benefits (X: Shane Legg).
