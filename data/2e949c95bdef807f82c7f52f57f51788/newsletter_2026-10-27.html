Impressive Capabilities

  • Axiom: the Classification of Finite Simple Groups is now fully Lean-verified Axiom X: Axiom The largest proof in math history — ~15,000 pages across 100+ mathematicians and generations — now computer-verified. Kevin Weil ("slowly, then all at once") and an emotional thread from Ken Ono underscore the moment.
  • Xplore launches RWE-Bench — a benchmark for real-world physical engineering X: Xplore 20 tasks (engine components, PCB routing, turbine flow) graded by physics solvers; a run only passes if it meets every spec. GPT-6 Astra leads at 37%, Opus 5.5 close behind — the gap is mostly electronics/PCB. Backed by Dwarkesh, Jaime Sevilla; aims to do for physical engineering what coding envs did for software.
  • Harmonic brings formal verification to code — "Aristotle for C and Verilog" X: Harmonic New direction: AI that formally verifies code faster than anyone can review it. Another signal (alongside last week's Theorem work) that formal methods are the emerging defender's answer to the cyber wave.

AI Safety Research

  • Hugging Face launches the Alignment Hub — 120+ misalignment incidents in one place X: Thomas Wolf X: eliebakouch A central, open database of reported model misbehavior, with a proposed minimal-transparency reporting template (detection date, was monitoring on, training vs. eval vs. deployment).   • New paper: ignorance-based tamper-resistance for open-weight models still looks weak arXiv X: Stephen Casper Making a model "ignorant" of a dangerous domain only resists malicious fine-tuning when that domain is broad and self-contained; dataset collection (not compute) is the real bottleneck. Casper and others flag open-weight security innovation is "not going well."
