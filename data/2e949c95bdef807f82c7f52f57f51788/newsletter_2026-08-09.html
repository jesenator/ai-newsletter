Top: The Clean Version of the HF Story

If my swarm goes rogue, your swarm can check it. But if our allegedly separate swarms act as one hive-mind, we're in trouble." A cleaner threat model than "one scheming model" — and it undercuts AI-overseeing-AI schemes that assume independence.

Continual Learning vs. Regulation

  • Dwarkesh: 8 predictions for the continual-learning era — and why pre-deployment testing is the wrong frame Dwarkesh If base weights update daily from millions of real sessions, "train then deploy" stops being a meaningful boundary; he'd rather see monthly or quarterly risk inspections than a single pre-release gate. Other sharp ones: alignment research is almost entirely about frozen weights and would need to change wholesale (plus a new attack surface — users injecting backdoors into the base model); labs feel pressure to ship their best model earlier, since Anthropic's four-month Mythos internal-only gap would mean ceding four months of deployment learning; and continual learning finally gives labs a real moat via switching costs, so expect carrots and sticks to get enterprises to allow training on their sessions.
  • Brundage: safety people outside industry have agreed on this for years — it's the companies that resist X: Miles Brundage Important caveat to the above: state laws aren't "what safety people think is ideal," they're the equilibrium after years of negotiation with labs — internal deployment is covered at all only because people pushed hard for minimal coverage. Also from him: "The most mistaken and harmful idea in AI today is that companies have the right incentives + laws already to sort this stuff out" (link).
