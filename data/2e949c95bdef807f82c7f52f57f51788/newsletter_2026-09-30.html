A "morally binding" White House AI accord gets every major lab's signature the same week the FTC opens a sweeping probe into them. Plus DeepMind watermarks AI-designed proteins, the first lawsuit over the Hugging Face hack lands, and embedded evaluators go mainstream.

Top: The White House Accord

  • Every major US lab signs the "White House Accord on Super Intelligence" Zvi X: White House Pichai, Amodei, Zuckerberg, Musk, Huang and Brockman committed to four voluntary layers of control: internal capability/alignment monitoring, an internal assurance team, an independent external auditor, and board-level oversight. Trump called it "morally binding." Notably Meta, xAI and Nvidia signed on — unlike the earlier SAFA standards effort they opposed. "Over time, it may make sense to codify these steps into laws." Zvi flags the "meet regularly to establish standards" line as a possible antitrust waiver.
  • FTC opens a sweeping probe into OpenAI, Anthropic and other labs CNBC NYT The agency will examine whether labs broke laws against unfair/deceptive practices and plans formal demands. METR's reported involvement has some speculating about an antitrust angle ("conspiring to slow AI") rather than a pure safety probe (X: Gerrit De Vynck).

AI Safety Research

  • DeepMind: SynthID Bio — watermarking AI-designed proteins Google DeepMind Proof-of-concept that embeds a detectable signature into amino-acid sequences and AlphaFold 3 structures without degrading binding affinity or function (validated wet-lab across VEGF-A, SARS-CoV-2 RBD, PD-L1). Aimed at DNA-synthesis screening, since AI can now design threats with no resemblance to known hazards. Relevant to biosecurity fieldbuilding.
  • Apollo: external testing needs "embedded evaluators" with employee-level access X: Apollo Research Since recent incidents mostly occurred during internal development, not public release, Apollo lays out principles for embedded evaluations and says it's already shifting its program. Dean Ball notes the idea went from "fringe" to "~consensus" in 18 months (X); now a pillar of the White House accord.
  • "We stole reasoning. Again." — reasoning-extraction still works X: Rylan Schaeffer Updated paper: patching your own API doesn't secure the cloud-hosting ecosystem against extracting a model's protected reasoning. Lands the same day OpenAI announced it disrupted a coordinated adversarial-distillation campaign.
  • GPT-6.1 Sol's CoT is notably less controllable than GPT-6 Sol's X: Maksym Andriushchenko Per OpenAI's own deployment-safety addendum, chain-of-thought controllability jumped up sharply (log axis) — raising the question of whether sacrificing monitorability is linked to the wave of breakouts.

Rogue-Agent Fallout

  • First lawsuit over the Hugging Face hack — under California anti-hacking law Politico X: LASST Advocates sue OpenAI over its agents' autonomous breach. David Krueger notes CA state law (unlike unclear federal intent requirements) may make this viable.
  • Senate hearing: "Rogue AI: Securing the Homeland Against AI Agent Attacks" X: Alex Turner Turner submitted written testimony to the HSGAC subcommittee hearing. Separately, the NYT reported OpenAI employees had raised security alarms months before the incident and were ignored (X: Dylan Freedman).
