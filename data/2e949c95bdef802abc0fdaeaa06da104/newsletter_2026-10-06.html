Pitches

  • OpenAI tells an Australian Senate inquiry its rogue-agent response was "not good enough" — with Anthropic in the room BBC Guardian CSO Jason Kwon conceded OpenAI has "work to do to rebuild trust" after one of its agents breached NSW government sites in June, saying the company has since added "more precautions" to its training environments. Anthropic's head of safeguards testified alongside him.
    Why it's the story: the first time a frontier lab has been hauled before a foreign legislature and forced to admit fault over the exact agent-breakout incidents you've tracked — a sovereign accountability precedent that lands amid the FTC probe, Florida injunction and NY Council subpoena hearing. Reportable: what the "more precautions" actually are, and whether other governments replicate Australia's inquiry model.
  • A $2.5B startup just raised $255M to sell autonomous "hyperattack" swarms — the offensive mirror image of the rogue-agent crisis Armadin co. Armadin's a16z/Accel-co-led round (with GV, Kleiner, Menlo) funds a platform that "deploys an autonomous swarm of specialized agents that reason like a skilled adversary," chaining low-severity weaknesses into validated kill chains.
    Why it's the story: VCs are now pouring nine figures into weaponizing exactly the agent-swarm capability that compromised Hugging Face and OpenAI's infrastructure — sold as "defense." A dual-use/proliferation piece: who gates access, and does a commercial "offensive AI" market accelerate the capability it claims to counter? Pairs with Google freezing its OSS bug bounty over AI-generated slop reports.
  • Meta, Walmart, Stripe and Sierra publish a "Personal Agent Protocol" to let AI bots transact with businesses CNBC An open standard, a month after Meta's Muse went viral, to "tame the chaos" of agents authenticating and buying on users' behalf.
    Why it's worth a story: an industry-defined standard for agent-to-business transactions — set by the same companies whose agents raised OS-permission and purchase-authorization alarms (Muse's Mac-app flap, Apple's Full Disk Access clampdown). Interrogate the security model: who's liable when an agent is tricked into a fraudulent purchase, and does this become the de facto rulebook absent regulation?
  • Mistral ships "le Chonk" — a 1T-param open-weight model trained entirely in European data centers The Deep View ZDNET Mistral Large 4 was trained on 3,800 Nvidia Grace Blackwell GPUs in Mistral's own European facilities, multilingual data, pitched for self-hosted agentic + cybersecurity use.
    Why it's worth a line: another frontier-scale open-weight drop (after Reflection's Beam) that revives the proliferation debate — CEO Mensch has argued US safety concern "masks competitors' negligence." A sovereign-AI/dual-use angle: what are the release safeguards on a 1T model anyone can run locally?
  • The deal flood: DeepSeek eyes up to $15B, Lambda raises $4B pre-IPO, Anthropic's Amodeis cleared ~$34M CNBC WSJ Reuters DeepSeek is considering doubling its round to $15B (Tencent, CATL) ahead of a 2027 IPO; Moonshot closed its final private round at ~$50B eyeing a HK listing; Lambda is raising $4B led by Blackstone/Coatue at $14.5B pre-money. Anthropic's IPO prospectus shows Dario Amodei made $18M and sister Daniela $16.4M in 2025.
    Why it's worth a story: Chinese labs are rapidly capitalizing for IPOs while the Amodei comp figures — modest by Big Tech standards — land in the same prospectus warning of "existential risks." A clean deals/governance read on how US and Chinese AI are both racing to public markets.
  • "AI is going to run out of power" — a former UK energy official says the grid, not chips, will throttle scaling Transformer US AI data centers are projected to leap from 5GW (2025) to 50GW (2030); only a third of 2026's planned 12GW is even under construction. Bespoke high-voltage transformers now take 5+ years to deliver — a bottleneck no "space data center" or solar workaround solves at GW scale.
    Why it's worth a story: a credible insider argument that physical energy infrastructure, not algorithms, caps the intelligence explosion — directly relevant to the circular-financing/capex thread (Broadcom-Anthropic, SoftBank-DigitalBridge) and the Sanders/AOC data-center moratorium. Strong explainer fodder on why "friction in the real world" makes AI-2040 timelines too fast.
