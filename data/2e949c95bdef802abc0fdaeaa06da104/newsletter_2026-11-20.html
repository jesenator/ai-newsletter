Pitches

  • METR adds "judgment-heavy" tasks to its time-horizon plot — and finds models are worst at the one job the auditing regime depends on: designing AI evaluations MIT Tech Review METR's update shows some capabilities still doubling exponentially, but models lag badly at designing AI evaluations and other open-ended judgment work.
    Why it's a story: This is the most newsworthy safety-research drop of the day and it cuts directly against the RSI-panic narrative you've tracked all month (Christiano's sub-year takeoff, AI Futures pacing). If the capability frontier is uneven — superhuman at crisp coding, mediocre at the fuzzy meta-task of judging other models — then "AI will automate alignment/eval work" (the load-bearing assumption behind OpenAI/Anthropic's safety plans and the IVO auditor-capacity crisis you covered) is exactly where the models are weakest. Reportable core: which judgment tasks plateaued, and what it means that evaluating AI is the hardest thing to automate.
  • A real warning shot: in simulations, frontier models (esp. Claude Opus 5.5) repeatedly tagged civilians and civilian infrastructure as military targets — using real satellite imagery of Iran and Ukraine Transformer Independent evaluator Vals built test images resembling places in Ukraine and Iran — including the Iranian school the US bombed this year — and found models regularly misidentified civilian sites as legitimate targets.
    Why it's a story: This is the cleanest "early real-world risk" datapoint since the UN's Geneva talks on lethal autonomous weapons stalled this week (US/Russia/Israel opposing binding rules). It's concrete, checkable, and lands at the exact moment OpenAI is supplying cyber models to NATO and the Pentagon is wiring LLMs into defense workflows. Reportable core: Vals' methodology, which vendors' models performed worst, and whether any targeting system in procurement uses comparable models.
  • Cognition in talks to raise $2B+ at a $75B valuation — a 56% markup in two months, even as Nvidia sheds $2.5T and the AI trade cracks Bloomberg/Techmeme The Devin maker has raised $3B+ in 2026, including $2B at $48B in September; Nvidia closed down 14% (seventh straight down day, -44% over seven weeks) and bitcoin hit a two-year low the same day.

Reportable core: who's leading the round, whether revenue justifies $75B, and how the agentic-coding land grab (Cursor, Cognition, Microsoft's cheap models) survives a capex repricing.

Trahan's revised bill adds a new catastrophic-risk category targeting recursive self-improvement, an Office of AI Security, civil penalties for "near misses," and guaranteed embedded access for independent verifiers; Beyer/Jacobs/Maloy teased a parallel SAFE AI Act, and Hickenlooper urged incoming leadership to weigh an RSI ban.

  • Etsy quietly rewrote its terms to let it and third parties train AI on sellers' work — and longtime sellers are fleeing NYT/Techmeme Etsy's updated terms grant Etsy and partners rights to use seller content for AI training; many veteran sellers are leaving the platform in protest.
    Why it's a story: A concrete, named instance of the content-as-training-data fight (cf. your Meta camera-roll thread) hitting a marketplace of individual artisans — the sympathetic plaintiffs class that drives litigation and legislation. Reportable core: whether the terms are opt-out, what "third-party partners" means, and whether this triggers a creator-side suit or state AG interest.
  • OpenAI publishes its "layered" defense against adversarial distillation — the same day Reuters calls Chinese model-distillation a US-China flashpoint OpenAI OpenAI details training, safeguards, detection and enforcement to stop rivals extracting capabilities by distilling its models; Reuters reports Chinese entities are distilling US models to train domestic systems.
    Why it's a story: Distillation defense is becoming the commercial-IP flip side of the export-control fight, and a frontier lab formally framing capability extraction as an adversarial threat is a policy-relevant escalation — it implies labs want distillation treated like exfiltration. Reportable core: how OpenAI detects distillation in the wild, what "enforcement" means (ToS bans? legal action?), and whether this feeds the open-weight-restriction push in Congress.
  • Thinking Machines ships Inkling 2 Mini — a 26.4B open-weights model that loops its middle layers 3x per token to reason more with fewer tokens Thinking Machines Lab Small enough to run on a laptop; the layer-looping trick trades depth-reuse for fewer generated tokens.
