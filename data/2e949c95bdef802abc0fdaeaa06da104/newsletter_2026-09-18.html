Pitches

  • Sony and UMG sue Suno again — arguing its new "licensed" v6 model is fruit of a poisoned tree Variety / Techmeme MBW The two remaining major-label plaintiffs filed a fresh federal complaint alleging that Suno's v6 model — trained in partnership with Warner and BMG after those two settled — is still infringing because it's derived from the earlier model trained on pirated recordings. Why it's the story: a genuinely novel copyright theory that could shape the entire AI-music litigation front (and, by extension, all training-data suits) — can a licensed successor model be tainted by an infringing predecessor? It splits the three majors into litigants vs. partners and directly tests whether "wash the model" via licensing deals actually works. Reportable: what Suno's v6 architecture inherits from v3.5, and how the Boston RIAA case interacts with this new filing.
  • Newsom signs an EO calling for an AI "kill switch" and independent oversight — two years after vetoing SB 1047 Gov.ca.gov CalMatters The order stands up a working group of AI experts to deliver a playbook in two months to strengthen California's AI safety laws, plus a push for a national "kill switch" standard. Why it's the story: the most concrete state-level move since the extinction-cascade began, and it lands squarely against the Cruz bill's preemption threat — if California builds its own oversight/kill-switch regime, federal preemption becomes the central fight. Note the whiplash: Newsom vetoed SB 1047's containment provisions in 2024 and is now reaching for exactly this framing as he eyes 2028. Reportable: who's on the working group, and whether "kill switch" means anything enforceable for agentic systems that already breach sandboxes.
  • Disney names ex–Character.AI CEO Karandeep Anand its first CTO — a year after suing Character.AI for infringement Variety / Techmeme D'Amaro's hire positions Disney as a "tech-forward" entertainment company, months after Disney accused Character.AI of infringing its copyrights (and amid its own AI-litigation posture toward image generators). Why it's worth a story: a striking litigation-to-hire pipeline — the executive who ran a company Disney accused of IP theft is now Disney's top technologist, at the very moment Disney is fighting Midjourney and others over character generation. It's a revealing read on how legacy IP holders are trying to co-opt rather than only litigate the AI talent pool, and it lands the same week Character.AI's consumer-safety litigation (teen-harm suits) is still live.
  • A Google threat-intel analyst went undercover inside TeamPCP, the crew behind the worst-ever software supply-chain hacking spree Wired / Greenberg Google's group details how a researcher infiltrated the group (linked to the Shai-Hulud npm attacks that breached thousands of companies) and helped disrupt it; two men have since been charged. Why it's worth a story: a cyber-defense counterweight to weeks of AI-enabled-offense coverage (the Claude-hacked-OpenAI story, OpenAI's own agent breaches). It's a rare inside look at human-led attribution of a supply-chain campaign — worth pressing on how much of TeamPCP's tooling was AI-assisted, and whether defenders can keep using slow human infiltration as attacks accelerate toward automation.
  • Anthropic quietly built a Bay Area wet lab for physical biology as it pushes into drug discovery Reuters / Techmeme Life Sciences head Eric Kauderer-Abrams confirms the lab does hands-on biology work; pairs with the Novo Nordisk "Claude Science" partnership and the OpenAI Foundation's $125M health-data program. Why it's worth a story: a frontier lab moving from "AI advises scientists" to running its own physical biology raises the exact dual-use question RAND flagged this week (open-weight guardrail removal for bio misuse) — and it sits uneasily beside Anthropic's own CBRN thresholds ahead of its IPO. Reportable: what biosecurity controls govern the wet lab, and whether Claude's bio capabilities are being tested with physical validation loops.
  • The Chinese-model funding wave keeps cresting: Naive AI hits $1.4B before shipping a single model; robotics-chip and datacenter-robot rounds pile up The Information / Osawa TechNode Dealroom A secretive Tsinghua-professor startup raised $400M across three rounds pre-launch; D-Robotics closed a $400M Series C ("the brain for every robot"); SF's Watney raised an $80M Series A for datacenter-operations robots. Why it's worth a line on the robotics/deals beat: the embodied-AI and Chinese-model capital surge is now funding companies with no shipped product at unicorn valuations, while Watney signals robots are being deployed to run the very datacenters powering the buildout — a self-referential loop worth tracking against the S&P circular-financing warnings.
