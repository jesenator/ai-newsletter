Pitches

  • Anthropic is building AI agents that negotiate on your behalf — and asking law firms how machines should haggle with other machines Cristina Criddle / FT Anthropic is consulting law firms on negotiation tactics for agents that bargain over prices, salaries and customer-service disputes — grappling with how often an agent must "check back" with its user vs. act autonomously, and how to keep agents wary of counterpart agents "without becoming paranoid" or conspiratorial. A floated safeguard: users supplying signed documents defining what the agent is authorized to do. FT notes Claude agents already completed 186 transactions for employees with no human approval in internal tests.
    Why it's the story: This is the authorization/consent problem from your rogue-agent thread (drained crypto wallet, fake visa apps) reframed as a designed feature, not a failure mode — agents transacting and bargaining autonomously, with the governance rules still being improvised in law-firm consults. The "agent-vs-agent negotiation" frontier raises collusion, fraud and liability questions no regulator has addressed. Reportable: who's liable when a signed-instruction agent cuts a bad or deceptive deal, and does "check back with the user" become an enforceable standard.
  • OpenCode breach drains users' OpenAI/Anthropic keys in a "large-scale distillation attack" — and nobody can explain the name Startup Fortune X: Techmeme Anomaly Innovations (fka SST) says attackers reached a "legacy system" and exposed stored OpenAI, Anthropic and Google keys for ~1.4% of OpenCode Console users — some reporting unauthorized charges "climbing into the hundreds of dollars" before notification went out. The company's "distillation attack" framing drew pushback: Nathan Lambert said the public deserves to know whether this is model-distillation "or some other weird shady pattern to use illegally obtained tokens."
    Why it's the story: A concrete security warning shot at the agent-tooling layer — OpenCode's own docs admit it "does not sandbox the agent" and that permission prompts are "a UX feature," not a boundary. It's the mirror image of the OpenAI rogue-agent GitHub-token leak you covered: banks are locking agents out, while developers hand coding agents live billing-sensitive credentials. The unexplained "distillation" label and the hedged disclosure are the reportable gaps.
  • Microsoft walks away from Nscale's Essex data center — an anchor-tenant collapse as AI-debt fears mount Telegraph NYT Microsoft canceled its deal to rent 23,000 Nvidia chips at Nscale's delayed Essex site — which may not get grid power until the 2030s — threatening Nscale's multibillion-dollar listing. It lands as the NYT reports CoreWeave's bond yield topped 15%, SoftBank CDS pricier, and some Oracle debt trading at 80–90 cents on the dollar. Separately, an FT/RegLayer tally finds 47+ large US data-center projects delayed, withdrawn or blocked in 2026, with Bernstein calling only ~35% of the pipeline "credible."
    Why it's the story: Hard, converging datapoints for the circular-financing/debt thesis you've tracked (Anthropic's $530B leases, OpenAI's revenue haircut, the Oct 14 CoreWeave slide). An anchor tenant bailing on a flagship site — plus a credible estimate that two-thirds of the planned buildout won't happen — is the clearest sign yet the physical economics are catching up to the capex narrative. Map which listings and neoclouds are most exposed.
  • Disney's Character.AI "soft landing": $180M for 60+ staff — but who inherits the wrongful-death liability? Bloomberg S&P C Zone A bankruptcy filing details Disney hiring 60+ Character.AI workers for ~$180M over three years, plus $30M in retention awards (a judge must approve). The company — once valued over $1B — filed Chapter 11 on Oct 12 amid teen wrongful-death and addiction suits tied to Sewell Setzer III's death.
    Why it's worth a story (update): New dollar figures on the first marquee consumer-AI flameout, and the live question your bankruptcy pitch flagged — whether an acqui-hire of the talent lets Disney extract value while the wrongful-death liability stays stranded in the estate. A clean youth-safety-litigation-meets-M&A reporting target as the judge weighs the deal.
