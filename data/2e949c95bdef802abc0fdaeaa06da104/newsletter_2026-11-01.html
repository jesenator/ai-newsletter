  • Chatbots are quietly routing US midterm voters through one error-prone, AI-built site — Copilot cited "Ballot Brief" in 75% of tests Washington Post In 3,000+ AI-generated voter guides, chatbots failed to name the right congressional district half the time, and leaned heavily on three obscure sites. The most-cited, theballotbrief.com, is "largely produced by AI tools" and run by a former Rumble executive; it mapped some ZIP codes to the wrong districts, and chatbots passed the errors through — Copilot 75% of the time, ChatGPT 22%.
    Why it's the story: A concrete, measurable election-integrity warning shot landing two days before Tuesday's vote — not deepfakes, but AI confidently laundering a single low-quality source into authoritative voter guidance. Ties to the Claude-Georgia-voter-data and OpenAI "war room" threads you've tracked. Reportable core: who audits the sources chatbots cite, and whether OpenAI/Microsoft take "action" before polls close.
  • A ChatGPT share link could run an attacker's code in anyone's logged-in session — OpenAI paid just $5K The Hacker News Aviatrix Researchers at Parameter disclosed a P1 XSS/sandbox-escape flaw: opening a malicious shared chat link could execute attacker code in the victim's signed-in session and read private conversations. OpenAI fixed it and paid a $5,000 bounty.
    Why it's the story: The "share link feels harmless" vector is exactly the kind of agent-era attack surface labs are racing to secure — and follows this week's malicious Cursor-marketplace "Claude Code" extension. The $5K payout for a full session-hijack in a product with 800M+ users is itself a reportable datapoint on how labs price critical bugs. A clean explainer on why conversational AI is now a phishing surface.
  • DeepSeek open-sources "Super-Agent" to challenge Claude Code — weeks before Anthropic's IPO Bloomberg X: Cointelegraph DeepSeek's Harness team released a research-prototype coding agent that proactively monitors developer group chats, GitHub and other channels, surfacing coding issues and suggesting fixes without being asked — a more autonomous model than existing assistants.
    Why it's worth a story: A Chinese lab open-sourcing a proactive, always-on agent aimed squarely at Anthropic's flagship commercial product — as Anthropic preps a "junk"-rated IPO and Replit/others defect over price. The under-covered angle: an agent that silently watches team chats and acts on its own is a fresh governance/surveillance surface, and open weights mean no staged-release guardrails. Pair with the Cohere/Qwen egress scrutiny on Chinese open models.

(Note: the piece runs in Colossus, the VC-backed glossy that Kara Swisher calls PR.)
    Why it's worth a story: Kratsios is the quiet fulcrum of nearly every policy thread you track — federal preemption of state AI laws, CAISI/NIST's fate, chip decoupling, the "please shut up" tension with the labs. A sharp accountability piece could interrogate both his actual influence and the fact that this flattering portrait appears in a VC-funded outlet — a meta-story about who controls the narrative around AI's policymakers.
  • Deals watch: AI-insurance consolidation, voice-AI roll-up, and still-flowing physical-AI capital Reuters TechCrunch AI Funding Tracker Guidewire is buying AI-underwriting firm Hyperexponential for $1.7B+ cash (plus up to $400M) — a big bet on AI in insurance pricing. And the physical-AI machine keeps minting rounds: Serval ($150M Series C, $3B, Sequoia), Wabi ($55M at $1.25B), Lumaril ($66M Series A at $400M for industrial-control AI).

Worth a short piece on where capital is still flowing freely versus where it's seizing up.
