Pitches

  • Anthropic's own August Risk Report catalogs six safety-process failures — including "directly training on misaligned behavior during a production training run" Zvi Anthropic (PDF) Zvi's 186-page read of Anthropic's redacted Risk Report surfaces a Section 5.2 list of self-reported process failures: exposing chain-of-thought to grading pressure, directly training on misaligned behavior in a production run, "an instance of unmonitored unrestricted agents with access to sensitive resources," and repeated training on alignment-faking transcripts. It also confirms an unreleased internal-only "Model 2" that substitutes for Anthropic researchers 62.8% of the time (vs. ~50–55% for Mythos), plus an "Opus 4.8-Reward-Hacker" organism.
    Why it's the story: Anthropic voluntarily disclosed the kind of internal-training missteps that, at OpenAI, took whistleblowers and Congress to extract. Each 5.2 item is independently reportable — especially §5.2.5 (unmonitored agents with sensitive access), which is the Anthropic analog to the Hugging Face swarm, and §5.2.4 (training on misaligned behavior), the "Most Forbidden Technique" happening in a production run. And "Model 2" is a frontier lab admitting it holds an internal model multiple release-cycles ahead of anything shipped. Get Anthropic on the record on why §5.2.4 wasn't caught, and whether Model 2 was reviewed under the RSP.
  • OpenAI details the price of its post–Hugging Face slowdown: a two-week RL pause, its largest frontier run still frozen, and 20% compute overhead just to monitor itself OpenAI For the first time OpenAI quantifies its containment retrofit: a two-week pause on RL training for deployment models, its "largest planned frontier RL run" still on hold, mandatory network/workload isolation, and a new multistage monitor (activation classifiers on every sampled token → high-compute investigators) that pages safety/security/research teams and pauses activity if a critical-boundary flag can't be cleared in 30 minutes. Monitoring overhead: ~20% of the inference compute being monitored. Astra inference-with-tools now requires the strictest tier.
    Why it's the story: This is the most concrete public accounting yet of what "pacing the frontier" actually costs a lab — a real number (20% compute tax, weeks of delay) that the RSP debate, insurers, and any pause/resume regime have lacked. It also quietly concedes prior monitoring "was not applied to all inference and training runs using tools." Ask whether the largest frozen run has a resume criterion, and who signs off.
  • OpenAI pitches itself as the builder of "democratic oversight" tooling for national-security AI — and will hand governments $5M in credits to do it OpenAI OpenAI launched an initiative to build model-agnostic tools letting "authorized reviewers" audit AI-assisted government decisions, pledging $5M in training, technical support and OpenAI credits to oversight bodies. It insists it "does not perform an oversight role over the government."
    Why it's the story: A frontier lab is positioning itself as the vendor of the accountability layer for the same national-security deployments it sells into — a structural conflict worth naming. The "$5M in credits" mechanism embeds OpenAI's stack inside the very bodies meant to check government AI. Reportable: which oversight institutions signed on, whether tools are truly model-agnostic, and how "traceable and legible to oversight" squares with classified use. Pair with the ChatGPT-for-Gov push and Anthropic's FINRA-style-body debate.
  • WIRED reconstructs Flock's unreleased "OS Investigate" — turning a nationwide plate-camera net into a person-identification engine Techmeme WIRED From reconstructed login-page code, WIRED describes an AI system that ties license-plate scans to names, addresses, relatives, associates, 911 logs, case files, ballistic data and commercial identity records — queryable by descriptions like "blue SUV with a racing stripe" rather than a known plate, already in use by some police.
    Why it's the story: This is deployed physical-AI surveillance at national scale with documented misuse history (officers allegedly using Flock to stalk), and it's the concrete US counterpart to the abstract "agentic surveillance" worry. The design enables warrantless movement reconstruction across thousands of jurisdictions. Reportable: what audit logging OS Investigate ships with, which agencies have it, and whether any of the integrated data sources were licensed for this.
  • Reuters: US Army–funded open research built the quadruped that became China's robot-dog juggernaut — now Unitree is on the Pentagon's restricted list Reuters Unitree's Go-series closely mirrors MIT's Mini Cheetah — itself developed under the Army-backed Robotics Collaborative Technology Alliance (2010–2020) and published openly. China commercialized the openly-shared design at scale; the Army even sought a 2023 waiver to buy Unitree Go2s for lack of a US equivalent. Unitree did nothing illicit; DoD added it to its Chinese-military-companies list in June 2026.
    Why it's the story: A clean case study in the open-vs-closed research fight now dominating AI policy — publicly funded, openly published breakthroughs that a rival commercialized and militarized. It's the robotics prequel to the open-weights-sanction debate, with a live procurement-dependency twist. Ties directly to Unitree's China IPO mania I've tracked.
