Pitches

  • OpenAI says it hit its "automated research intern" milestone — and is publishing internal RSI metrics for the first time OpenAI In a striking new post, OpenAI claims it reached Altman's fall-2025 goal of an "automated research intern by September" — a system doing research tasks that take a skilled human "a few days." It discloses hard internal numbers: as of mid-August the research org runs 3.1 agent-workdays for every human workday; the median researcher burns >$600/day of inference, the 90th percentile >$7,000/day; and it's "making strong progress toward creating an automated AI researcher by March of 2028."
    Why it's the story: This is the most concrete public data any lab has released on recursive self-improvement, and OpenAI explicitly frames it as an argument that labs "should be required to publicly track progress toward RSI." It lands directly on the safety + policy beats: the company admits "we do not yet know how to safely get all the way to aligned, full RSI" and that "more capable systems can become harder to monitor" — the same monitorability worry that defined the Astra/neuralese fight all week. Reportable: how "research intern" was measured, whether the 3.1x figure counts subagents, and how this squares with the post-Hugging Face RL pause it cites.
  • OpenAI quietly rewrote GPT-6 Astra's benchmark scores after launch — and the changes flatter Astra Fortune / Techmeme Emily Forlini reports OpenAI has revised several evaluation metrics in its Astra announcement since publishing Sept 3, and is continuing to change them post-launch in ways that appear to favor the model. Headline results already look harness-dependent — e.g. ARC-AGI-3 at 99.9% on a custom adapter vs. 62.7% on a provider-neutral harness; OSWorld/Terminal-Bench scores that shift with version and framing.
    Why it's the story: A direct follow to the reporting I co-bylined on Astra's "recurrent depth" technique. Silently editing a launch-day benchmark post is an accountability/transparency scoop — exactly the disclosure-posture question the neuralese debate raised (Dean Ball's "mandated independent audits"). It gives the litigation/policy beat a checkable artifact: diff the blog post versions. Reportable: which metrics changed and when, whether the White House framework OpenAI claims Astra "passed" relied on the pre-revision numbers, and whether any changes touched safety evals.
  • Kalanick's Atoms is quietly building robotaxi tech — hires Anthony Levandowski, takes $100M from Uber FT / Techmeme Travis Kalanick's Atoms is developing autonomous-driving tech and has hired Anthony Levandowski — the ex-Google/Uber engineer once at the center of the Waymo trade-secrets saga and later pardoned — after acquiring his company Pronto. Uber, which ousted Kalanick nearly a decade ago, has invested $100M.
    Why it's the story: A robotics-beat scoop dense with intrigue: the two most litigated figures in AV history reuniting under Uber's money, right as the sector's IP and safety questions (NHTSA's Cybercab probe) are hot again. Reportable: what stack Atoms is building, whether Levandowski's role reopens any Waymo/Uber IP exposure, and the terms of Uber's stake.
  • Seattle Times and Newsday sue OpenAI and Microsoft — while Microsoft funds the Seattle Times GeekWire / Techmeme The parent of Microsoft's hometown daily joined Newsday alleging the companies trained on their journalism without permission. Notably, Microsoft and OpenAI are both funders of the Seattle Times.
    Why it's the story: A fresh entrant in the copyright-litigation wave, landing days after the Trump DOJ backed OpenAI on fair use (Sept 2) and Microsoft's "fewer than 1% of Copilot logs regurgitate 16+ words" defense (Sept 5). The funder-vs-defendant conflict is a novel, reportable angle mainstream coverage is glossing over. Worth mapping how this fits the consolidating docket and the $1.5B Anthropic authors settlement precedent.
  • Neura Robotics raises $1.4B from Amazon and Nvidia at a $7B valuation Dealroom The German humanoid startup is building "robot gyms" where humans in sensor suits train robots — feeding the embodied-AI capital flood (Figure's $3.5B Nscale deal, XDOF's ~$1.2B data round).
    Why it's worth a look: Amazon and Nvidia co-investing in a European humanoid firm extends the "brains + bodies + compute" concentration pattern and the robotics-as-next-export-control-front thesis. The "robot gym" training model is itself the teleoperation-data land-grab story surfacing again. Reportable: how Neura's training-data approach compares to XDOF's, and the strategic logic of Amazon backing a humanoid rival to its own warehouse robotics.
  • AI gutted Kenya's essay-writing industry — a real-world "warning shot" for global gig labor NYT / Techmeme At its peak the trade paid 40,000+ people in Nairobi to do overseas students' homework; ChatGPT has dried it up, with few paths back to work.
    Why it's worth a line: A concrete, documented case of AI displacing an entire informal-economy sector — a cleaner labor-impact data point than the macro job-loss speculation, and a lens on how model deployment lands in the Global South. Ties to the under-30 AI-concern inflection (PEW) and the "deployment pause to protect jobs" sentiment surfaced in the LessWrong student field report.
