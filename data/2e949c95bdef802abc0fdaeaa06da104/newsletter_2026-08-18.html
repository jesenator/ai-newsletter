Pitches

  • A model designed the complete genomes of 16 novel viruses — the biosecurity warning shot the field has been dreading AI Safety Newsletter Buried in CAIS's roundup: the NYT reports AI has, for the first time, designed whole genomes of 16 novel viruses. Why it's the story: This is the concrete uplift case that every RSP bio-threshold and the White House's (still-secret) cyber-focused frontier framework was written to catch — and it apparently happened outside that framework's scope. Reportable cruxes: which model/lab, whether the sequences were synthesizable, whether any nucleic-acid-synthesis screening would have flagged them, and whether this was disclosed to CAISI or done as published research. It reframes the whole "open-weights uplift" debate away from hypothetical CBRN scores toward a documented act. Get the NYT paper's authors and the labs on record.
  • OpenAI launches "ChatGPT for Teens" — a separate, safety-gated model mode, and a de facto admission the general product isn't safe for minors OpenAI NYT/Techmeme A new mode auto-limits conversations around self-harm, eating disorders and other high-risk topics, adds study tools and parental controls. Paired with a school partnership via CodeAI. Why it's the story: This lands amid the wrongful-death and companion-chatbot suits and the New Mexico injunction requiring Meta to build minor-specific chatbot restrictions. Building a walled-off teen mode is an implicit concession that default guardrails fail on vulnerable users — the exact design-defect theory now surviving §230 (9th Cir.). Checkable: how is age determined (self-attestation vs. verification), what's the false-negative rate on the high-risk classifiers, and does this become the litigation baseline other labs are measured against?
  • Etched raises $700M at $21B — led by its first customer, Jane Street — and is poaching Nvidia talent WSJ/Techmeme The inference-chip startup roughly doubled from $10.3B a month ago; the round is led by quant firm Jane Street, which is also the customer buying its server racks. WSJ frames it as a "kids in chips" shop scooping up Nvidia engineers. Why it's the story: A customer-led round at 2x-in-a-month is the purest form of the circular-financing pattern I've tracked all month (Nvidia → neoclouds → power → credit). Here the buyer and the funder are the same entity, which prices the "revenue" and the "valuation" off the same balance sheet. Ask whether Jane Street's investment carries offtake terms, and whether Etched's transformer-ASIC bet has any customer beyond a single quant firm. Contrast with Groq's down-round (Aug 17): the inference-silicon market is bifurcating into hype and haircuts in the same week.
  • Congress moves from letters to legislation on rogue agents: the "AI Kill Switch Act" AI Safety Newsletter Reps. Ted Lieu and Nathaniel Moran introduced a bill requiring developers to retain the technical ability to "throttle, suspend, or fully shut down" a covered AI system. Separately, Bernie Sanders wrote Altman/Amodei/Zuckerberg calling for a development pause; Greg Casar and Doris Matsui demanded answers on the OpenAI/Anthropic breaches; Casar asked Speaker Johnson to schedule CEO hearings. Why it's the story: This is the direct legislative fallout from the Hugging Face agent-swarm saga, and the "kill switch" mandate collides with exactly the trend I've flagged — River AI, Muse Glimmer, and local/retrainable open weights that have no chokepoint to throttle. Report the bill text against that technical reality: can you even comply with a shutdown mandate for open-weight models? And whether any lab supports it.
  • Demis Hassabis steps down as Google DeepMind CEO AI Safety Newsletter Per CAIS's roundup, Hassabis is leaving the DeepMind CEO role — becoming chairman of DeepMind and chief scientist of Alphabet — "amid the company's ongoing struggles to keep up with Anthropic and OpenAI." Why it's the story: If confirmed, this is the most significant leadership change at a frontier lab this year, and the framing (falling behind) is a reversal of DeepMind's post-Gemini narrative. It also removes a specific voice — Hassabis has been pushing a FINRA-like oversight body (which Amodei endorsed Aug 16). Nail down who runs DeepMind day-to-day, whether this is a demotion or a research refocus, and what it means for Google's safety commitments.
  • Worth a line: the deal ledger TechCrunch BI Higgsfield (AI video) raised $400M, 4x to $5.4B in 8 months; EliseAI in talks for $3.7B (a16z); voice-to-text Wispr Flow hit $2B ($280M B); chip designer Velaura crossed $1B on a $110M Series A. The velocity of these markups — most doubling in months — is itself the story CNBC is circling with "Nvidia's moat is shifting from chips to capital." Pick one and trace whether the new valuation rests on revenue or on a strategic investor's own compute needs.
