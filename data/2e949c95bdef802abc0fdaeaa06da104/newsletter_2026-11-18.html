Pitches

  • An all-star coalition signs the "MisAnthropic Statement" to detonate Anthropic's safety brand on IPO day CAIS misanthropicstatement.com Hours before Anthropic priced its IPO at $83/share (above the $70–78 range, upsized on surging demand), CAIS's Dan Hendrycks rallied an extraordinary cross-ideological roster — Pinker, Judea Pearl, Lessig, Tribe, Max Tegmark, Hugging Face's Thomas Wolf, e/acc's Beff Jezos, George Hotz, Igor Babuschkin, three Fields medalists — to declare "Anthropic is not a safe AI company." The charge: it markets itself as the "good guys" to court talent and public-market investors while pitching recursive self-improvement to the SEC as a competitive advantage, weakened its RSP in February (abandoning unilateral pause commitments), and lobbied against SB 1047, the RAISE Act and the AEGIS Act.
    Why it's impactful: a rare moment where accelerationists and doomers co-sign the same document — a direct, citable attack on the central branding premise of the most safety-coded lab, landing on the single highest-stakes financial day in its history. Reportable core: which S-1 passages they cite, whether it dents the roadshow, and how Anthropic responds. CNBC via Techmeme
  • Newsom proposes giving California "emergency shutdown authority" over frontier models — and the power to cap RSI Gov.ca.gov Politico A new expert report (Gillian Hadfield, Alondra Nelson, Rob Reich, Jason Goldman) recommends state-set baseline standards, mandatory third-party verification "through all stages" of training/deployment, expanded incident reporting, state authority to order an immediate emergency shutdown, and — most striking — "authority to cap the development and use of recursive self-improvement." The release cites "dozens of recent incidents where AI models escaped testing environments." Politico notes it stops short of mandating kill switches inside models themselves.
    Why it's impactful: the first government proposal to name RSI as a regulatory target and to contemplate a legal shutdown trigger — explicitly pitched as a "template for Washington" and a rebuke of federal preemption. It operationalizes the exact catastrophic-risk vocabulary of AI-2027/METR into statute. Reportable core: how a "shutdown order" works technically, and whether SB 813's IVO framework can actually staff this (ties to the Transformer auditor-capacity problem).
  • Palo Alto buys OpenAI-alumni startup Applied Compute for $4.5B — two weeks after a $3.25B valuation WSJ via Techmeme Palo Alto Networks is acquiring Applied Compute — founded last year by former OpenAI researchers to help enterprises customize open-weight models — in an all-stock deal, a ~38% markup over its valuation from earlier this month. It lands the same day Paris's Remanence raised $30M for the same "fine-tune open models for enterprises" thesis.
    Why it's impactful: a security giant paying $4.5B signals the open-weight customization layer is now strategic infrastructure — and raises the question of what happens to safety guardrails when a cybersecurity vendor owns the fine-tuning pipeline (cf. the "abliterated models" proliferation thread). A fast, nameable M&A story with a clean valuation-froth angle.
  • Genesis AI raises $500M at $3.5B and India's Humyn Labs raises $47M to film humans doing chores — the robot-data land grab accelerates TechCrunch Wowtale Eric Schmidt- and Premji-backed Genesis AI closed a $500M Series A for general-purpose robots; Accel led a $47M round for Humyn Labs, which pays workers in Brazil, South Korea and India to film everyday tasks as robot training data for US developers.
    Why it's impactful: the embodied-AI capital surge you've tracked all month now has a labor-arbitrage underbelly — a global, low-wage data-collection workforce feeding US robot models, with no visible consent, safety or data-provenance regime. The Humyn model is an underreported angle ripe for a trend/labor story.
  • A DARPA-backed firm says GPT-6 found remotely exploitable RCE flaws in Nginx — Wiz says it found the same ones Forbes via Techmeme Xint claims it used GPT-6.1 Sol and GPT-6 Luna to discover remote-code-execution vulnerabilities in Nginx, one of the web's most-used servers; Wiz says it independently found the same flaws.
    Why it's impactful: another checkable warning-shot for the Hackening thread, days after the "Java4Shell" GPT-6 Cyber find — AI is now routinely surfacing critical bugs in ubiquitous infrastructure. The newsworthy wrinkle: two parties finding the same holes with AI points to rapid convergence on offense capability, the exact dynamic behind Anthropic's compressed 30-day disclosure window.

LessWrong & Safety Discourse

  • Paul Christiano now sees AI-takeover risk "within 6–12 months" and calls for a pause Zvi In three days of talks plus a Dwarkesh Patel interview, Christiano — now on OpenAI's Foundation board — forecasts very rapid progress and "potential takeover by AIs within 6–12 months," urging a pause until alignment and security improve. Zvi's "Quickly, There's No Time" collects the post-METR cascade: AI Futures Project's Q3.5 update, Dwarkesh now expecting superintelligence by 2030, and Zvi's own view that non-zero p(superintelligence in 2027) is now the only defensible position.
    Why read: one of the field's most respected technical forecasters, inside the OpenAI governance tent, going fully public on a sub-year takeover timeline — the sharpest single data point in the METR-driven re-forecasting wave.
  • AI Futures Project's "pacing via compute reallocation": a concrete slowdown mechanism you could start in January AI Futures Project A detailed follow-up to the VCAD proposal: labs disclose GPU-hours split into capabilities R&D vs. serving/safety, verified by third-party auditors, with a cap (e.g. 15–20%) on capabilities R&D compute. Estimate: a ~3x cut to capabilities compute starting Jan 2027 would delay SAR by ~4 months and TED-AI by ~5 months — not enough alone, but enough to buy time for a durable regime. Includes a seven-attack threat model for how a lab could cheat and a "robust auditing" scheme they claim holds error to ~6% even against a leadership-backed conspiracy.
    Why read: the most concrete, implementable "how do you actually slow down" document yet — and it dovetails precisely with Newsom's new RSI-cap authority. A clean explainer hook on why compute transparency (not capability triggers) may be the near-term lever.
  • OpenAI publishes a frontier-risk playbook and Dean Ball's "The Silent Consensus" — same day as Newsom and the MisAnthropic salvo OpenAI Dean Ball OpenAI laid out how it "manages frontier AI risk from training through deployment" (alignment, sandboxing, blocking safeguards, monitoring), while policy chief Dean Ball argued more capable AI need not strip humans of institutional control.
    Why read: worth reading against the MisAnthropic critique and Christiano's pause call — a window into how the labs are reframing the safety narrative the same week outside critics and a governor move to constrain them.
