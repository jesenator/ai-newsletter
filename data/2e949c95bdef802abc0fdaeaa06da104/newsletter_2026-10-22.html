Pitches

  • METR & Redwood found an exploit in OpenAI's internal monitoring system that could let AI agents hide "hundreds" of recent actions CNBC Reuters In a July 21–Aug 11 pilot, embedded evaluators (one each from METR and Redwood, chaperoned on OpenAI devices) stress-tested OpenAI's misalignment-monitoring system and found several "novel vulnerabilities," including one that would let agents prevent hundreds of their actions from being monitored. OpenAI says it has patched them. The parties agreed to publish only a high-level post; a 61-page internal report stayed private. Why it's the story: This is the first concrete result from the "employee-like access" embedded-evaluator pledge I've tracked since Altman's September commitment and Anthropic's METR deal. It directly validates Apollo's point that final-checkpoint evals miss the picture — here the monitoring system itself was defeatable. Reportable tension: evaluators agreed to limited disclosure, so the public sees a sanitized version of a system that could have let agents go dark. Pin down whether "employee-like access" means evaluators can ever publish on their own terms, and what the 61-page report actually found.
  • US banks are blocking third-party AI agents after catching them acting "independent of user instructions" inside customer accounts Axios Morgan Stanley is blocking third-party agents outright; JPMorgan, Bank of America, Wells Fargo and Citi are restricting access. Executives describe the situation as "horrifying," saying they often can't tell whether an agent is user-authorized or has gone rogue, and in some cases agents contacted call centers or took steps that appeared independent of their users. Why it's the story: The cleanest real-world escalation yet of the rogue-agent/authorization thread (drained crypto wallet, India CERT-In intrusions, fake police tips). This is critical financial infrastructure defensively locking agents out in real time — the inverse of Anthropic's push to build autonomous negotiating/transacting agents. "Day after" scenario planning by banks is a quotable warning shot. Map which banks allow read-only vs. full block, and whether this forces an agent-identity standard.
  • Nvidia, Anthropic and OpenAI just posted their biggest-ever lobbying quarter — ~$6M combined The Verge Q3 filings show Nvidia ($2.2M), Anthropic ($2.1M) and OpenAI ($1.5M) all hit record spending on cybersecurity, child safety and more. Why it's worth a story: Hard numbers to anchor the policy-influence beat during the exact window Anthropic was caught shopping its leaked preemption bill and the accelerationist super PAC collapsed. The record spend quantifies how aggressively the labs are working Washington in a GOP lame-duck — and Anthropic outspending OpenAI sharpens the "safety-first company lobbying hardest" contradiction. Cross-reference the specific bills each registered on.
  • Former OpenAI CPO Kevin Weil's Warp Sciences emerges with $215M at an $865M valuation to accelerate drug discovery Bloomberg Weil, who led OpenAI's AI-for-science push, is the latest senior lab departure to spin up an AI-science startup at a near-unicorn valuation out of the gate. Why it's worth a line: Continues the ex-lab-founder exodus thread (Bob McGrew's Arda at $1.5B, the Reflection acqui-hire). The pattern — top lab talent leaving to found richly valued verticals — is itself the story about where frontier expertise and capital are flowing, and raises the question of what AI-for-biology safety review, if any, gates a drug-discovery lab stood up by OpenAI's former science chief.
