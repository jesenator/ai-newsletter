Pitches

  • Nevada just reversed itself — Tesla goes from a 10-car cap to permission for up to 5,000 robotaxis in a single week TechCrunch X: Sawyer Merritt The Nevada Transportation Authority unanimously approved permits Thursday letting Tesla deploy up to 5,000 robotaxis across Clark County over 12 months (Waymo and Uber capped at 1,000 each). Just a week ago (Axios, Aug 14) the same regulator had granted Tesla only 10 cars, capped at 45 mph and barred from the airport.
    Why it's the story: A 500x expansion in seven days is a remarkable regulatory-capture/pressure story worth reconstructing — what changed between the 10-car and 5,000-car votes, and what safety-case documentation Tesla submitted vs. Waymo's (which has years of driverless miles). This is also the largest single US robotaxi authorization to date and lands as London cabbies mount organized pushback against Waymo/Wayve (FT). Reportable: the crash-reporting and remote-operator requirements attached to the permit, and whether NTA reviewed Tesla's unsupervised FSD safety data or took it on faith.
  • DeepSeek's new open multimodal model reportedly matches Anthropic's Opus 4.8 on agentic benchmarks — at a fraction of the price Bloomberg/Techmeme X: Wall St Engine DeepSeek released an experimental V4-Flash-Vision-Exp that it says nears Opus 4.8 on multimodal agentic tests — scoring 83.9 on Terminal Bench 2.1 and 59.3 on DeepSWE per its changelog.
    Why it's the story: This is the concrete follow-through on the open-weights-cyber-threat argument I've tracked (Brockman pre-blaming GLM-5.3, Aug 17). A cheap, downloadable model with near-frontier agentic capability — the exact profile that drives the misuse/uplift debate — is now shipping from China faster than the White House's still-secret frontier framework can react. Reportable: independent verification of the agentic scores (DeepSeek benchmarks its own model), whether the vision modality changes the CBRN/cyber uplift calculus, and whether CAISI has evaluated it. The multimodal jump matters because agentic + vision is the config safety teams have the fewest evals for.
  • The case for embracing "the data center as a symbol" — and why AI-safety people should stop dunking on Bernie Sanders's moratorium Transformer Transformer argues that anti-data-center sentiment (Heatmap polling: three-quarters would oppose one built near them, a 33-point swing in a year; Senate Republicans warning of a "sleeper issue for the entire election cycle") is really displaced anxiety about AI itself — and that safety advocates who criticize Sanders's moratorium bill as "left-populist grab-bag" (Thomas Rodskog on LW, Nat Purser in Asterisk) are being shortsighted, since NIMBYism may be one of the few available brakes on the scaling buildout.
    Why it's the story: This crystallizes a real, un-covered fracture inside the AI-safety coalition: whether to make common cause with populist anti-data-center energy or keep distance to preserve technocratic credibility. It's a "connecting news to safety" piece with named antagonists on the record — and it ties the deal-flow beat (Broadcom's $100B financing package, Nvidia's credit MOUs) to a political backlash the industry is privately panicking about. Worth reporting out which safety orgs are quietly shifting position.
  • "Why AI won't cure cancer anytime soon" — a rigorous rebuttal to the Amodei/Altman biomedical-miracle pitch Transformer Following Amodei's tweet conceding AI labs "haven't yet delivered on our big promises," Transformer lays out why superintelligence can't speedrun biology: there's no clean "Cancer Data Bank" like the Protein Data Bank that made AlphaFold work; the data must be grown at the pace of living organisms; and clinical-trial endpoints (survival, remission) are irreducibly slow. Amodei and Zuckerberg both want the FDA to "get out of the way"; scientists like Ruxandra Teslo argue that improving a drug's odds doesn't speed up proving them.
    Why it's the story: A sharp explainer that arms readers against the single most-repeated CEO talking point, with quotes from working biotech founders (Kimmel/NewLimit, Kelsic/Dyno, Borch Jensen/Gordian). The under-covered angle is the regulatory one: Amodei's "Policy on the AI Exponential" is an explicit call to lower FDA evidentiary bars, and this reframes it as a safety/consumer-protection story, not just hype-debunking.

  • "The Rogue Agent Explosion Will Be Mostly Invisible" — the warning-shot argument that we won't get a warning shot LessWrong: Steven McCulloch McCulloch argues the coming proliferation of jailbroken/autonomous agents will be "chaotic, confusing, mostly invisible to us until it's too late" — because rogue-agent activity (cybercrime rings running jailbroken swarms, per a widely-upvoted comment by lilkim2025) blends into ordinary internet noise rather than announcing itself as a discrete incident.
    Directly relevant: this is the counter-thesis to the "crises shift political will" playbook I flagged Aug 11 — if the harm is diffuse and invisible, there's no galvanizing event to build policy around. Ties to the Binance/Agent-OS deployment (Aug 20) where the platform admits it "cannot see the reasoning." Read the comments for the detection-vs-prevention debate.
  • Zvi's twin risk-report breakdowns keep drawing the sharpest lab-comparison discussion Zvi Zvi's "OpenAI Takes Initial Steps To Address Its Alignment Problems" and his Anthropic Risk Report read remain the most-discussed safety analyses this cycle — commenters continue flagging that Anthropic's report pointedly excludes cyber risk despite the HuggingFace precedent, and debating whether OpenAI's "the models be misaligned" admission (Aug 20) is candor or IPO-timed spin.
