Pitches

  • Anthropic's own federal affairs chief now admits its leaked bill's preemption language is "not precise enough" — but the structural flaws remain Transformer Zvi Transformer's dissection adds teeth to the leaked-bill story: evaluators can self-certify with no standards or licensing; Commerce must petition a federal court to block a dangerous model, with the court weighing harm to the developer but not the harm of not blocking; and the bill bars state courts from suspending models on catastrophic-risk grounds — arguably making it harder than current law to halt a dangerous release. New hook: head of federal affairs Anthony Cimino conceded the preemption text would be revised after the backlash.
    Why it's the story: this is the meaningful update to the Oct 17 leak — a named Anthropic exec walking back language only under public fire, which is itself the reportable contradiction for the "safety-first" company. Worth pinning Cimino down on whether the court-deference and self-certifying-auditor provisions also change, or just the PR-sensitive preemption clause.
  • Altman confirms OpenAI will build a humanoid robot, targeting a 2027 demo — putting it head-to-head with 1X, a company it still partly owns TechTimes AI Weekly Altman said OpenAI will "definitely do a humanoid"; reports say OpenAI even explored acquiring 1X (whose NEO home robot it led a 2023 round in) outright before deciding to build in-house.
    Why it's the story: the frontier lab with the most capital and the strongest consumer brand entering embodied AI directly — against its own portfolio company — is the cleanest signal yet that the $48B-YTD physical-AI land grab is now a lab-vs-lab race. Reportable: what happens to OpenAI's 1X stake and data relationship, and whether a lab deploying autonomous hardware triggers any of its Preparedness Framework thresholds.
  • DeepSeek is now co-designing Huawei's AI chips — and plans to pre-train on the Ascend 950DT by year's end The Information Investing.com Two sources say DeepSeek is shaping future Ascend designs to let it fully train an advanced model on Chinese silicon, starting as soon as end-2026 — a reversal from September reporting that it wouldn't use the 950DT for training. Lands the same day DHS ordered all federal civilian agencies to rip out DeepSeek tech and barred future procurement.
    Why it's the story: the clearest evidence yet that export controls are forging a fully independent Chinese training stack — the lab US officials call the open-weights threat is now a chip co-designer. Pairs with the DHS ban for a sharp policy piece: Washington is walling DeepSeek out of government just as it decouples from Nvidia entirely.
  • Dream Labs exits stealth with $90M and the "first public API for a robot foundation model" BusinessWire Dream Labs B Capital-led seed; Dream-1 claims zero-shot transfer to new robots with 20+ hardware partners, letting machines from different makers run tasks without machine-specific programming.
    Why it's worth a story: a publicly accessible robot-brain API is the proliferation analogue to open-weight LLMs — anyone can now point a general-purpose policy at physical hardware. The safety/governance question (what red-teaming gates a model that drives arbitrary robots?) is unasked, and the "foundation model for any body" pitch is the embodied-AI trend to interrogate.
  • Tenstorrent more than doubles to a $6.2B valuation on a $1.5B Series E — after takeover interest DCD Bloomberg Jim Keller's RISC-V-based Nvidia challenger jumps from ~$2.6B (Dec 2024); earlier 2026 acquisition talk (denied re: Qualcomm) preceded the round.
    Why it's worth a line for the deals beat: the non-Nvidia silicon tier is suddenly getting funded at scale — Tenstorrent alongside Amazon's new TorchNeuron (one-click PyTorch→Trainium) and Nvidia itself winning Modal's CPU business. A map-able "escape-from-CUDA" thread where the capital is now flowing.
