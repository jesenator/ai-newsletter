Pitches

  • Trump formally stands up the "Super Intelligence Force" — chaired by his intel chief, with an explicit anti-regulation mandate TechCrunch WSJ DNI Jay Clayton becomes AI/SI czar chairing the SIF, with FTC's Andrew Ferguson, Undersecretary of War Emil Michael, and OPM's Scott Kupor as vice chairs; a risks-and-opportunities report is due in 120 days. The charter reportedly pledges to "develop plans for responding to SI-enabled threats… while preventing overregulation and regulatory capture that would stifle innovation," and Clayton says the biggest risk is "not being first."
    Why it's the story: The institutional successor to the "morally binding" accord and the FTC probe — the US government's actual AI-governance architecture is now taking shape, and it's being run out of the intelligence community with a stated bias against regulation. Reportable: that Ferguson sits as vice chair of a body charged with "preventing overregulation" while his own FTC is mid-subpoena of the labs. Who staffs it, and does the 120-day report supersede CAISI/AISI work?
  • Google freezes its open-source bug-bounty program after being buried in AI-generated "slop" vulnerability reports Tom's Hardware As of Oct 1, Google stopped accepting product-vulnerability submissions to its OSS Vulnerability Reward Program, with maintainers reportedly overwhelmed by thousands of invalid AI-driven reports; a fix isn't promised until Q1 2027.
    Why it's the story: An early, concrete case of agentic AI degrading real security infrastructure — the inverse of the "AI finds bugs" optimism (cf. Gemini 4 Argon / Wiz). A checkable warning shot about AI flooding open-source maintainers and the brittleness of volunteer security plumbing; pairs with the broader "AI slop breaks institutions" thread.
  • OpenAI used its own internal models to help design its "Jalapeño" inference chip More Than Moore In a Q&A, OpenAI hardware VP Richard Ho details the Broadcom-codesigned inference chip — and confirms OpenAI leaned on its own internal models to help design the silicon.
    Why it's worth a story: A tangible data point for the recursive-self-improvement/internal-automation thread Kokotajlo and Pachocki keep flagging — a frontier lab closing the loop by using its models to build the compute that trains the next models. Vertical-integration + RSI angle, concrete and on the record.
