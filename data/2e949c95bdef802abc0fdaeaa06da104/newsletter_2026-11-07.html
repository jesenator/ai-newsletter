Pitches

  • OpenAI's AI disproved the Exponential Time Hypothesis — and the field's own co-proposer calls it "shocking" Techmeme NYT (Cade Metz) Russell Impagliazzo, who co-proposed the 27-year-old Exponential Time Hypothesis, calls OpenAI's AI-generated disproof "shocking news." The ETH underpins much of complexity theory; the NYT frames a future where computers could eventually crack encryption that guards the world's secrets or accelerate chip and drug design. Why it's the story: this is the second verified frontier-math result in a week (after the PSI/Anthropic Aaronson–Ambainis proof) where an AR system settles a central open problem — a checkable artifact of AI R&D acceleration far beyond benchmark-gaming. The security angle is the hook mainstream coverage is under-playing: if ETH is genuinely false, cryptographers must revisit which hardness assumptions are load-bearing. Reportable core: is the proof holding up under peer scrutiny, and what exactly did the model do vs. its human handlers?
  • NYT: dozens of Commerce-approved H200 handlers in China have empty offices and ties to sanctioned firms Techmeme / NYT A new investigation finds the Commerce Department approved dozens of Chinese companies to handle Nvidia H200 chips — some with empty offices and links to firms already under US national-security sanctions. Why it's the story: this is the export-control enforcement failure made concrete, landing the same week ~600K H200s sit stranded in Hong Kong and a new Democratic Congress eyes tougher Nvidia oversight. It exposes how thin the vetting is behind the "licensed sales" compromise, and gives the chip-decoupling beat a documented accountability case. Pair with the FT's Shenzhen smuggling-market sweep and the SK Hynix license lobbying, both moving today.
  • Axon puts $1.2B into Skydio at a $5.2B valuation — after the two rejected an outright acquisition Resilience Media Yahoo Finance Axon takes a ~20% minority, non-voting stake in autonomous-drone maker Skydio — whose systems are used by US public-safety agencies, the military and 32 allied nations — plus exclusive distribution rights to law-enforcement customers. Skydio stays independent. Why it's worth a story: it binds the dominant US police-tech vendor (Taser, body cams, evidence software) to the leading American autonomous-drone maker, consolidating an AI-powered surveillance stack aimed squarely at domestic law enforcement — with real civil-liberties and procurement-lock-in questions and no visible autonomy-safety regime. Fits the defense/physical-AI capital surge (Shield AI's $1B Series H at $28.8B also closed this week).
  • FT: US officials believe Jensen Huang had a romantic relationship with an alleged Chinese intelligence asset Techmeme / FT US intelligence reportedly warned that the Nvidia CEO was involved with Alice Huang, whom they allege was recruited by China's MSS to gather information on the company. Three House GOP chairs (Foreign Affairs, China, Intelligence) have already requested a closed-door Huang briefing. Why it's worth a story (update to earlier NYT thread): the FT's independent sourcing escalates a national-security story into live congressional action, directly entangling the most important company in AI with the chip-decoupling and espionage debate. Reportable core: what Huang knew, what the MSS allegedly sought, and whether this reshapes the H200-to-China licensing fight.
  • OpenAI closes just $11B of its round at $1.25T as AI-investment sentiment turns cautious Reuters The Decoder OpenAI completed only the first ~$11B tranche of a round that had targeted far more, with investors "expressing concern about infrastructure spending levels" after the post-midterm selloff. The Decoder notes OpenAI is now even courting dentists and other retail-adjacent capital to fill the round. Why it's worth a line (update): a concrete new datapoint for the circular-financing/AI-debt thesis — the sector leader can't fill its round at target even as Anthropic's IPO valuation gets talked down toward ~$1T. The "shaky projects killed" framing (Deutsche Bank, via Bloomberg) suggests the capital crunch is now repricing the whole buildout.
  • Censys finds 800+ exposed copies of ARTEX, a Chinese AI-powered pentest tool tied to real attacks Techmeme / TechCrunch (Zack Whittaker) Researchers found hundreds of publicly accessible instances of ARTEX, an open-source, AI-driven penetration-testing tool that has been linked to several cyberattacks since July. Why it's worth a line: a tangible warning-shot for the "Hackening" thread — an open, AI-augmented offensive tool proliferating in the wild just as OpenAI and Google ship gated "Critical"-rated cyber models. The asymmetry is the story: labs stage-release offensive capability while a Chinese open-source equivalent sits unguarded on 800+ servers.
