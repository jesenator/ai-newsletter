Pitches

  • A central bank governor just made the AI-risk case in financial-stability terms: Bailey warns frontier AI could trigger a global downturn Sky News Bank of England Governor Andrew Bailey warned that frontier AI's "increasingly sophisticated autonomy and problem-solving abilities, as well as threat capabilities," could "materially alter the speed, scale and economics of cyber risk," and that many jurisdictions "do not yet have the protocols" to manage the release of advanced models. He separately flagged a possible "disorderly correction" from stretched AI valuations and cross-investment between AI firms and hyperscalers.
    Why it's the story: This is a rare instance of a top financial regulator fusing the loss-of-control/cyber-uplift argument with systemic market risk — the HuggingFace-swarm concerns (per METR/Cotra) reframed as a threat to financial stability. It gives the safety community an unlikely institutional ally and a policy hook: who regulates frontier-model release when the central bank says no one has the protocols? Reportable: whether the BoE's Financial Policy Committee record names specific labs or the circular-financing structures I've tracked all month.
  • Together AI routes around the US data-center backlash by renting Saudi compute from Humain NYT / Techmeme Together AI, which serves open-weight models, announced a deal to use Humain's Saudi Arabia compute, explicitly framed as a way to "bypass US backlash over data centers." Humain is the same PIF-backed vehicle that put $3B into xAI's Series E this year and just launched an "AI in a Box" with AMD.
    Why it's the story: The domestic NIMBY revolt (Trump's "backwards and poor" comments today; Abbott/Bernie braking projects) has a pressure-release valve — offshoring inference to Gulf states with cheap power and no permitting fights. That's a policy and safety story: US-origin open-weight serving increasingly runs on sovereign compute outside any US oversight or export-control frame. Ties the data-center-politics thread to the open-weights-governance fight. Reportable: what data/residency terms apply, and whether US customers' workloads now physically sit on Humain hardware.
  • Trump escalates the data-center fight: reject them and go "backwards and poor," because "China could not be happier" Politico / Techmeme The President championed the buildout despite growing bipartisan backlash ahead of November's midterms, casting the "anti Data Center movement" as a gift to Beijing.
    Why it matters: The White House is now nationalizing the data-center debate as a China-competition loyalty test — the same framing that underwrites export-control policy. It lands the day Together AI publicly offshores compute to the Gulf and the UK unveils a sovereign-AI procurement push, making "where does the compute physically live" a live 2026 political issue. Worth a piece on how the "backwards and poor" line collides with the local grid-strain and water data (Zvi's "The American People Really Hate Data Centers") animating the revolt.
  • UK launches a £100M Sovereign AI procurement scheme amid a Palantir backlash FT / Techmeme The scheme funnels money to domestic AI startups for public-services work, arriving "in the wake of growing opposition to government contracts with US software group Palantir."
    Why it's the story: A government explicitly building a homegrown alternative to a controversial US surveillance-adjacent vendor is the procurement-side counterpart to the Flock/Palantir civil-liberties thread I've tracked. It also tests the "sovereign AI" thesis in the one domain where accountability matters most — public services. Reportable: which startups qualify, and whether the scheme actually displaces Palantir's NHS/policing footprint or just supplements it.
  • ChatGPT Ads hits a $1B annualized run rate in under 200 days — OpenAI frames advertising as the way to "expand access to AI" OpenAI CNBC OpenAI says the ad platform, used by "tens of thousands" of advertisers, is on track for $2.6B by year-end.
    Why it's the story: An ads business inside a consumer product now used for search-replacement raises exactly the alignment/incentive question the safety community warned about — when the assistant is monetized by advertisers, whose interests does its "advice" serve? It's a clean explainer peg (how ad incentives interact with model outputs and ranking) and a business-model inflection worth pressing OpenAI on: disclosure, labeling, and whether ad demand shapes model behavior.
