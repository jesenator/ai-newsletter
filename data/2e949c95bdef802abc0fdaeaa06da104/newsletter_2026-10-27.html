Pitches

  • The buried Anthropic IPO story: founders can rewrite the Long-Term Benefit Trust's rules — without the trustees' consent Transformer A deep read of the S-1 finds the LTBT's safety veto is less durable than advertised. A single Class F share held by a "Founder LLC" carries 50.1% of the vote; a shareholder "failsafe" lets the founders (with simple majorities of public and employee votes cast, in the first three years) instruct the trust to vote away its own Class T rights — or redeem the trust's share outright, cancelling trust-elected directors without a class vote. Drafting closes the merger and board-expansion end-runs, but the trust-agreement-amendment path needs no board approval at all.
    Why it's the story: this is the single most important unexamined detail in the first frontier-lab IPO. Anthropic sells the LTBT as the structural guarantee of its safety mission; the mechanics show seven founders can reshape or neuter it if their interpretation of the mission diverges. "Who protects the mission from the founders?" is a clean, original governance piece no one else has nailed.
  • Google's Genie 4 world model is being wired into Gemini Robotics — training humanoids inside AI-generated reality Google DeepMind The Verge Genie 4 (powered by Gemini 3.8 Flash as an in-loop agent) generates real-time, photorealistic, physics-aware interactive worlds with native audio. DeepMind explicitly demos Gemini 4 Argon + Genie 4 driving Apptronik's Apollo humanoid through tasks inside the simulation, pitching "safe, infinitely variable" training environments for robots and agents.
    Why it's worth a story: this is the clearest sign the frontier labs see generated world models — not just real-world data — as the training substrate for embodied AI, directly rivaling Machenta's simulation-first bet (below). The under-covered angle: a model that can "defeat CoT monitoring when motivated" (per Zvi's Argon read) is now being used to generate the environments robots learn in. Who validates the sim's fidelity, and its safety guardrails?
  • Robotics funding is now a daily unicorn machine — Q3 raises up 185% YoY Crunchbase Forbes Tech in Asia Today alone: London's Machenta (Imperial spinout) took a $40M seed — the UK's largest ever — to teach robots a task from one demonstration via simulation; Travis Kalanick's Atoms raised $1.7B (a16z-led) for KitchenBASE automation; Generative Bionics raised €70M for a European humanoid facilities network; MoSense $15M for robot e-skin. Crunchbase pegs Q1–Q3 robotics funding up ~185% YoY, US and China leading.
    Why it's worth a trend piece: the "physical AI" land grab you've tracked is consolidating around two theses — simulation-trained generalist models (Machenta, Genie 4) vs. real-world deployment (Bedrock, Atoms) — while a Kalanick comeback and a 185% funding jump mark a genuine inflection. Still no safety-eval regime for autonomous hardware.
  • Oracle puts OpenAI's "Jalapeño" inference chip, Google TPUs and Cerebras on the same cloud — and bakes ChatGPT/Codex into its apps CNBC OpenAI Oracle's OCI will for the first time offer Google TPUs, OpenAI's in-house Jalapeño inference silicon, and Cerebras hardware; Cerebras stock jumped ~8%. Separately OpenAI's apps/agents get embedded in Oracle's enterprise stack.
    Why it's worth a line: the scrambled compute/alliance map keeps getting stranger — OpenAI's own chip renting space next to Google's TPUs on a third party's cloud. A clean datapoint on how the circular-financing web and custom-silicon race are fusing as the deals beat heats up.
