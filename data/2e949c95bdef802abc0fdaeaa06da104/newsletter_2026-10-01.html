  • Anthropic targets IPO roadshow the week of Nov 9 — and Broadcom will lend it up to $42B to finance a $125B TPU lease Bloomberg Reuters Formal marketing could begin the week of Nov 9, putting Anthropic on track to trade before Thanksgiving. The prospectus reveals Broadcom agreed to a convertible note up to $42B to help fund Anthropic's $125.2B, five-year TPU lease commitment. Why it's the story: The financing mechanics are the news — a chip supplier effectively lending its customer the money to buy its chips, folded into the same prospectus that devotes nearly a third of its pages to risk factors warning of "existential risks to humanity." A deal/governance piece on circular AI financing and whether a sub-$5B-revenue lab racing to a >$2T listing can carry $125B in lease obligations.

  • Google ships Gemini 4 Argon — and is releasing it to trusted defenders without cyber guardrails Google DeepMind New frontier model rolling out via the "Fairwind Program," priced at $2/$10 (matching Sol/Argon rivals). Google says it's going through the US government's voluntary pre-release access process — but will give trusted defenders and internal teams a version "without cyber guardrails" for full offensive-grade capabilities. Wiz already used it to find a critical healthcare-software vuln prior models missed. Why it's the story: A frontier lab deliberately shipping an ungated cyber-capable model to a trusted-defender whitelist is a live test of the dual-use "defense" framing — who's on the list, how is access controlled, and does "US reviews first" (the AISI squeeze) actually gate this? Note the autonomous C→Rust migrations and 300TB+ datacenter memory savings as real internal-automation data points for the RSI thread.

Why it's worth a line: The first major ruling in the wave of publisher litigation over AI-generated search answers — an early precedent on whether antitrust law reaches AI content ingestion, with direct implications for every media company watching referral traffic collapse.
  • Newsom vetoes CA bill banning secret recording with smart glasses AP Governor vetoed legislation penalizing covert smart-glasses recording in private places, calling the definition of "wearable recording devices" too broad.
