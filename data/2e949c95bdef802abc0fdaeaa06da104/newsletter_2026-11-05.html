  • METR's high-access audit: Anthropic is now improving its models ~2x as fast — and credits internal AI use for most of it METR The Information Zvi After an Aug–Oct evaluation with "unprecedented access" to Anthropic's internal R&D metrics, METR concludes capabilities on well-specified tasks are growing at ~1.9x the pre-Oct-2025 rate, "a majority" attributable to Anthropic using AI to build AI, and "no observed bottlenecks" on further acceleration. Zvi flags that Anthropic's amended S-1 now says it "may imminently meet" its RSP's definition of "dramatically accelerated progress attributable to AI." Why it's the story: this is the clearest independent evidence yet of recursive self-improvement at a frontier lab — the exact loss-of-control precursor METR says the public needs to track. The buried bombshell is governance: Anthropic redacted "a majority of the evidence METR found most informative," so the public must trust METR's judgment on the field's highest-stakes question. Reportable core: what triggers the RSP clause, and what happens when it does.

It has already surfaced 81+ "novel" vulnerabilities in Firefox. Why it's a story: a direct escalation of last week's government-gated "Daybreak Red" cyber thread (GPT-5.6 Cyber in GovCloud) — now a full GPT-6-class model whose own vendor rates its capability "Critical." Why it's worth a story: the H200 stockpile is a vivid inversion of the export-control narrative — Beijing, not Washington, is now the chokepoint, reshaping the decoupling debate. And Nvidia scavenging failing telecom real estate is a tangible sign the physical buildout is hitting power/permitting walls, right as the post-midterm "blue wave" threatens data-center moratoria (per earlier coverage).

LessWrong & Safety Discourse

  • A direct attack on a load-bearing doom premise: "Instrumental convergence is incoherent" LessWrong EA Forum A detailed cross-posted critique argues the instrumental-convergence thesis (Bostrom/Omohundro/Carlsmith) is too poorly defined to make real-world predictions — none of the seminal papers specify how to identify ex-ante which systems "have goals," and the famous "you can't fetch the coffee if you're dead" intuition equivocates across goal-architectures that imply the opposite. The author contends overindexing on IC-derived takeover scenarios may be reducing EA's impact and even fueling "warlike and nationalistic" AI-race sentiment. Worth a read: a rare rigorous, from-inside challenge to the conceptual foundation of x-risk arguments, explicitly invoking the Hugging Face attack as a case of IC being applied loosely. A clean explainer hook on why "the AI will seek power by default" is more contested among safety researchers than public debate suggests — watch the comments for pushback from IC defenders.
