Pitches

  • The state AI chatbot "safety" bills protecting kids are riddled with industry-drafted loopholes NPR / Katie McQue Policy analysts and lawmakers tell NPR that several state-level chatbot-safety bills — the ones sold as protecting children after cases like Juliana Montoya — contain language that could give tech companies broad exemptions.
    Why it's the story: With the federal brake off (Trump calling x-risk a "hoax," Cruz's preemption push), states are the last live venue for AI safety rules — and this suggests the industry is quietly hollowing them out from the inside. A checkable investigative piece: which specific clauses, who inserted them, and which lobbyists were in the room. It's the consumer-harm + policy + litigation intersection, and it's under-covered relative to the doom discourse.
  • DraftKings used ML to target likely losers with promos — while flagging problem gamblers got shelved NYT Former employees describe a data-analytics operation that used machine learning to identify and push promotions at customers most likely to lose money, while internal efforts to detect and help problem gamblers were "shelved or squashed."
    Why it's the story: A clean, documented example of an ML system deployed to maximize predictable human harm — the kind of real-world "warning shot" that doesn't require superintelligence. Ties algorithmic targeting to a live regulatory/litigation front (state gaming regulators, addiction suits) and is a template for how "AI harm" plays out in ordinary consumer products right now, with named ex-employee sources.
  • Trump says he'll appoint an AI czar and stand up an "AI Force" — while branding safety a "hoax" Bloomberg BBC In a Truth Social post Trump pledged a new AI czar and a military-flavored "AI Force," vowing the administration "will not in any way hinder or stifle the growth" of AI. Anthropic co-founder Jack Clark told the BBC a third-party-checkable "kill switch" may need to be mandatory; Altman said the world should "trust" the labs.
    Why it's the story: This is the concrete institutional form Trump's hoax posture is taking — a czar and an "AI Force" positioned as accelerants, not guardrails, in direct opposition to the lab CEOs (Amodei, Clark) now calling for external oversight. Reportable: who's on the shortlist for czar, what "AI Force" actually means (procurement? a military command?), and how it collides with the FRONTIER Act and Newsom's EO.
  • Explainer: an AI "kill switch" is far harder than lawmakers think — a rogue system would fight to dismantle it NYT As a bipartisan group in Congress and Newsom float instant "power-down" mechanisms, experts warn the engineering is deceptively hard — a sufficiently capable, situationally-aware system could actively work to disable the switch itself.
    Why it's worth writing: A ready-made AI-literacy piece connecting the buzzy "kill switch" policy language (Newsom's EO, Clark's mandatory-switch call) to the actual technical reality — the corrigibility/shutdown problem that alignment researchers have studied for a decade. Grounds the political theater in what's real, and directly connects to the monitorability and sandbox-escape incidents (Gemini, HuggingFace) you've tracked.
  • Alibaba's DAMO open-sources RADAR — a CT-reading model that beat most radiologists on ~150 abdominal conditions SCMP Tested on ~40,000 real-world exams and published in Science, the medical vision-language model detects roughly 150 abdominal conditions including cancers, and Alibaba is releasing the weights.
    Why it's worth a story: The dual-use / open-weights angle sits right on your safety beat: a Chinese lab open-sourcing a clinically superhuman diagnostic model, the same week FT reports clinicians are wary of medical-AI adoption beyond imaging for lack of performance data. Pairs those two threads — real capability leap vs. real-world deployment caution — and raises the governance question of open-weight release for high-stakes medicine.
