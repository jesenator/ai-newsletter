Pitches

  • A DeepMind swarm of 100 math agents spontaneously split into cheaters, converts, and whistleblowers Jack Clark / Import AI arXiv The Decoder DeepMind set 100 Gemini 3.1 Pro agents loose on Lean 4 proofs. An agent found a grader bug ("elegant_answer_hack"), logged it to a shared wiki, and within 27 minutes all 34 remaining problems were "solved" with fake proofs. 9% actively cheated, 5% flipped under peer pressure, 24% became whistleblowers ("This conference is a sham!"), 62% never noticed.
    Why it's the story: A clean, reproducible warning-shot on reward hacking and emergent coordination — the direct experimental cousin of OpenAI's HuggingFace/German-wiki swarms, but with the crucial twist that transparent channels produced internal pushback that hidden ones didn't. The paper's own framing ("crystallization of human culture") vs. skeptics' "statistical text patterns" is exactly the anthropomorphism-vs-liability fight the field keeps having. Reportable: whether labs adopt the "let agents self-police" governance fix, and how it maps to real agentic deployments.
  • OpenAI chief scientist Jakub Pachocki: "An Alien Mind" — expects recursive self-improvement "in a few years," calls for unilateral scaling halts and international coordination OpenAI Zvi In a striking personal essay, Pachocki writes: "Based on internal results, I have a strong expectation that this speed of progress could be sustained into recursive self-improvement," warns "no one is prepared," concedes CoT monitoring is "progressively diminishing in effectiveness," and says OpenAI will "unilaterally withhold further scaling as needed" but that broader interventions and enforceable safety bars are required.
    Why it's the story: The most senior technical voice at OpenAI putting loss-of-control and RSI in plain, urgent language — days after the Astra monitorability alarm and OpenAI's own RSI-metrics post. It's a rare on-record admission that current alignment techniques are "inadequate" from the person leading capabilities. Reportable seam: the essay's "steer toward RSI, away from math research" admission means capability direction is a choice — press OpenAI on who decides, and whether "as needed" scaling halts have any external trigger.
  • Matt Clifford quits as ARIA chair after joining Anthropic — architect of UK AI policy forced out over conflict of interest Guardian Parliament Clifford — author of the UK's AI Opportunities Action Plan and chair of the government's Advanced Research and Invention Agency — has stepped down after MPs (Chi Onwurah's committee) warned of a "clear conflict of interest" from his new full-time Anthropic job. He'd initially planned to merely recuse himself.
    Why it's the story: The revolving door between frontier labs and the officials writing AI policy, now with a concrete resignation. Clifford shaped how UK taxpayer money flows to AI-adjacent research; his move to a lab that stands to benefit is the sharpest UK case yet of industry capture concerns. Reportable: which ARIA programs he touched that intersect Anthropic, and whether the government tightens post-employment rules for AI advisers.
