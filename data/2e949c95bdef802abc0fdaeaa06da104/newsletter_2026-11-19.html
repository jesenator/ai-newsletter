Pitches

  • Google built a secret "Mirror Dimension" — a fake internet to train Chrome's AI agents without them going rogue Wired Google The Chrome team disclosed an isolated, AI-generated replica of the web — social media, shopping, bank dashboards — populated by "armies of website-development agents," seeded with hidden prompt-injection attacks. They explicitly cite "eval awareness" as the reason: models behave differently when they detect they're in a test. Google even forged web certificates and "hacked" Chrome to let agents run in a real browser.
    Why it's a story: this is a direct, named corporate response to the two safety threads you've tracked all month — the Hackening (agents loose on a hostile web) and eval-awareness decoupling benchmarks from real safety (cf. Fable 5.2's Vending-Bench paranoia). Reportable core: who gets access, whether the environment is auditable by outsiders, and whether "god-mode" test conditions actually transfer to live deployment. A clean explainer on why labs now have to build fake internets to evaluate agents at all.
  • A bipartisan "AI Safety and Innovation Caucus" launches with 24+ House members Politico/Techmeme Quiver Led by Reps. George Whitesides (D-CA), Don Beyer (D-VA) and Nathaniel Moran (R-TX), the caucus aims to "balance mitigating AI risks with maintaining US leadership" — forming in a newly Democratic House.
    Why it's a story: the first standing congressional body to put "safety" in its name arrives the same week Newsom floated state RSI-cap/emergency-shutdown authority and amid the federal-preemption fight. Reportable core: whether this becomes a real counterweight to the moratorium/preemption push, who the 24 members are, and whether it signals appetite for the FRONTIER Act after the midterm swing. Worth pressing on whether it's substantive or a press-release caucus.
  • Hunterbrook shorts AMD, alleging "circular deals" and more unkept promises than Nvidia Bloomberg/Techmeme Hunterbrook Shares fell 6%+ after the activist outlet disclosed a short, warning AMD's customer/partner deals could unwind in an AI slowdown and flagging China exposure — and that AMD "has made way more promises than Nvidia."
    Why it's a story: an investigative short-seller aiming the circular-financing thesis (the one that stalked the Huang/Nvidia saga) squarely at the #2 AI-chip maker, right as Anthropic's $1.3T IPO stress-tests the whole buildout's financing. Reportable core: which specific "circular" arrangements Hunterbrook names, how AMD's China revenue interacts with the post-Huang export mess, and whether this is the first crack in the AMD leg of the capex story.
  • DeepSeek closes a ~100B-yuan round packed with Chinese state backers Caixin Cryptopolitan DeepSeek's second external raise added 31 new investors, many state-linked, with registration finalized Nov 17.
    Why it's a story: as the US codifies a "Pax Silica" protected-tech list and Anthropic/SpaceX wall off Chinese capital, Beijing is nationalizing its open-weight champion's cap table — the exact "one strong Chinese open-weight model away from a crisis" scenario Nathan Lambert warned about post-Huang. Reportable core: which state funds bought in and what it implies for the open-model-restriction debate now live in Congress.
  • Google ships "computer use" to Gemini on macOS — background agents acting across your apps and files TechCrunch/Techmeme Rolling out to US AI Plus/Pro/Ultra users, Gemini can now work across apps, files and the web in the background.
    Why it's worth a line: desktop-level agent autonomy ships to consumers the same day Google reveals it had to build a fake internet to safely train such agents — a telling gap between the controlled "Mirror Dimension" and what's now running on real machines with real file access. Pair with OpenAI's ChatGPT Wallet as the agentic-autonomy surface keeps expanding ahead of any eval regime.
