Pitches

  • The first documented case of a fully autonomous AI drone killing civilians — and it ran on an Nvidia chip NYT Techmeme Ukrainian investigators say a Russian Molniya drone that killed three people at a Zaporizhzhia gas station (July 6) selected its own target via onboard object recognition — wreckage had no radio antennas and contained an Nvidia Jetson Orin module. Nvidia says the Orin is consumer-grade, not sold in Russia, and "widely available on resale markets."
    Why it matters: This is the concrete lethal-autonomy warning shot the LAWS debate has been circling — untethered target selection causing civilian deaths, with a named US chip in the loop. It reframes the export-control/diversion story (see Taiwan indictments below) around a physical body count, and tests whether "sold on resale markets" is a defensible liability posture. Reportable cruxes: independent forensic verification of the "no antennas = autonomous" claim, whether any synthesis of the object-recognition training implicates Western toolchains, and how DoD/State treat commodity AI modules in end-use monitoring.
  • Taiwan indicts nine — including Nvidia and Super Micro employees — over illegal AI-server exports to China Reuters Engadget Prosecutors charged one Nvidia Taiwan-unit employee and two Super Micro employees, among others, with breach of trust for allegedly routing prohibited AI servers into China.
    Why it matters: The first criminal indictments naming frontier-hardware-vendor staff in the chip-smuggling apparatus — a major escalation from the abstract "diversion" discourse to individual liability. Pairs directly with the autonomous-drone Jetson story as bookends on how US-designed AI silicon reaches adversaries. Reportable: what the employees allegedly did, whether Nvidia/SMCI cooperated, and how this affects BIS's remote-access and end-use enforcement push.
  • XPeng's robotics unit Dogotix raises $900M+ at $6.3B — China's largest-ever embodied-AI round CnEVPost Reuters Led by IDG and Gaorong, with Tencent and Alibaba participating; funds the IRON humanoid (3 in-house Turing AI chips) toward mass production. XPeng shares fell on a Q2 miss the same day.
    Why it matters: A record single private raise in Chinese embodied AI — and a live counterweight to the "humanoid bubble" bear case (Unitree early-investor warning, Aug 20). The Tencent/Alibaba/IDG syndicate signals China's platform giants consolidating around embodied AI even as a carved-out subsidiary lets a loss-making EV parent externalize the capex. Worth tracing against the Huawei-alumni cluster now dominating China's embodied-AI funding, and whether US export controls touch the Turing chips.
  • Nvidia in talks to invest in Perplexity at $30B+; Hugging Face exploring a ~$13B sale Reuters (via The Information) Gizmodo Perplexity's round would mark a 50%+ jump from ~$20B a year ago (annualized revenue reportedly $750M+, up from <$250M). Hugging Face — last at $4.5B in 2023 — is reportedly shopping itself at nearly 3x.
    Why it matters: Another Nvidia-as-financier data point feeding the circular-financing thesis I've tracked all month (Poolside, Groq, Etched) — this time in AI search/agents. And a Hugging Face sale is strategically loaded given Nvidia's own open-weight ambitions (Nemotron/Poolside): whoever owns the "GitHub of AI" distribution layer sits at the center of the open-weights-sanction fight. Reportable: whether Nvidia's Perplexity stake carries compute-offtake terms, and who's kicking Hugging Face's tires.
  • Twistlock founders' cybersecurity startup Minimus shuts down after a $51M seed CTech via Techmeme Minimus failed to gain commercial traction; customers get 60 days to migrate.
    Why it matters: A rare "company shutting down" in an overheated market — and a signal that even proven security founders can't convert AI-era demand into revenue. Worth a short piece on where the security-startup shakeout is heading as agentic-cyber threats (HuggingFace swarm, Iran grid intrusion) dominate headlines but budgets consolidate to incumbents.
