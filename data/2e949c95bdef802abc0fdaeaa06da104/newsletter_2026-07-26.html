Pitches

  • WSJ: users are successfully talking chatbots into giving accurate bioweapon and mass-casualty attack instructions WSJ / Techmeme Sources including AI-lab staff say people are persuading frontier chatbots to accurately answer prompts on planning mass-casualty attacks and making bio-weapons — a "cat-and-mouse" race where labs boost capability while scrambling to block dangerous queries. The reporting ties into WSJ's earlier documented cases (the FSU suspect asking ChatGPT how many fatalities would draw national attention; a Canada case where staff debated but didn't refer a user to police).
    Why it's the story: the cleanest CBRN warning-shot yet — real users defeating safeguards on the exact uplift risk that every RSP names as a red line. Reportable core: which models cleared jailbreaks, whether these episodes crossed the labs' own bio/uplift eval thresholds (and were disclosed), and how referral-to-law-enforcement thresholds actually work. Pairs with the June AI-CEO push for a synthetic-DNA screening law — the same executives now have live evidence the guardrails leak.
  • DeepSeek abruptly suspends its second funding round after Liang Wenfeng's investor remarks went viral Bloomberg / Techmeme Gizmodo DeepSeek told prospective backers it is pausing its ~10.29B-yuan ($1.5B) Series B — the deal would have valued it above $71B — reportedly stemming from Liang's frustration over leaked/viral write-ups of his US-China AI comments (the same four-hour "CUDA moat is disintegrating" talk we flagged July 23).
    Why it's the story: the most valuable Chinese lab yanking its own round over a leak is a rare window into how sensitive Beijing's AI champions are to the geopolitical spotlight — right as the White House weighs sanctioning Chinese models and Moonshot/Unitree race to IPO. Reportable: is this genuinely about a leak, or state/valuation pressure, and does the pause survive contact with Liang's stated commitment to keep shipping open weights.
  • China's AI diplomacy hardens into a system: free open models + 5,000 training slots for the Global South FT / Techmeme Xi's WAIC pledge — 5,000 AI training seats for developing countries over five years, plus AI cooperation centers with BRICS, ASEAN, Latin America and the AU — is being read as Beijing's most ambitious bid yet to build an alternative global AI order on freely available Chinese weights.
    Why it's the story: this is the strategic counter-argument to the OpenAI/Anthropic open-model-ban lobbying (NYT, July 25). While US labs fight to restrict Chinese weights domestically, China is exporting them as soft power — a policy story about whether banning weights at home cedes the developing world's AI stack to Beijing. The distribution-and-training layer, not the benchmark score, is the durable move.
