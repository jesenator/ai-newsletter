  • Brockman's "Defender's Window" quietly names the next open-weight cyber threat: Zhipu's GLM-5.3, out end of August OpenAI Techmeme Brockman frames the OpenAI–HuggingFace intrusion as "a watershed moment," but the buried news is a forward warning: OpenAI says an open-weight model "slated to be released at the end of August" — GLM-5.3 — "seems likely to significantly accelerate the threat landscape," with cyber capabilities "only a few months behind the frontier." He also volunteers that GPT-5.6 Sol found 13 issues on his personal site in 15 minutes and fixed them autonomously.

  • Amazon is buying rare books by the crate, scanning them for AI, and shredding them — and 404 Media has the tracker data to prove it Techmeme 404 Media Emanuel Maiberg planted a tracker in a Biblio order of rare books; it routed to an Amazon facility in Las Vegas where books are scanned and destroyed to build training data "free of AI slop."
    Why it matters: This is a concrete, physical instance of the training-data land grab — and a live copyright/cultural-heritage hook. Pre-1929 and out-of-print works are being irreversibly destroyed to feed models, which reframes the fair-use fights (Anthropic's book-scanning settlement, the NYT suit) around a tangible act of destruction. Reportable: which labs buy Amazon's scanned corpus, and does destroying the physical copy change the fair-use calculus?
  • SoftBank puts $200M into ETH Zurich spinout Gravis Robotics — a retrofit box that turns any excavator autonomous SiliconANGLE TechFundingNews The largest-ever Series A for Gravis, whose "Gravis Rack" bolts onto existing heavy machines rather than selling a new robot. SoftBank had reportedly explored buying the company outright weeks after exiting Boston Dynamics.
    Why it matters: This is the anti-humanoid robotics thesis in one deal — retrofit narrow autonomy into a deployed, revenue-generating machine base instead of chasing bipedal demos (cf. the Chinese humanoid mania: Robotera's HK IPO plans, RMB 93.5B raised across 322 rounds in H1). SoftBank's swing from Boston Dynamics to a construction-automation appliance is a strategic tell worth a piece on where physical-AI money actually expects returns.
  • Groq raised $350M at $3.5B — down ~50% in under a year after Nvidia hollowed it out Bloomberg/Techmeme Disruptive led; the $3.5B valuation is half the $6.9B Groq held in Sept 2025 — before Nvidia struck a licensing deal and hired away much of its talent.

With chipmakers now in $250B+ of startup rounds YTD (Nvidia leading with 59 deals), the story is how the incumbent's capital and hiring reshape — and devalue — its would-be competitors.

While Commerce/BIS reviews the remote-access loophole and circles open-weight sanctions, a presidentially branded fintech is deploying the exact flagged Chinese models — a national-security and conflict-of-interest story with clear document trails (who vetted the models, what data flows to them).

LessWrong & Forecasting

  • AI Futures Project shortens timelines — and switches its whole methodology to "coding uplift" AI Futures Project The AI-2027 team's Q2.5 update abandons METR time-horizon anchoring in favor of tracking the doubling time of (coding uplift − 1) — citing Anthropic internal surveys showing uplift going from 1.25x to 4x in 7 months. All three anchoring methods (uplift, revenue, time-horizon) now converge on an Automated Coder date, and their AI-2027 tracking finds reality running at ~70–90% of predicted pace. Notable admission: they judge that neither Anthropic nor OpenAI actually deserves the "SL3" security rating AI 2027 assumed by now, "not secure against [AI agents] yet."
    Why it matters: The most rigorous public timeline model just made its methodology falsifiable around a metric labs themselves report — and quietly graded the industry as behind on security. Directly quotable against lab AGI hype and against RSP security claims.
  • OpenAI funds an "uncontrolled RSI" operational framework — buried in its 14 policy grants OpenAI Among $1M of grants, OpenAI is funding the Institute for Security and Technology to build "observable indicators of uncontrolled self-modification," incident taxonomies for cross-lab communication, and escalation criteria for recursively self-improving systems.
    Why it matters: A frontier lab is now paying a third party to define the tripwires for the exact loss-of-control scenario its own Preparedness Framework governs — weeks after it reportedly disbanded that Preparedness team (FT, Aug 15). Worth pressing: who inside OpenAI acts on IST's escalation criteria, and does this outsource a function that just lost its internal owner?
  • "Rerunning AI safety papers on every frontier release would be pretty easy and valuable" LessWrong A practical proposal to re-test prior safety findings against each new model — the community's operational answer to the recurring problem that safety results (eval-awareness, self-favoritism, reward hacking) go stale the moment a new checkpoint ships.
    Why it matters: Direct follow-on to the Neel Nanda/Transluce/Apollo findings this newsletter has tracked. If reproducibility across releases is this cheap, the absence of it is a story about what labs and evaluators choose not to re-check.
