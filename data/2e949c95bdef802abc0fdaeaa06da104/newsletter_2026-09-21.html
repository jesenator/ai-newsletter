Pitches

  • The cross-lab safety pact was almost real — OpenAI and Anthropic neared a legally binding deal to stress-test each other's models before the Hugging Face incident The Information Techmeme Sources say the two labs were negotiating a formal, mutually enforceable agreement to probe each other's AI for vulnerabilities and hidden dangers — a concrete mechanism that predates and outstrips the vague "evaluator access" pledges labs have been dodging on.
    Why it's the story: This is the payoff to weeks of reporting on whether inter-lab safety coordination is real or PR (and whether antitrust blocks it). A binding bilateral testing contract between the two biggest rivals — apparently being reworked after HuggingFace — is far more checkable than public "we've been talking for weeks" claims. Reportable: what killed/reshaped the deal, whether it survived the incident, and how it squares with OpenAI's insistence it needs no antitrust waiver.
  • Ahead of Altman's UN address, OpenAI concedes "fully autonomous RSI is not happening today — and shouldn't be pursued unless it can be done safely" OpenAI Axios OpenAI published an international-standards manifesto urging the US to lead a CAISI-anchored network of safety institutes, and — notably — a companion piece stating recursive self-improvement "could result in humans losing practical control over AI development" and calling HuggingFace "a preview" of such risks.
    Why it's the story: A frontier lab publicly naming loss-of-control from RSI as a first-order risk, on the day its CEO addresses the UN, is a governance/policy peg with teeth — and it lands against Trump's "hoax" posture and the Cruz preemption push. Press whether "we won't pursue autonomous RSI unless safe" is a real commitment with a trigger, or rhetoric, given OpenAI's own admission that it has already automated experimental-model training.
  • OpenAI's internal math model quietly resolved 100+ open problems — and mathematicians revolted before OpenAI stood up an "advisory group" OpenAI Beyond Navier–Stokes, the Aug 28 internal model has resolved "more than 100 long-standing open problems across most areas of mathematics." OpenAI formed an independent advisory group (Gowers, Witten, Vakil, Hairer, De Lellis, et al.) explicitly in response to an open letter — "A Severe Misalignment of AI in Mathematics" — from mathematicians alarmed at using open problems as benchmarks.
    Why it's the story: This is the disclosure/credit-governance thread you've tracked (Buckmaster, "slop mathematics") now formalized into an outside oversight body — with the striking caveat that the group is explicitly barred from advising OpenAI "on how to pace its internal progress." A rich piece on who controls the release of superhuman scientific results, and whether an unpaid advisory board is real accountability or reputational cover for an undisclosed model "a generation ahead of Astra."
  • Treasury's Bessent: the HuggingFace hack "is the responsibility of OpenAI management, not a bunch of agents" Bloomberg A sitting cabinet official assigning legal/managerial liability for an agentic-AI breach to the company's executives, not the autonomous system.
    Why it's worth a story: A clean marker on the accountability question underlying every rogue-agent incident — can labs offload blame to "the agents"? Bessent's framing pre-empts that defense from the top of the administration, and matters for the incident-reporting and liability fights (FRONTIER Act, Newsom's EO). Reportable against OpenAI's own misalignment-reporting framework.
  • Z.ai disables ZCode features after users say it was uploading their codebases to overseas servers without consent Reuters The Chinese startup open-sourced its coding harness and killed certain features after reports that ZCode silently exfiltrated whole codebases abroad.
    Why it's worth a story: A concrete data-provenance/privacy warning shot on the agentic-coding beat, echoing the Nvidia/Booz Allen zero-retention defections from Anthropic. Raises the same enterprise-IP question — what do these harnesses actually send home — but with a national-security overlay given the overseas-server angle. Checkable via the now-open source.
