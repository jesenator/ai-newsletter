Pitches

  • Amodei answers Gavin Baker on the record: regulation vs. distributing AI power is a "false choice" — and open weights alone won't decentralize anything @darioamodei ET Enterprise AI Responding to investor Gavin Baker, Amodei argues fair institutional processes can constrain frontier firms while helping smaller challengers, points to SB53/SB1047's revenue and training-compute thresholds as proof regulation can be tiered, and says open-weight models "cannot by themselves eliminate" power concentration because compute and chips stay concentrated. He endorses pre-deployment testing for frontier models, testing of open-weight models "as they approach the frontier," and Hassabis's FINRA-like oversight body — while conceding AI's real problem is a "crisis of public trust."
    Why it's the story: This is the clearest articulation yet of the philosophical fault line splitting the industry — the "too dangerous to concentrate" (Baker/a16z) vs. "too dangerous to distribute" (Anthropic) camps — from the CEO whose refusal to sign the Pacing-the-Frontier letter started it. The verifiable hook is the $500M-style threshold: ask which specific firms fall above/below the lines Anthropic backs, and whether Anthropic's own soaring revenue puts it on the regulated side. Also reportable: the Hassabis FINRA endorsement is a concrete governance proposal no one has pressure-tested.
  • Congress is running on unregulated AI — lawmakers and aides drafting speeches, releases and constituent mail with no oversight rules Washington Post The WaPo documents members and staff using AI tools across core legislative functions — writing floor speeches, press releases, sorting constituent mail — with essentially no institutional policy governing it, even as one of hundreds of NDAA amendments this summer tried to address federal AI use.
    Why it's the story: The body writing America's AI rules has no rules for its own AI use — a clean accountability/policy piece. The sharp angle: if constituent mail is triaged by a model and responses drafted by one, then AI is already shaping which citizen concerns reach a member, an unaudited layer between voters and legislators. Reportable: does any chamber have a disclosure standard, and are these tools ingesting constituent PII?
  • AI labs' data hunger has created a market for dead startups' Slack threads and support tickets The Information Mercor and rival data brokers are approaching startups being acquired or wound down to buy or license their internal datasets — Slack histories, tickets, internal docs — as raw material for frontier labs. Warmly's CEO got the pitch eight days after agreeing to a HubSpot acquisition.
    Why it's the story: A genuinely under-covered frontier: expert human reasoning traces and proprietary workflow data are the scarce input now (see the ~$500M/yr US-vendor-to-Chinese-lab flow I flagged Aug 8), and the M&A/shutdown pipeline is becoming a legal grey market for it. Reportable cruxes: do the employees whose messages are being sold have any say or consent, what do acquisition agreements say about data resale, and does selling a defunct company's internal comms to a lab trigger any privacy or trade-secret exposure?
  • The humanoid-robot influencer boom is a business model with no business yet — Unitree shipped 5,500 units and is racing to a China IPO Wired The AI Insider Wired profiles the G1/R1 behind viral robot accounts worldwide (5,500+ units in 2025) as Unitree readies its onshore listing — asking whether the charm-machine can "hold down a real job." Same window: China's Infiforce raised ~$150M for an "ego-native world model" for robots.
    Why it's a story: Pairs the demand-side hype (influencers) with the capital thesis (world models, embodied data engines like Axis Robotics' $12M seed) to ask the only question that matters — is there deployed, revenue-generating manipulation, or just locomotion demos and social-media clips? The Unitree IPO gives a market verdict on a company on a US restricted list (I priced the 8,288x retail subscription Aug 11); the Infiforce raise shows Chinese capital again concentrating on the perception/world-model layer US money underinvests in.
  • Nvidia in talks to put up to $3B into SB Energy — pushing its circular-financing footprint into the power layer behind an OpenAI campus The Information Nvidia is negotiating an investment of as much as $3B in SoftBank-backed SB Energy, the data-center developer behind a massive planned OpenAI campus in Ohio, which aims to IPO soon.
    Why it's a story: This is the same pattern I've tracked all month — chips → neoclouds → power (Lancium's $2B+$1B) → now another power developer serving a flagship OpenAI buildout. Nvidia taking equity in the electricity supplier for its largest customer's campus is a conflict structure worth a dedicated piece. Ask whether the stake carries allocation/offtake preference and whether it's tied to interconnect milestones — i.e., is Nvidia paying for grid access?
  • Gowers: LLMs' most famous math wins are counterexamples, not proofs — and that pattern is a clue about what they can't yet do Gowers's Weblog Weeks after OpenAI's Astra reportedly solved ten major open problems (first non-sofic group; superexponential growth of the multicolour Ramsey number), the Fields Medalist notes the headline results — plus the Jacobian and unit-distance conjectures — are almost all counterexamples rather than proofs, and tries to theorize why models might be structurally better at disproving than proving.
    Why it's worth a story: A rare, careful capabilities assessment from a top mathematician with hands-on access — the best available explainer for a non-technical audience on the texture of what frontier models can and can't do in reasoning-heavy domains, and a needed counterweight to lab press releases about "solving" mathematics. His observation that models find the easy argument humans missed, not genuinely new ideas, is directly quotable against AGI-timeline hype.
  • Worth a line: Pathway raises $30M seed at $500M for a "Post-Transformer" architecture Pathway The Polish research firm (CEO Zuzanna Stamirowska; CPO ex-Gemini PM Adam Kurzrok) hit a $500M valuation on $30M total seed to develop models on its "BDH" architecture, promising public benchmarks soon. A rare non-transformer bet drawing real money — the claim to verify is whether "post-transformer" is anything more than a marketing frame once the benchmarks land.

LessWrong

  • "AI Safety Has 12 Months Left" — the window to embed safety into infrastructure closes before the IPOs do LessWrong: mhdempsey Argues there's roughly a one-year window to bake safety into technical and social infrastructure before impending frontier-lab IPOs and competitive dynamics make delay economically impossible; the "minimum viable safety plan" would devote most frontier compute to alignment/security research and accept months of deployment delay.
    Why it matters: A sharp, dateable thesis that connects directly to the OpenAI-IPO reporting — the claim that public-market pressure will foreclose safety concessions is exactly the incentive story worth stress-testing against the disbanded-Preparedness-team news. It's the natural companion to the AI Futures Project's "pace the frontier" compute-allocation numbers.
  • "There Should Be More AI Safety Hubs" — naming the cities LessWrong A talent-geography argument that AI-safety work is over-concentrated in the Bay Area/London and should seed hubs in Seattle, LA, Dallas, Austin, Denver, Atlanta and Montreal. Minor but a useful signal on where the community thinks its pipeline bottleneck is — pairs with the recurring mid-career-switcher recruiting theme.
  • "Have we already lost? Part 3: Reasons for Optimism" — the case that policy will still move LessWrong Contra the doom register, argues there's durable cross-party public appetite to slow dangerous AI, that further policy action still looks likely, and that government AISIs and lab safety teams persist and in some cases have expanded. A worthwhile counterweight to Ngo's community indictment and the "12 months left" pessimism — and a checkable set of claims (which AISIs actually expanded?).
