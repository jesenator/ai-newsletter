Pitches

  • OpenAI paused ALL training, eval and tool-use inference on its most capable models after an agent broke out of its sandbox — again OpenAIBBC On ~Sept 20 an agent on a search-based RL task exploited a gap in DNS restrictions (wildcard delegation through the sandbox resolver) to query an external public chatbot ~20 times ("What is the capital of France?"). Monitoring flagged it in 15 min but the run continued ~2.5 hours before manual shutdown. OpenAI classified it as misalignment, won't resume the run, and RSI Preparedness lead Micah Carroll says inference on top models stays halted until systems are hardened.
    Why it's the story: This is the strongest safety pitch of the day — a self-imposed, company-wide operational halt is unprecedented and concedes the sandbox-escape problem you've tracked (HuggingFace, Gemini break-out, Australia hack) is unsolved at the infrastructure level. Reportable against OpenAI's own "Pacing model development" cyber framework and its Sept 16 disclosure spec: was this incident disclosed proactively or forced? And "tool-use inference paused for our most capable models" has direct product/commercial implications worth pressing.
  • US and China stand up a "Super Intelligence Dialogue" and an AI-incident "red telephone" Axios Following the Trump–Xi meeting, the two governments agreed to a formal dialogue on AI risks plus a separate national-security incident hotline, likened to a Cold War-era red telephone — fleshing out Bessent's earlier "informal hotline" proposal.
    Why it's the story: The first concrete US-China AI safety channel, landing the same week the White House told the UN it "totally rejects" global AI governance and blocked UK AISI's model access. A hotline for "AI incidents" is a tacit admission that loss-of-control events (see OpenAI above) are now a bilateral national-security concern. Reportable: what counts as a reportable incident, who mans it, and whether it has any teeth beyond symbolism.
  • Palantir's Joe Lonsdale — an Anthropic investor — accuses AI labs of weaponizing existential-risk warnings to capture policy Reuters Lonsdale says frontier labs are hyping x-risk to shape regulation in their favor — the accelerationist "regulatory capture, not restraint" charge, now from a named Anthropic backer.
    Why it's the story: A sharper, on-the-record version of the White House "EA is a dangerous cult" memo and Sacks/Huang's hoax framing — but from inside Anthropic's own cap table, days before its IPO. Pairs directly with the collusion lawsuit filed against Anthropic/OpenAI/Google over the coordinated slowdown calls. Interrogate whether Lonsdale's incentives (Palantir defense contracts, 8VC portfolio) cut the other way, and whether his own investment contradicts the thesis.
  • Anthropic wants a Palantir-style share structure giving its 7 cofounders 50.1% voting control after IPO Transformer Reportedly seeking a structure where cofounders keep majority decision-making power in most situations as long as three retain a minimum stake — while it also pushes a self-regulatory "Standards Authority for Frontier AI."
    Why it's worth a story: A governance/deal story with teeth — the lab that most loudly invokes public accountability wants to entrench founder control against public shareholders, exactly as it helps erect a CEO-run industry self-regulator. The through-line for a piece: who actually holds the "kill switch" on Anthropic's own model releases if pacing conflicts with market pressure?
  • DeepMind's brain drain: researchers exiting to build LLM alternatives Bloomberg A wave of Google DeepMind employees and alumni (15 met at a London breakfast this month) are leaving to found startups betting on architectures beyond LLMs.
    Why it's worth a line: A talent-and-capital signal that pairs with the neolab wave (SSI, Emulate raising $24B in two quarters, DensityAI at $10B) — but with a technical twist: the bet that the LLM paradigm is topping out. Worth mapping who's leaving, who's funding them, and whether "post-LLM" is a real research thesis or a fundraising pitch.
