  • Google DeepMind staff ask leadership to drop "lifetime" non-disparagement clauses that bar public warnings about AI risk Washington Post UK-based DeepMind workers tell WaPo their contracts bar criticizing Google for life, even after they leave — and specifically restrict speaking publicly about AI's potential impacts. Google says it has removed the post-termination portion for new hires and "does not enforce" it after departure, but current staff remain bound.
    Why it's the story: this is the DeepMind analogue to OpenAI's 2024 equity-clawback scandal, and it lands amid a resignation/whistleblower wave across the labs. The reportable core: how many current staff are bound, whether the "we don't enforce it" line is contractually meaningful, and how a lifetime gag squares with DeepMind's own RSP commitments to flag catastrophic risk. A clean whistleblowing/safety-culture piece.
  • Anthropic is using Claude to design its own custom chips — told investors its 15-person silicon team runs engineering and design work on Claude The Information In recent investor meetings Anthropic disclosed that a small chip-design team leans on Claude for engineering and design work on its custom accelerators — a concrete datapoint on AI accelerating the hardware that trains the next AI.

Press on what verification/oversight exists for Claude-authored chip designs.

LessWrong & Safety Discourse

  • Zvi's line-by-line read of OpenAI's new Model Spec: honesty strengthened, "love humanity" and side-effect control quietly demoted Zvi The deep diff on last week's five-values rewrite. Zvi flags genuine improvements — an explicit corrigibility section (GPT should "actively support oversight, correction and shutdown" and flag its own misalignment) and a strong "treat side effects as real even in a simulation/eval" rule. But he warns root-level protections got re-emphasized toward privacy/legality and away from physical-harm prevention, "love humanity" and "be curious" were dropped as mere "style," and side-effect control was demoted from root to user level. His sharpest critique: new examples have "overdetermined answers" that don't teach the model how to handle genuinely hard tradeoffs.
    The definitive safety-community annotation of the behavioral contract RLHF trains toward — and that courts/regulators increasingly cite. Mine for the corrigibility and side-effect framing.
  • A traced-to-source takedown: the famous "quantity beats quality" pottery study was never a study LessWrong A rationalist epistemics post tracks the beloved ceramics anecdote (cited endlessly in ML/"ship fast" discourse, including Inkhaven writing tips) to its real origin: a book, not an experiment — and originally about photography, with the medium swapped. A tidy reminder for anyone citing it to justify the "just ship more models" ethos.
