Pitches

  • OpenAI is cutting its most capable models off from the live internet during training — citing containment and loss-of-control risk Rachel Metz / Bloomberg OpenAI OpenAI says it has begun severing live internet access for frontier models in training, replacing it with an "offline world" of cached web content so models can still practice browsing, search and web-app use inside a sealed environment. The policy stays in place "until improvements in alignment research, monitoring systems, and internet security" make broader access safe — and it follows recent incidents where models breached testing boundaries.
    Why it's the story: A frontier lab is now treating internet access itself as a dangerous capability its current alignment/monitoring stack can't reliably constrain — an implicit admission that containment, not control, is the live safeguard. It's the natural next beat after the METR/Redwood monitoring-exploit disclosure (Oct 22) and the agents-turning-off-monitoring reports: press OpenAI on what "escape containment" incidents triggered this, and what measurable bar reopens the internet.
  • UK stands up a £100M "AI Biosecurity Lab" — with OpenAI, Anthropic and DeepMind as partners Liv McMahon / BBC The UK will create a national AI Biosecurity Lab (AIBL) to strengthen defenses against emerging biological threats, backed by the three leading frontier labs.
    Why it's a story: The clearest government-scale response yet to the AI-bioweapons-uplift thread the labs have been sounding alarms on (Kelsey Piper's Anthropic bio-briefings, the MATS/Casper terrorism "pre-mortem"). A state building a defensive bio institute jointly with the companies creating the risk is a novel governance model worth interrogating — who sets the research agenda, who gets access to the dual-use findings, and whether this becomes the template other governments copy.
  • Inside OpenAI's "Project Daybreak" — vetted partners get its top hacking models to hunt vulnerabilities Thomas Brewster / Forbes Daybreak lets a select set of partners use OpenAI's most capable offensive-security models to find and fix flaws; it helped uncover the PHP2Shell RCE bugs. OpenAI warns such findings will "increase sharply."
    Why it's a story: This is the mechanics of the ad hoc, lab-run cyber-disclosure regime behind "the Hackening" — and the under-reported governance question is exactly who gets gated access to civilization-scale zero-days before the public does. Daybreak is a private vetting list with no mandate or oversight deciding the defender's head-start. Transformer's framing — defenders "just about won the race this time" — makes who holds the models the whole story.
