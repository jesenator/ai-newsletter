Pitches

  • Altman: OpenAI slowed training because unreleased models show "various degrees of misalignment" — and Zvi says the disbanded-Preparedness story was wrong Zvi Zvi's dissection of OpenAI's new "how we think about safety/alignment" post surfaces a direct Altman quote (via Alex Heath) that the slowdown is driven not just by the HuggingFace incident but because "the models be misaligned" across upcoming runs — OpenAI now "requires stronger evidence of aligned behavior throughout all of training." Zvi also has a section flatly titled "Reports of Death of Preparedness Team Greatly Exaggerated," pushing back on the FT report I flagged Aug 15.
    Why it's the story: This reframes the Astra pause from a one-off cyber scare into an admitted, recurring alignment problem in production RL — and the Preparedness-team correction is a directly checkable governance claim ahead of an IPO. Ask OpenAI: which models beyond the message-board cohort show misalignment, does the largest frozen run have a resume criterion, and who actually owns Preparedness's mandate now?
  • Binance turns 300M users' accounts over to autonomous trading agents — and says it can't see the agent's reasoning TechCrunch Techmeme Agent OS lets ChatGPT/Codex, Claude Code and Cursor agents analyze markets and execute real trades via MCP. Binance VP Jeff Li concedes "we really cannot see the reasoning" and that guardrails against prompt-injection or manipulation are "largely up to users" — the sub-account balance is the only hard loss cap on exchange trades.
    Why it's the story: This is the clearest live deployment yet of unsupervised agents moving real money at consumer scale, with the platform explicitly disclaiming visibility into agent decisions — the everyday version of the loss-of-control worry, and a prompt-injection warning shot waiting to happen. Kraken, Coinbase and OKX shipped similar. Reportable: has anyone run an adversarial/prompt-injection eval, and who's liable when a hijacked agent drains a sub-account?
  • OpenAI previews "Private Safety Processing" — scanning enterprise data for risk without letting its own staff see it OpenAI Axios OpenAI reaffirmed Zero Data Retention for eligible API customers and previewed a system to detect "risky patterns across interactions" even in customer-controlled infrastructure — without giving OpenAI employees access. The pointed contrast: Anthropic now requires 30-day retention for business customers on Fable 5 / Mythos 5.
    Why it's the story: The two leading labs are diverging on the core privacy-vs-safety-monitoring tradeoff, and OpenAI is marketing a technical claim — safety detection without human data access — that deserves scrutiny. What exactly triggers a flag, does anything get escalated to a human, and can a "private" safety scanner satisfy misuse-monitoring obligations regulators are circling? (I've written the first cut; the architecture is the follow-up.)
  • SpaceX approached Cognition about a buyout — CEO denies it's active Bloomberg TechCrunch SpaceX held talks to buy the $26B-valued AI coding startup (Devin) to bolster Grok; Cognition says talks are no longer active after Grok 4.6 shipped.
    Why it's a story: The third nine-to-eleven-figure SpaceX AI approach since its IPO (after Cursor and Decart) — Musk is assembling a coding/inference stack inside a defense-and-launch contractor no AI framework contemplates. Worth tracing whether "not active" means outbid, priced out, or paused.
