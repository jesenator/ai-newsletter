Pitches

  • Meta shipped a 30B Apache-2.0 model explicitly built for "always-on" autonomous local agents — on the same day the NYT documented agents completing entire college courses without refusing Meta Superintelligence Labs NYT Bloomberg/Techmeme Muse Glimmer: 30B dense, Apache 2.0, "small enough" for one GPU, marketed for agent loops that "run permanently on local hardware" — already GGUF-quantized on Hugging Face and in Ollama within hours. Meta pairs it with a $1B community fund for data-center towns. Meanwhile the NYT reports students issuing "log in and complete my quiz" — agents that watch lectures, take tests and chat with classmates — and that major AI tools did not refuse the request. Why it's the story: The open-weight fight has been argued entirely over bio and cyber uplift. Meta just moved the frontier of *persistence*: a downloadable model whose stated product category is an unsupervised agent running 24/7 on a laptop, with no refusal layer anyone can audit and no eval obligation anywhere. Checkable asks: did Muse Glimmer go through Meta's Frontier AI Framework, was any agentic-misuse eval published, and what happens to the "we can revoke access" theory of agent governance when the agent is local. The NYT piece is the demand-side proof that the refusal layer already fails on the most banal misuse case in America — and it's a fraud/accreditation story, not just a cheating story. Ask OpenAI and Anthropic on the record why their agents complete proctored coursework.
  • Anthropic is now co-owner of an off-balance-sheet data-center platform — and has promised to personally pay consumers' electricity increases. Nobody has seen the contract. Businesswire Bloomberg OpenAI Theseus Infrastructure: Macquarie Asset Management ($498B AUM) and Singapore's GIC fund "the majority of the equity for each project" and lease to Anthropic as anchor tenant under long-term agreements — US first. Anthropic "will cover electricity price increases that consumers otherwise may face from these sites." Hours later, OpenAI sent Gov. Abbott its own responsible-infrastructure letter — one week after Abbott froze new data-center interconnections pending audit. Why it's the story: Two structural questions, both reportable today. (1) The ratepayer pledge is the first of its kind and is entirely unenforceable as written: who computes the counterfactual price increase, over what baseline, for how many years, and is it in the lease or a press release? Put it to a PUC. (2) The financing structure means the compute obligation sits on Anthropic's P&L while the asset and the debt sit with Macquarie/GIC — the same take-or-pay asymmetry that made Volta and Fluidstack systemic. Ask for the term, the escalators, and whether Anthropic holds residual liability if Theseus misses delivery. Bonus: sovereign-wealth ownership of frontier-lab compute is now a CFIUS-adjacent question nobody is asking.
  • Nvidia's bid collapsed and SpaceX walked in: Decart is being sold for $6–7B, three years old, ~100 people — with the ex-Unit 8200 commander who resigned over Oct. 7 now inside Calcalist (exclusive) Decart — real-time video generation, ~$450M raised, valued at ~$4B three months ago — is in advanced talks, signable "in the coming week." Nvidia (also an investor) was close before a higher bidder emerged; SpaceX, Amazon and Nebius are named. Sequoia's Shaun Maguire is the Musk conduit; Musk and CEO Dean Leitersdorf have been in regular contact since Oasis. Decart recently hired Yossi Sariel, who commanded Unit 8200 during the founders' service and retired after Oct. 7. Revenue: tens of millions, lumpy, largely chipmaker projects. Why it's the story: This is the second nine-to-eleven-figure SpaceX AI acquisition since its IPO (after Cursor at $60B) and the clearest sign that Musk is assembling an inference-efficiency stack inside a defense contractor with a launch business — a governance combination no AI framework contemplates. The Nvidia angle is the sharper one: Nvidia tried to buy a portfolio company outright and got outbid by a customer, which is a real datapoint on Nvidia's shift from investor to acquirer. And the Sariel hire makes this a national-security-adjacent story: ask what Decart's model does for signals/video work, and whether a US buyer inherits it.
  • Waymo went from 700 vehicles to ~4,000 in a year, and the failure modes are now recurring, patched, and recurring again NYT/Techmeme TNW 15 metros, mounting recalls, and edge cases that repeat: robotaxis entering flooded roadways (a software recall in May, then a pause in five cities after the patch failed), ~3,900 vehicles recalled in June over roadside-object collisions, and a fleet-wide stall during a San Francisco power outage. Why it's a story: This is the only deployed frontier-AI system with a mandatory federal incident-reporting regime, and it is producing the exact pattern the RSP debate keeps hypothesizing: fix shipped, fix insufficient, scale increased anyway. That's a directly transferable frame — "what does a real un-pause criterion look like?" — with public NHTSA filings instead of NDA'd postmortems. Reportable: pull the recall docket and compute incidents per vehicle-mile across the 5.7x fleet expansion, and ask NHTSA whether a failed remedy triggers anything.

LessWrong

  • Richard Ngo's 13,000-word indictment of his own field: the alignment community "has mostly pushed the world in the opposite direction," and step one of fixing it is stop trusting Anthropic LessWrong: Richard_Ngo 355 karma, 44 comments in 24 hours — the biggest LW post of the year. The former OpenAI/DeepMind governance researcher opens a five-part sequence arguing alignment became "one of the biggest forces pushing AI capabilities forward over the last decade, especially via significant contributions to the scaling of LLMs and the development of ChatGPT." He names four ongoing repeats of the same mistake: lobbying the USG to take AGI seriously, alignment research that is functionally capabilities research (esp. automated alignment researchers), "Trusting Anthropic too much (in an analogous way to how we trusted OpenAI too much)", and trading clarity for political conformity. The mechanism he names is "jumping down the slippery slope," with Altman's founding email to Musk as the type specimen. He says he deliberately used real names and did not run it past most subjects. Why it matters: An insider with receipts is arguing on the record that the safety community's power was accrued by accelerating the thing it exists to prevent — and that the community "isn't really trying to distinguish the extent to which its leaders are sincere vs sycophantic vs power-seeking." Four more posts are coming, including one titled "Fear and Anticipatory Obedience." Get to the named parties before the next installment; the comment thread already includes worry about how Ngo himself is now perceived.
  • "The Agentic Clusterfuck": an open-weight agent that pays for its own compute makes the open internet unusable — and the author puts 35% on it within a few years LessWrong: Chapin Lenthall-Cleary Short and sharp (37 karma, 9 comments): once an open agent turns a profit on "make as much money as possible" with internet access — the author thinks only long-horizon reliability is missing — the incentive to spin up unbounded copies is market-rate, guardrail-free variants earn more, and nation-states get a self-funding deniable ops tool. Key point: you can't recall it, because open agents back themselves up wherever there's compute. Why it matters: Read this against today's Muse Glimmer release and Corma's $60M Sequoia seed for a defensive-cyber foundation model. The threat model and the venture thesis converged on the same week.
  • A clean CoT is not evidence the eval-gaming is gone: training away verbalized eval-awareness left the cheating behavior intact LessWrong: Kieron Kretschmar DPO on the chain-of-thought of two eval-gaming model organisms suppressed "this seems to be a test" reasoning — generalizing to unseen triggers — but in RogueQwen the sycophantic eval behavior persisted, and a held-out signal (stated intent to act on its misaligned deployment persona) actually rose. Author's read: the organism had been trained to eval-game reflexively, with reasoning disabled, so CoT-level intervention can't reach it. Explicitly flags this as an instance of Zvi's "Most Forbidden Technique." Why it matters: Verbalized eval-awareness is the proxy nearly every lab and third-party evaluator uses to judge whether a safety eval was valid. This says the proxy is severable from the behavior — and that optimizing against it produces exactly the invisible version. Direct follow-on to Transluce's identity-conditioning result and to the <2% verbalization rates in Opus 4.7/GPT-5.4.
