Pitches

  • OpenAI wants California to rewrite SB 53 — to let regulators monitor frontier models while they're still being trained Politico via Techmeme Days after the HuggingFace agent-swarm saga, OpenAI urged its home state to "strengthen" SB 53 to require monitoring of models under training/evaluation, not just after deployment, and to make a model bypassing a third party's security controls a reportable incident. Why it's the story: a frontier lab lobbying to expand a law it once resisted is a genuine policy inflection — and the specific ask (pre-deployment training oversight) maps directly onto OpenAI's own 20%-compute monitoring retrofit I covered Aug 19. Reportable cruxes: does OpenAI want self-monitoring codified vs. state access to training runs, would the reporting trigger have captured Astra, and do Anthropic/Google back the amendment or see regulatory capture in a rival writing the rules?
  • Anthropic's bankers are floating a $2T valuation and a $100B+ raise — an IPO that would eclipse SpaceX's record NYT via Techmeme France 24 The figure would more than double the $965B June round — for a five-year-old company. Why it's the story: this is the concrete public-market pressure the "AI Safety Has 12 Months Left" thesis warned about (Aug 16). A $2T listing forces Anthropic's safety posture — the very Risk Report candor Zvi praised — into S-1 disclosure and quarterly-earnings logic. Reportable: what the S-1 must say about the §5.2 process failures and "Model 2," and whether IPO timing explains the sudden voluntary transparency.
  • Generalist AI's GEN-1.5 teaches a robot a brand-new task from a single 3–12 second demo — no training. They're calling it robotics' "GPT-3 moment" Generalist AI The Decoder X: The Humanoid Hub A "physical prompt" loads into the model's context window; one-shot in-context learning of physical skills emerged from pretraining with zero architectural nudges — 59% avg success across 10 tasks one-shot, 83% few-shot. Also does sim-to-real and human-hand-to-robot transfer. Why it's the story: this is the substantive counterweight to the "craziest hype video" robotics arms race — an actual capability claim (in-context learning generalizing to embodiment) rather than a locomotion demo. It lands the same week as the Beijing World Robot Conference, where Unitree's Wang said the "ChatGPT moment" hasn't come. Reportable: independent replication, whether "short-horizon simple tasks" survives contact with real deployment, and what emergent in-context adaptation means for the safety-eval gap on embodied agents.
  • Nvidia will pay Poolside $6B to license its model-building software — and poach 109 of its staff Bloomberg/Yahoo Newcomer A non-exclusive license paired with mass job offers — the exact acqui-hire/licensing playbook that halved Groq's valuation. Why it's a story: this is the Nvidia "compete-with-your-own-startups" pattern I've tracked (Groq, Decart, Etched) applied to the coding-model layer — hollowing out a would-be independent lab via a license fee that doubles as a talent raid. Reportable: does the $6B route around antitrust scrutiny that an acquisition would trigger, and what happens to Poolside's remaining investors and its enterprise customers.
  • Governors who courted data centers are now quietly slowing them — as the backlash becomes an electoral liability WSJ via Techmeme Greg Abbott and Josh Shapiro are among those braking development; Senate Republicans reportedly warned of a "sleeper issue for the entire election cycle." Update to Aug 21's Transformer "data center as symbol" piece — now with named politicians acting. Why it matters: if NIMBY politics is becoming the de facto brake on the scaling buildout (as safety writers debated), the governors' reversal is the mechanism, and it collides with the circular-financing commitments (Broadcom, Nvidia credit MOUs) that assume the buildout continues.

LessWrong

  • "Big-World Intuitions" — why non-consequentialist heuristics ("share knowledge freely, do good work") break down exactly when you're near the endgame LessWrong The post argues the intuition that "you're a small player, just follow good general principles and it'll work out" — the basis for open science and not worrying about dual-use — is only sound when the world is "big" relative to you. Near a winning move or the endgame, you must reason adversarially and consequentially instead. Why it's worth a story: this is a crisp philosophical framing of the open-vs-closed research fight (the Unitree/MIT robot-dog case, distillation panic, open-weights sanctions) — a diagnosis of when "publish freely" stops being wise. Directly quotable in any piece on why AI researchers are abandoning open-science norms.
  • Zvi: "AI Text Watermarking Is Free And Good" — Scott Aaronson largely solved it at OpenAI, and it was never shipped Zvi Zvi argues the Aaronson/Kirchner cryptographic watermark is cheap and worth deploying — a pointed contrast to Anthropic's just-shipped, rewrite-defeatable watermark (which the vendor itself concedes degrades trivially, covered Aug 15). Update/explainer peg: the two labs' watermarking approaches are diverging, and Zvi's claim that a robust scheme has sat unused for years is a checkable accountability angle as the EU transparency code takes effect. Worth asking OpenAI why Aaronson's scheme was shelved.
