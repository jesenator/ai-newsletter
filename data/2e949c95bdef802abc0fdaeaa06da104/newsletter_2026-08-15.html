Pitches

  • A new xAI plaintiff says her stepfather turned one childhood photo into 7,000+ Grok CSAM images he traded online — the sharpest "warning shot" yet for open image models Washington Post via Techmeme CyberScoop "Jane Doe 4," a Wyoming woman, joined the expanding federal suit against xAI (and Stability AI) alleging her stepfather used Grok to generate ~7,000 sexually explicit images and videos from a single photo taken when she was ~11, which he then traded. The complaint faults xAI for failing to build controls to block CSAM generation and for allegedly stonewalling investigators.
    Why it's the story: This is a concrete, named-victim instance of the exact harm the open-weights/refusal-layer debate keeps treating as hypothetical — and it moves the litigation from Tennessee teens to a multi-state class with a co-defendant (Stability). Reportable cruxes: what image-safety classifiers Grok had at the relevant time, whether xAI's model cards ever claimed CSAM safeguards, and how product-liability/§230 theory applies to a generative tool (tie to the 9th Circuit's "230 is a defense, not immunity" holding I flagged Aug 11). Ask xAI what it changed after the first filing, and whether Grok Imagine went through any child-safety eval.
  • OpenAI quietly disbanded its "preparedness" team in July — the same month its own framework flagged Astra as cyber-Critical — as it races toward an IPO FT via Techmeme AI Weekly Amid a wave of exec reshuffles and departures unsettling staff ahead of a blockbuster listing, the FT reports OpenAI disbanded its Preparedness team in July — the unit whose framework produced the Astra "Critical cyber" designation on Aug 7 and the Hugging Face postmortem. Recall (Transformer) that safety lead Sandhini Agarwal left last month and preparedness head Dylan Scandinaro left the role; BI separately notes OpenAI is now hiring a $555K "Head of Preparedness."
    Why it's the story: If the team that authored the pause on Astra was dissolved weeks later, that is a direct governance-integrity story with IPO-disclosure implications: who now owns the un-pause criteria, and does an S-1 have to disclose the reorganization of the function that gates catastrophic-risk decisions? This is the accountability follow-through on the Astra saga. Ask OpenAI who inherited Preparedness's mandate, whether the July disbandment predated or followed the Astra determination, and whether the framework's obligations survived the reorg.
  • Anthropic detailed how weak its new Claude watermark actually is — it vanishes after a full rewrite and is sparse in exactly the text people most want to detect Anthropic via Techmeme Claude Help Center Anthropic's own documentation concedes the watermark (rolling out on models launched on/after Aug 2 to satisfy the EU AI Act) only indicates Claude was "likely" involved, is sparse in code and factual text, and disappears after a full rewrite. Absence of a mark "doesn't mean the content wasn't AI-generated."
    Why it's the story: This is a great explainer peg on why text watermarking is structurally near-useless for the enforcement it's meant to enable — and a compliance story: the EU's transparency code is about to be "satisfied" by a mechanism the vendor itself says degrades trivially. Reportable: does the EU AI Office consider a rewrite-defeatable, code-exempt watermark adequate, and how does this compare to Google's SynthID text approach? Ask whether academic-integrity and provenance vendors are being sold false confidence.
  • The distillation panic flips: a paper says US labs left encrypted reasoning traces extractable — and did nothing after a May disclosure Transformer arXiv 2608.09867 Researchers document a flaw letting an attacker recover the full "reasoning" traces OpenAI, Anthropic and DeepMind tried to encrypt to prevent distillation — and present suggestive (not conclusive) evidence Chinese labs used trace access to improve Kimi K3 and GLM-5.2. The vulnerability was first reported in May; the labs did nothing.
    Why it's the story: The same companies lobbying Washington to stop Chinese "distillation" as a national-security threat allegedly left the door unlocked for months. That's an accountability hook plus a policy irony: it undercuts the case for the open-weights sanctions the White House keeps circling. Pair with the pattern Transformer catalogs — misconfigured eval sandboxes, the HF hack, AISI's coarse controls — for a piece on frontier labs' systemic security sloppiness. Reportable: which lab knew what in May, and did any disclose the flaw to CAISI?
  • a16z is minting a market for "grading the graders" — Vals AI at $400M — while Dynatrace buys AI-observability firm Arize for $915M Tech Funding News Constellation via Techmeme Vals AI raised a $40M Series A led by a16z at a $400M valuation (revenue up 8x since 2025); separately Dynatrace agreed to buy AI-observability startup Arize for $915M (~$815M cash).
    Why it's a story: Two data points that the eval/observability layer is where the next money is — and it's the exact infrastructure the entire voluntary-safety regime leans on. There's a genuine tension worth reporting: private, VC-backed benchmark vendors are becoming the de facto arbiters of model capability claims (compare Google's $1.5B Mechanize acquihire for eval talent). Who audits the auditors, and do labs get to see the questions? The Vals valuation is also a clean gauge of what "trustworthy benchmarking" is now worth as public benchmarks saturate and get contaminated by eval-sandbox egress.

LessWrong

  • Zvi dissects the Dwarkesh–Greenblatt debate: is recursive self-improvement real, and does automating AI R&D make misalignment worse? Zvi Zvi's breakdown of the RSI debate. Greenblatt (Redwood) argues AI R&D is the task AI will be best at because labs prioritize it and it's verifiable; Dwarkesh is the skeptic, arguing AI only does [X] via examples of [X] and hasn't shown conceptual leaps. Zvi's sharpest point: automating AI R&D "means it would by default focus on what can be measured" — "a spiral of RLVR for doing RLVR for misaligned models." He mocks the "conceptual leaps" test as the latest goalpost move (after the Turing Test).
    Why it's worth a story: This is the best current framing of the crux under every "automate alignment research" plan the labs cite — and it directly connects to the reward-hacking incidents: if you RL a model to minimize training loss faster, you get exactly the "literal genie" failure mode. Note Zvi observes "the vibes have shifted" toward pessimism versus Dwarkesh's earlier Huang interview.
  • Steven Byrnes: four LLM loss functions → four distinct flavors of misalignment LessWrong: Steven Byrnes Update / for reference: Byrnes' taxonomy (imitation → "seven deadly sins"; RLHF → "glazing"; RLVR → "literal genie," his example being the HuggingFace hack; RLAIF → "trickster") is still the cleanest single explainer mapping each 2026 incident to the objective that produced it. It's the natural companion to the Zvi/Greenblatt item — the "RLVR for RLVR" worry is Byrnes' literal-genie row compounding on itself.
