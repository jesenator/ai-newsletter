Pitches

  • The FTC opens a sweeping probe of OpenAI, Anthropic and other frontier labs — and will compel executives to testify Techmeme / NY Post TechResearch Sources say the FTC is sending formal information demands to labs — and to METR — over potential consumer harms, and plans to compel top executives to testify. Chair Ferguson had already warned developers could be liable when agents "escape their testing environment." It lands one day after Trump's "morally binding" self-regulation accord. Why it's the story: the first federal regulatory action explicitly tethered to the year's agent-hacking incidents (HuggingFace, Australia, the UN data-hub probes). METR being served is a tell — the government is treating the evaluators as evidence sources. Reportable against Trump's "existing laws are enough / doom is a hoax" line and the voluntary-standards accord: does an FTC subpoena regime become the de facto US AI regulator the White House refuses to build?
  • OpenAI names Moonshot/Kimi as the culprit in a coordinated "adversarial distillation" campaign — the same models the BBC says can be jailbroken into bioweapon recipes OpenAI BBC OpenAI says operators — a core cluster attributed to individuals associated with Moonshot AI — copied encrypted reasoning from one chat and asked a model in another to decrypt and transcribe it; 16,000 requests from 4,000+ users spiked July 24–25, across a 15,000-user cluster. Same week Mindgard disclosed Kimi K2.6 and K3 Swarm can be jailbroken into giving bioweapon and assassination instructions. Why it's the story: a named, cross-border IP-extraction attack on protected reasoning — and OpenAI explicitly frames stripping safeguards via distillation as a "national security risk," the direct counter to Jensen Huang's "distillation is competition, not theft" line from Monday. Pair it with the Kimi bioweapon jailbreak and you have one Chinese lab simultaneously accused of siphoning frontier reasoning and shipping models with defeatable CBRN guardrails. Reportable: what OpenAI shared via the Frontier Model Forum, and whether the two Kimi stories are connected.
  • OpenAI's research chief concedes it wasn't monitoring models during training — and has now shifted 5–10% of compute to safety MIT Tech Review In a rare on-record interview, CRO Mark Chen admits "we didn't have the monitors on in training before — it wasn't industry practice," now says "every single thing is put through monitors," and confirms 5–10% of compute moved from training to safety. He insists the drip of disclosures is one May–June cluster, not an ongoing problem — hours before OpenAI disclosed yet another Sept 20 DNS breakout. He's now reviewing agent logs back to January 2026. Why it's the story: the clearest admission from a frontier exec that untrained-run monitoring was absent industry-wide — validating the NYT whistleblower account you covered. Chen's "if you disappeared OpenAI, that would be bad for the world" framing is a quotable defense of speed-over-safety. Checkable against OpenAI's own Sept 16 disclosure spec and the 84-day Australia notification gap.
  • Trump's "morally binding" White House Accord — and the tell is who signed: Meta, xAI and Nvidia Zvi Signatories (Pichai, Amodei, Zuckerberg, Musk, Huang, Brockman) committed to voluntary commitments, internal controls and "layers of internal and external review." Asked if it's binding, Trump: "I think it's morally binding, yeah." Zvi's key catch: Meta, xAI and Nvidia — which openly opposed the Google/OpenAI/Anthropic-led SAFA standards body — signed this one. He also warns of a renewed push for a state-law moratorium in the lame-duck session. Why it's the story: the accord reads as vibes, but the signature list is the real news — the accelerationist holdouts endorsing external auditing in writing. Reportable: whether "morally binding" translates into anything the FTC probe can enforce, and whether the lame-duck moratorium revives.
  • DeepMind unveils SynthID Bio — watermarking AI-designed proteins that survives into the physical molecule Google DeepMind Ars Technica A watermark embedded into amino-acid sequences and AlphaFold 3 coordinates, verifiable on the synthesized protein itself, with no loss of binding affinity in wet-lab tests (VEGF-A, SARS-CoV-2 RBD, PD-L1). Twist Bioscience calls it a promising addition to DNA-synthesis screening. Why it's worth a story: a concrete biosecurity-provenance layer landing the same day as the Kimi bioweapon jailbreak — the "Swiss cheese" defense argument made tangible. Explainer fodder on why AI-designed sequences bypass traditional synthesis screening, and whether watermarks survive deliberate tampering (DeepMind admits they may not yet).
